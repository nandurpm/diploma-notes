BFD conventions and utility unitsA block-flow diagram uses simple labelled blocks and arrows to represent major operations. Utility units in the syllabus include the boiler house, cooling water, process and instrument air, demineralized water and nitrogen generation.
Overall BFD of utility systemsThe overall utility BFD shows how services are generated, conditioned and distributed to process users. It should communicate the plant-level relationship rather than detailed piping.
BFD of boiler and nitrogen unitsA boiler BFD identifies feedwater, fuel, combustion, steam and flue-gas paths. A nitrogen-generation BFD identifies feed air, separation or generation, product nitrogen and purge or waste streams.
Chemical-equipment symbolsPFD symbols represent vessels, pumps, compressors, heat exchangers, columns, reactors and other process equipment. Equipment tags and flow arrows establish a consistent drawing language.
PFD of a boiler unitA boiler PFD can show feedwater preparation, boiler, steam outlet, fuel and air supply, combustion products and relevant heat-recovery paths at a process level.
PFD of a nitrogen-generation unitA nitrogen-generation PFD represents air preparation, separation or generation, product nitrogen, waste or purge and storage or distribution. The exact process route must follow the stated plant basis.
ISA-5.1 valve and pipe-fitting symbolsValves and fittings control, isolate, divert, reduce or connect process lines. ISA-5.1 provides conventions for identifying instruments and control functions; the drawing must use the supplied standard consistently.
Pipe-line symbolsLine symbols distinguish process piping, utility piping, pneumatic or electrical signals and other connections. Line number, size, service and flow direction support maintenance and operation.
Instrumentation symbolsInstrument bubbles and function labels identify measurement and control devices. A loop may include a sensor, transmitter, controller, final control element and signal path.
P&ID of a boiler unitThe boiler P&ID develops the PFD by adding valves, instruments, alarms, control loops, safety devices and utility connections needed for operation and protection.
P&ID of a chemical reactorA reactor P&ID commonly includes feed control, agitation or mixing, temperature measurement and control, pressure protection, product withdrawal and utility connections.
P&ID of a heat exchangerA heat-exchanger P&ID shows hot and cold streams, isolation, bypass, temperature measurement, pressure indication and drain or vent provisions where appropriate.
P&ID of a distillation columnA distillation P&ID includes column, reboiler, condenser, reflux, feed, distillate and bottoms paths together with level, pressure, temperature and flow control as required.
Piping plan of the chemical reactorThe piping plan locates the reactor and connected equipment in relation to the plant layout. It should show line routes, access, isolation and the principal operating connections from the M3.05 P&ID.
Piping isometric drawingAn isometric drawing represents the three-dimensional route using standard axes. It records fittings, valves, dimensions, elevations and connection points required for fabrication or installation.
