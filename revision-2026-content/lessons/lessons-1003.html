Engineering Graphics

Engineering graphics is the language used by engineers to communicate shape, size, construction and manufacturing information. This handbook develops standards-based drawing habits, spatial visualisation and the ability to convert three-dimensional objects into accurate two-dimensional views.

Module I

Introduction — terminology, dimensioning and polygons

9CO1
Engineering Drawing-ൽ line type, lettering, dimensioning, symbol എന്നിവ ഒരേ standard പ്രകാരം ഉപയോഗിക്കണം. Drawing മനോഹരമാക്കുന്നതിനെക്കാൾ പ്രധാനമാണ് അത് ambiguity ഇല്ലാതെ manufacture ചെയ്യാൻ കഴിയുന്ന രീതിയിൽ വായിക്കാനാകുന്നത്.

Drawing terminology and BIS conventions

Drawing sheet sizes, title block, scales, lettering, visible line, hidden line, centre line, dimension line and extension line. Keep line weights consistent and never use object lines as dimension lines.

Principles of dimensioning

Use one unit system, place dimensions outside the view where possible, avoid duplicate dimensions and dimension to functional features. Explain aligned and unidirectional systems and the correct use of diameter, radius, square and angle symbols.

Scales — study purpose

Recognise representative fraction, full-size, reducing and enlarging scales. Plain, diagonal and vernier scales are included for conceptual study; the drawing should clearly state the scale used.

Regular polygons

Construct pentagon, hexagon, heptagon and octagon when side length is given. Use the general polygon method or a suitable exact construction and verify equal sides before darkening.

Rules / common mistakes

  • Dimensions must be sufficient but not repeated.
  • Arrowheads must touch extension lines and remain uniform.
  • Construction lines are thin; final object outlines are thick.
  • Never measure from a printed drawing when a numerical dimension is given.

Practice and self-learning

  • Prepare an A3/A4 sheet with title block and standard line samples.
  • Redraw a dimensioned component using BIS conventions.
  • Construct four regular polygons from a given side.

Module II

Basic figures — conics, involutes and developments

12CO2
Conic section എന്നത് cone-നെ plane കൊണ്ട് cut ചെയ്യുമ്പോൾ ലഭിക്കുന്ന curve ആണ്. Development of Surface എന്നത് 3D object-ന്റെ surface cut ചെയ്ത് flat sheet ആയി തുറക്കുന്ന രീതിയാണ്; sheet-metal work-ൽ ഇത് വളരെ പ്രധാനമാണ്.

Ellipse — rectangular method

Draw the bounding rectangle using major and minor axes, divide the semi-axes equally, project the divisions and obtain points of the ellipse. Join points using a smooth curve, maintaining symmetry about both axes.

Ellipse — concentric-circle method

Draw concentric circles with radii equal to the semi-major and semi-minor axes. Divide both circles into equal radial parts; combine vertical and horizontal projectors to locate ellipse points.

Parabola — tangent method

Construct the axis, base and vertex; divide the base and axis appropriately, draw tangent-related construction lines and obtain the parabolic curve. State focus/directrix terminology even when the tangent method is used.

Involute of circle and rectangle

An involute is the locus of the end of a taut string unwound from a curve. Divide the perimeter, draw successive tangents and set off cumulative lengths to locate the involute.

Development of prism and cylinder

Use the parallel-line method. Stretch out the base perimeter, draw generators and transfer heights. Include seam allowance only when asked; preserve true lengths.

Development of a 90° elbow

Develop a two-piece symmetric elbow with equal circular sections and standard side outlets. Divide the half-circle, project generators to the miter line and transfer ordinates to the stretch-out.

Rules / common mistakes

  • Use a French curve or smooth freehand curve; do not join conic points with straight segments.
  • Generators must be numbered consistently in elevation and development.
  • A development requires true lengths, not apparent lengths.

Practice and self-learning

  • Construct one ellipse by both prescribed methods and compare point accuracy.
  • Draw involutes of a circle and a rectangle.
  • Prepare developments of a closed rectangular prism, cylinder and two-piece elbow.

Module III

Basic projections — points and lines

9CO3
Projection എന്നത് object-ൽ നിന്ന് imaginary projectors reference plane-ലേക്ക് വിടുന്ന പ്രക്രിയയാണ്. HP, VP എന്നിവ fold ചെയ്ത ശേഷം XY line-ന്റെ relative position നോക്കിയാണ് point ഏത് quadrant-ലാണെന്ന് കണ്ടെത്തുന്നത്.

Theory of projection

Define projector, plane of projection, horizontal plane (HP), vertical plane (VP), profile plane and XY reference line. Compare first-angle and third-angle projection and identify the standard symbol.

Projection of points in four quadrants

Locate front view from distance above/below HP and top view from distance in front of/behind VP. Use sign discipline and place views on the correct side of XY.

Lines parallel to both planes

A line parallel to HP and VP shows true length in both front and top views. End projectors remain aligned and distances from XY stay constant.

Lines perpendicular to one plane

A line perpendicular to a plane appears as a point in that plane and as true length in the other view.

Lines inclined to one plane

For first-quadrant problems, draw the view parallel to the plane first at true length and given angle, then project the shortened view on the other plane.

Rules / common mistakes

  • Projectors are thin and perpendicular to XY.
  • Label corresponding ends with the same lowercase letter and prime convention.
  • Read wording carefully: inclination to HP is seen in elevation only when the line is parallel to VP.

Practice and self-learning

  • Prepare a quadrant chart showing the position of front and top views.
  • Solve one point problem in each quadrant.
  • Draw line cases: parallel to both, perpendicular to one, inclined to HP, inclined to VP.

Module IV

3D projections — orthographic and sectional views

15CO4
Orthographic Projection-ൽ 3D object-നെ mutually perpendicular directions-ൽ നോക്കി Front, Top, Side views വരയ്ക്കുന്നു. Sectional View-ൽ imaginary cutting plane ഉപയോഗിച്ച് hidden internal details വ്യക്തമായി കാണിക്കുന്നു.

Pictorial and orthographic views

Distinguish isometric view from orthographic views. Select the front view that shows maximum shape information and minimum hidden detail.

First-angle arrangement

In first-angle projection, the top view is placed below the front view and the right-side view is placed to the left. Use a 45° miter line when transferring depth to side view.

View correlation

All views must align: widths between front and top, heights between front and side, and depths between top and side. Project before darkening.

Hidden and centre details

Use dashed hidden lines only when they add necessary information. Draw centre lines for axes, holes and symmetry; extend centre lines slightly beyond the feature.

Full sectional view

Pass a cutting plane through the object, remove the portion in front and hatch the cut material at a uniform angle and spacing. Do not hatch holes, ribs cut longitudinally, shafts or standard fasteners unless specified.

Sectional orthographic problems

Develop front, top and side views of simple engineering objects and produce full sections from pictorial views using first-angle projection.

Rules / common mistakes

  • Choose the minimum number of views needed to define the object.
  • Hatching of adjacent parts must differ in angle or spacing.
  • Do not show hidden lines in a sectional view unless essential.

Practice and self-learning

  • Convert five simple isometric objects into three orthographic views.
  • Draw a full sectional front view and corresponding top view.
  • Perform a final drawing-sheet audit for alignment, line type, labels and dimensions.
