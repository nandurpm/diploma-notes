Course introduction

Industrial Engineering and Management studies the optimisation of complex processes, systems and organisations. It develops the ability to plan plant locations and layouts, improve productivity through work study, coordinate production and inventory, manage projects using network analysis, and ensure quality through statistical control while respecting the technical and financial boundaries of industrial operations.

Important point: Inventory models assume stable demand and lead times. Real-world fluctuations require safety stock and regular review of inventory policies.

Module 4 — Project Management, Forecasting and Quality

Explain Project management (PERT and CPM) and state one correct method, application or precaution.

Network analysis tools like PERT (Program Evaluation and Review Technique) and CPM (Critical Path Method) help plan and control complex projects. They identify the critical path—the sequence of tasks that determines the project duration.

Answer framework: Draw a simple network diagram for a project with four activities and identify the critical path and total project duration.

Important point: Project networks reflect estimates of activity durations. Delays on the critical path will delay the entire project unless managed through crashing or resource reallocation.

Explain Statistical quality control (SQC) and state one correct method, application or precaution.

SQC uses statistical methods like control charts (X-bar, R, p, c) to monitor process stability and detect variations. Total Quality Management (TQM) and ISO standards provide the broader framework for continuous quality improvement.

Answer framework: Interpret a conceptual X-bar control chart and identify a point that indicates the process is 'out of control'; explain the difference between variable and attribute charts.

Important point: Control charts detect variation but do not fix the underlying process. Quality improvement requires root-cause analysis and corrective action by competent personnel.
