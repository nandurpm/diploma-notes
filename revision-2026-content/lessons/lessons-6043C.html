Course introduction

Mobile Communication studies the principles, architectures and standards of wireless cellular networks. It develops the ability to analyze cellular concepts like frequency reuse and handoff, evaluate multiple access techniques (CDMA, OFDMA), understand the evolution from 2G to 5G technologies, and coordinate mobile network solutions while respecting the technical and regulatory boundaries of electronic and telecommunication engineering.

Important point: CDMA systems require precise power control to prevent the 'Near-Far' effect. Do not operate CDMA networks without authorised fast closed-loop power control algorithms.

Explain GSM and 3G Evolution and state one correct method, application or precaution.

GSM (2G) is the global standard for digital mobile. Its architecture consists of the Mobile Station (MS), Base Station Subsystem (BSS), and Network Switching Subsystem (NSS). GPRS and EDGE provided data enhancements. UMTS (3G) introduced WCDMA for higher speeds and video calls.

Answer framework: Sketch the 'GSM Architecture' and label its main components; explain the role of the 'HLR' and 'VLR' in mobility management.

Important point: Mobile networks handle sensitive user data and location information. All network operations must follow authorised privacy and security protocols as per local telecommunication laws.

Module 3 — 4G LTE and 5G Technology

Explain 4G LTE and OFDMA and state one correct method, application or precaution.

4G LTE (Long Term Evolution) uses an all-IP architecture called the Evolved Packet Core (EPC). It employs OFDMA for the downlink and SC-FDMA for the uplink. MIMO (Multiple Input Multiple Output) uses multiple antennas to increase data rates and reliability.

Answer framework: Describe the 'EPC' architecture of 4G LTE; explain why 'SC-FDMA' is preferred for the uplink in mobile devices.

Important point: High-order MIMO and OFDMA require complex signal processing and synchronization. Do not deploy 4G systems without authorised timing and frequency synchronization verified at the base station.

Explain 5G NR and Future Trends and state one correct method, application or precaution.

5G New Radio (NR) offers ultra-high speeds and low latency. Key features include 'Beamforming' (focusing signals to specific users), 'Network Slicing' (creating virtual networks for different tasks), and support for massive IoT (Internet of Things) connectivity.

Answer framework: Explain the concept of 'Network Slicing' in 5G; describe the benefits of 'Beamforming' for system capacity.

Important point: 5G operates in higher frequency bands (mmWave) with limited range. All 5G site planning must follow authorised small-cell deployment strategies and safety guidelines for millimeter-wave radiation.

Module 4 — Network Management and Security

Explain Mobility and Security and state one correct method, application or precaution.

Mobility management keeps track of the user's location for call routing. Location updating and paging are key processes. Security involves authentication (verifying the user) and encryption (protecting the data) to prevent unauthorized access and eavesdropping.

Answer framework: Describe the 'Location Updating' process in a mobile network; explain how 'Authentication' is performed in GSM.

Important point: Encryption algorithms can be broken over time. All mobile security implementations must use authorised, up-to-date cryptographic standards and secure key management.

Explain Mobile IP and Satellite Systems and state one correct method, application or precaution.

Mobile IP allows a device to maintain its IP address while moving across different networks. Mobile satellite systems (e.g., Iridium, Starlink) provide global coverage in remote areas where terrestrial networks are unavailable. 6G is the next frontier, promising even higher speeds and AI integration.

Answer framework: Explain the role of 'Home Agent' and 'Foreign Agent' in Mobile IP; discuss the advantages of 'Satellite Mobile Systems' for remote connectivity.

Important point: Satellite communication involves significant latency and path loss. All satellite-based mobile services must follow authorised orbital regulations and frequency coordination to prevent interference.
