1.1 Regular Polygons: The Pentagon

A regular pentagon has five equal sides and internal angles of 108°. The most accurate manual method is the Protractor Method.

  • Draw the base line AB of given length (e.g., 40mm).
  • At point A and B, use a protractor to mark 108°.
  • Draw lines AC and BD of 40mm length at these angles.
  • From C and D, swing arcs of 40mm radius to intersect at point E.
  • Join CE and DE to complete the pentagon.

1.2 Conic Sections: The Ellipse (Concentric Circle Method)

This method is highly accurate and preferred for manual drafting when major and minor axes are known.

Fig 1.2: Concentric Circle Method

  • Draw two concentric circles with diameters equal to Major Axis (D) and Minor Axis (d).
  • Divide the circles into 12 equal parts (30° intervals).
  • From each point on the outer circle, draw vertical lines towards the center.
  • From each corresponding point on the inner circle, draw horizontal lines outwards.
  • The intersection of these lines gives points on the ellipse.
  • Join points with a smooth French curve.
Pro Tip: Always keep your pencil sharp for ellipse points. A 0.5mm deviation in plotting can make the curve look "lumpy" instead of smooth.

Module II: Orthographic Projections & Sectional Views

Translating 3D reality into 2D technical data. This is the core skill of any design engineer.

2.1 First Angle Projection Principle

In First Angle Projection, the object is imagined to be in the first quadrant (between the observer and the plane).

Standard Layout: FV above XY, TV below XY, Side View next to FV.

2.2 Sectional Views: Revealing the Internal

A section view is used when internal hidden lines become too complex to understand. We "cut" the object with an imaginary plane.

Hatching Standards (BIS)

  • Angle: 45° to the main axis.
  • Spacing: Uniform (usually 2mm - 3mm).
  • Line Type: Continuous Thin.
  • Rule: Never hatch through a hole or void.

Example: Section of a plate with a hole.

3.2 Professional CAD Workflow

1. Setup Phase

Before drawing a single line:

  • UNITS → Set to Decimal, Millimeters.
  • LIMITS → Set to 297,210 (A4).
  • LAYER → Create layers:
    • Outline: Continuous, 0.40mm
    • Hidden: Dashed, 0.20mm
    • Center: Center, 0.20mm
    • Dim: Continuous, 0.15mm

2. Drafting Phase

The "Skeleton" method:

  1. Draw Center Lines first.
  2. Use OFFSET to create main boundaries.
  3. Use TRIM and EXTEND to refine the shape.
  4. Apply HATCH last for sections.
  5. Use DIMLINEAR for sizing.
CAD Power Move: Use the Object Snap (OSNAP) settings (F3). Never "eye-ball" an intersection. Always snap to Endpoint, Midpoint, or Center for 100% accuracy.

3.3 Sectional View Example in CAD

To draw a sectional view of a hollow cylinder in CAD:

  • Draw the Front View using two concentric circles (C).
  • Draw the Top View by projecting lines downwards.
  • Draw a horizontal cutting plane line across the FV.
  • In the TV, identify the area cut by the plane.
  • Command H → Select 'ANSI31' → Click inside the solid regions.
  • Ensure the hatch scale is adjusted so lines are visible but not too dense.
