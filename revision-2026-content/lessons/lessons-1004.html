1. Introduction to Engineering Graphics

Engineering Graphics is the universal language of engineers. It allows for the precise communication of technical ideas, designs, and specifications without language barriers.

Drawing Standards (BIS SP 46)

All drawings must follow the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) code SP 46. This ensures uniformity in line types, dimensioning, lettering, and scales across the industry.

  • Line Types: Continuous thick (outlines), Continuous thin (construction/extension), Short dashes (hidden), Long chain (center lines).
  • Lettering: Use single-stroke Gothic lettering (upright or inclined). Standard heights: 3.5mm, 5mm, 7mm.
  • Scales: Full scale (1:1), Reduced scale (1:2, 1:5, 1:10), Enlarged scale (2:1, 5:1).

Drawing Instruments

Professional manual drafting requires a set of precision tools. Even in the CAD era, understanding these tools builds the foundation for spatial visualization.

Mini Drafter
Used for drawing parallel, perpendicular, and inclined lines quickly.
Set Squares
30°-60° and 45° squares for standard angles.
Pencils
H/2H for construction (light), HB/H for outlines (dark).

Geometrical Constructions and Developments

1.1 Regular Polygons

A regular polygon has all sides and internal angles equal. For Course 1004, you must master the construction of Pentagons (5 sides) and Hexagons (6 sides).

General Method

Can be used for any polygon. Draw the given side AB. At B, draw a perpendicular. With B as center and AB as radius, draw a quadrant. Bisect AB and the quadrant arc to find centers for circumscribing circles.

Angle Method

Internal angle of Pentagon = 108°. Internal angle of Hexagon = 120°. Use a protractor to set these angles from the base line and mark side lengths.

1.2 Conic Sections: The Ellipse

An ellipse is a curve where the sum of distances from two fixed points (foci) is constant. In 1004, we focus on construction based on Major and Minor axes.

Rectangular (Oblong) Method

  1. Draw a rectangle with sides equal to the Major and Minor axes.
  2. Divide the axes and the rectangle sides into equal segments (e.g., 4 parts).
  3. Project lines from the vertices of the minor axis through these points to find the curve points.

Concentric Circle Method

  1. Draw two circles with diameters equal to the Major and Minor axes.
  2. Divide circles into 12 equal sectors (30° each).
  3. From outer circle points, draw vertical lines; from inner circle points, draw horizontal lines. Their intersections form the ellipse.

1.3 Development of Surfaces

Development is the unfolding of a solid's surface onto a single plane. This is critical in sheet metal work and packaging.

Rectangular Prism

The development consists of four rectangles (lateral faces) joined side-by-side, plus two rectangles for the top and bottom bases. Total length = Perimeter of base.

Cylinder

The development of the curved surface is a rectangle. Height = Cylinder Height, Length = Circumference (πD). Add two circles for the bases.

Lab Exercises (Module I)

Ex. I.1

Polygon Construction

Outcome: Construct a regular pentagon and hexagon of side 40mm.

Procedure (Hexagon)

  1. Draw base AB = 40mm.
  2. With A and B as centers and 40mm radius, draw arcs to intersect at O.
  3. Draw a circle with center O and radius 40mm.
  4. Step off 40mm arcs around the circle to find all vertices. Join them.

Pro Tip

For a pentagon, use the 108° method for highest accuracy on paper. Keep construction lines (2H) very light.

Ex. I.2

Cylinder Development

Outcome: Draw the development of a cylinder (Dia 50mm, Height 70mm).

Procedure

  1. Calculate Circumference: 3.14 * 50 = 157mm.
  2. Draw a rectangle 157mm (length) by 70mm (height).
  3. Divide the length into 12 equal parts to show generators.
  4. Add top and bottom circles of 50mm diameter.

Calculation

C = πD
C = 3.14159 × 50 ≈ 157.1 mm

2.1 Theory of Projection

First Angle Projection

Standard in India and Europe. The object is between the observer and the plane. Front View (FV) is above the XY line, Top View (TV) is below the XY line.

Third Angle Projection

Standard in USA. The plane is between the observer and the object. Top View (TV) is above the XY line, Front View (FV) is below the XY line.

2.2 Projections of Points and Lines

Points in 4 Quadrants

  • 1st Quadrant: Above HP, In front of VP (FV above XY, TV below).
  • 2nd Quadrant: Above HP, Behind VP (Both above XY).
  • 3rd Quadrant: Below HP, Behind VP (TV above XY, FV below).
  • 4th Quadrant: Below HP, In front of VP (Both below XY).

Lines (1st Quadrant)

A line can be parallel to both planes, parallel to one and perpendicular to the other, or parallel to one and inclined to the other. True Length (TL) is only seen on the plane to which the line is parallel.

2.3 Sectional Views

Used to show internal features clearly. Imagine a Cutting Plane passing through the object. The part between the observer and the plane is removed.

Hatching Rules: Use thin parallel lines at 45°. Hatching is only done on the solid material cut by the plane. Hidden lines are usually omitted in sectional views unless necessary for clarity.

Lab Exercises (Module II)

Ex. II.1

Orthographic Projection

Outcome: Draw FV, TV, and Side View of a given step-block.

Steps

  1. Identify the direction of Front View (usually marked by an arrow).
  2. Draw XY line. Project FV above and TV below (First Angle).
  3. Ensure all views are aligned using vertical/horizontal projectors.
  4. Add dimensions using BIS standards.

Checklist

  • Are all visible edges thick?
  • Are hidden edges dashed?
  • Are projection lines thin?
  • Is the spacing between views uniform?

Module III

CAD Basics

Transition from paper to screen. Learn the fundamental commands and settings of Computer-Aided Drafting.

9 HoursCO3Familiarize

Initial Setup

  • UNITS: Set to Millimeters, Precision 0.00.
  • LIMITS: Set drawing area (e.g., 297,210 for A4). Use 'Zoom All'.
  • OSNAP: Enable Endpoint, Midpoint, Center, Intersection for precision.
  • ORTHO: Toggle F8 for perfectly horizontal/vertical lines.

The CAD Workflow

1. Setup Environment → 2. Draw Geometry → 3. Modify/Edit → 4. Add Layers/Colors → 5. Dimension & Text → 6. Plot/Print.

Module IV

CAD Drafting Practice

Apply your CAD skills to produce professional-grade orthographic and sectional drawings.

9 HoursCO4Apply

CAD Project: Sectional Front View

Convert your manual exercise from Module II into a digital drawing.

  1. Layer Management: Create layers for 'Outline' (0.5mm), 'Hidden' (0.2mm dashed), 'Center' (0.2mm chain), and 'Hatch' (0.15mm).
  2. Drafting: Use 'Line' and 'Offset' to build the views. Use 'Trim' to clean up intersections.
  3. Sectioning: Draw the cutting plane line. Use the 'HATCH' command (Pattern: ANSI31) for cut surfaces.
  4. Dimensioning: Use 'DIMLINEAR' and 'DIMCONTINUE'. Adjust 'DIMSTYLE' for readable text and arrow sizes.
Common CAD Errors: Drawing everything on 'Layer 0', overlapping lines (use OVERKILL to fix), and inconsistent scale in dimension styles. Always verify your 'Units' before starting.
