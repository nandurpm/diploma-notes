Detailed Learner Guide

How to study Engineering Drawing with CAD from zero

This course is not a memorisation subject. Marks come from neat construction, correct projection placement, proper CAD command use, and the ability to explain why each line is drawn. Use this section as the descriptive foundation before starting the module-wise lab sheets.

1. What an engineering drawing communicates

An engineering drawing is a precise graphical language used to manufacture, inspect, assemble, or repair an object. A good drawing answers four questions: shape of the object, size of every feature, position of each feature, and material/finish or special instruction when required. Unlike a freehand sketch, every line has meaning and must follow a standard.

  • Visible outlines show edges directly seen by the observer and are drawn thick and continuous.
  • Hidden lines show edges not directly visible and are drawn as thin dashed lines.
  • Centre lines show axes of circles, holes, cylinders, and symmetry using long-short chain pattern.
  • Construction lines are temporary thin lines used to locate points; they should not dominate the final answer.

2. Standard sheet workflow

Before starting any sheet, reserve margins, draw a title block, select scale, and keep the reference line or datum clear. Read the question fully, underline the given dimensions, identify the final view required, and only then start construction. The examiner checks not only the final shape but also the method of construction and neatness.

  1. Write problem number, scale, date, and name/roll number in the title block.
  2. Draw all construction/reference lines lightly first.
  3. Locate points using measured distances, perpendiculars, projectors, or arcs as required.
  4. Darken final object outlines only after confirming the construction is correct.
  5. Add dimensions, labels, hatching, and projection symbols clearly.

3. Projection logic in simple words

Projection means showing a 3D object on 2D planes. Imagine light rays from the observer falling perpendicular to a transparent sheet. The shadow formed on that sheet is a view. The three most common views are front view/elevation, top view/plan, and side view/end view. The position of each view depends on first-angle or third-angle convention.

For Kerala polytechnic practice, use first-angle projection unless the question says otherwise. In first-angle projection, the object lies between observer and plane; therefore, the top view is placed below the front view, and the right-side view is placed on the left side of the front view.

4. CAD drawing mindset

CAD is not just a digital pencil. It is an accuracy tool. Use coordinates, object snaps, layers, ortho/polar tracking, trim/extend, hatch, and dimension styles to produce repeatable drawings. If the paper method teaches the geometry, CAD teaches speed, precision, editing, and professional plotting.

  • Set units to millimetres before drawing engineering objects.
  • Use layers for outline, construction, hidden, centre, dimension, hatch, and text.
  • Use OSNAP modes such as endpoint, midpoint, centre, intersection, and perpendicular.
  • Use dimensions only after the geometry is complete and cleaned.
Exam-friendly answer pattern: For every construction/projection question, write a short heading, show the reference line/axes, label points consistently, use correct line types, and finish with dimensions or hatching. For CAD viva, explain the command name, shortcut, purpose, and one example of use.

Geometrical Constructions and Developments

Concept handbook

Regular Polygons

A polygon is regular when all sides and angles are equal. General method (using perpendicular bisectors or angle calculations) or semi-circle methods are used to draw a pentagon or hexagon given the side length 'a'. For a pentagon, the interior angle is 108°. For a hexagon, it is 120°.

Ellipse: Rectangular Method

In the rectangular (or oblong) method, the major axis and minor axis form a bounding rectangle. The axes are divided into equal segments, and lines are projected from the vertices of the minor axis through these points to find the locus of the ellipse.

Ellipse: Concentric Circle Method

Draw two concentric circles with diameters equal to the major and minor axes. Divide the circles into equal angular divisions (usually 12 parts). From points on the outer circle, draw vertical lines, and from points on the inner circle, draw horizontal lines. Their intersections trace the ellipse.

Development of Surfaces

The development of a solid is the flat, 2D pattern representing its entire outer surface when unfolded. For a rectangular prism, the development consists of a series of joined rectangles. For a cylinder, the development is a single large rectangle with width equal to the circumference πD, plus the two circular bases.

How to decide the correct construction method: If the question gives side length, start from the side and construct the polygon outward. If it gives major/minor axes, choose an ellipse method. If it gives a solid such as prism or cylinder, first identify all faces, then unfold them in their true size. Development problems are about surface length, not pictorial appearance.

Detailed example: cylinder development

For a cylinder of diameter 50 mm and height 80 mm, the curved surface development is a rectangle. Its height is 80 mm and its length is the circumference: πD = 3.1416 × 50 = 157.1 mm. Draw a rectangle 157.1 mm × 80 mm, divide the length into equal generators if required, and add two circles of diameter 50 mm for the top and bottom bases.

Neatness checklist for Module I

  • Keep all construction arcs visible but light.
  • Mark centre points, axes, and vertices using small clear letters.
  • Do not erase important construction because it shows the method.
  • Use a smooth curve through ellipse points; never join ellipse points by straight lines.

Practical Centre / Lab Sheet

Ex. I.1

Polygon Construction

Outcome: Construct a regular pentagon and hexagon of side 40mm on paper.

Procedure

  1. Draw a line segment AB = 40mm.
  2. For a Pentagon: Draw 108° lines from A and B. Mark sides of 40mm. From the top vertices, swing 40mm arcs to intersect at the apex C. Join the points.
  3. For a Hexagon: Draw AB = 40mm. Using AB as radius, swing arcs from A and B to find center O. Draw a circle of radius AB. Step off the 40mm radius around the circumference to find all 6 vertices. Join in order.

Drawing tips

Ensure your compass pencil is sharp. Construction lines must be thin (2H), and final outline must be thick and dark (H/HB).

Ex. I.2

Ellipse Construction

Outcome: Construct an ellipse of major axis 100mm and minor axis 60mm using the Concentric Circle Method.

Procedure

  1. Draw a horizontal major axis AB = 100mm and a vertical minor axis CD = 60mm, intersecting at O.
  2. Draw two circles from center O with diameters 100mm and 60mm.
  3. Divide the circles into 12 equal sectors of 30° each using a protractor.
  4. For each sector line, draw a vertical line downwards/upwards from the outer circle intersection and a horizontal line from the inner circle intersection.
  5. Plot the intersection points and draw a smooth, continuous curve through them.

Common errors

Drawing straight line segments between plotted points instead of a smooth freehand curve. Keep the curve continuous.

Concept handbook

First Angle vs Third Angle

In First Angle Projection, the object is placed between the observer and the plane of projection. The Front View is above the XY line, and the Top View is below it. In Third Angle Projection, the plane of projection is between the observer and the object. The Top View is above the XY line, and the Front View is below it.

Projections of Points

A point is defined by its distances from the Horizontal Plane (HP) and Vertical Plane (VP). In the 1st Quadrant: Point is Above HP, In Front of VP. 2nd Quadrant: Above HP, Behind VP. 3rd Quadrant: Below HP, Behind VP. 4th Quadrant: Below HP, In Front of VP.

Projections of Lines

When a straight line is parallel to a plane, its projection on that plane shows its true length. If it is perpendicular to a plane, its projection on that plane is a single point. If it is inclined to HP or VP, its projection on that plane is foreshortened.

Sectional Views

A sectional view represents an object as if it has been cut along a cutting plane. This reveals the internal features. The cut surface is marked using thin, inclined parallel hatching lines, typically drawn at an angle of 45°.

Projection memory rule: Elevation tells distance from HP; plan tells distance from VP. Above HP gives elevation above XY. Below HP gives elevation below XY. In front of VP gives plan below XY. Behind VP gives plan above XY. This single rule solves most point-projection problems.

Detailed example: line inclined to HP

If a 65 mm line is inclined 30° to HP and parallel to VP, draw the front view at the true length of 65 mm making 30° with XY. Then project both endpoints downward to obtain the top view. Because the line is parallel to VP, front view shows the true length; because it is inclined to HP, top view becomes shorter than the actual length.

Section view explanation

A section is used when hidden internal details would make the normal view confusing. Imagine cutting the object with a plane and removing the front portion. Only the material actually touched by the cutting plane is hatched. Holes, voids, and empty spaces are not hatched.

Practical Centre / Lab Sheet

Ex. II.1

Projections of Points

Outcome: Draw the projections of points in all four quadrants with respect to HP and VP.

Procedure

  1. Draw a horizontal reference line XY.
  2. Plot Point A (1st Quadrant, e.g., 25mm above HP, 35mm in front of VP): Elevation a' is 25mm above XY; Plan a is 35mm below XY.
  3. Plot Point B (2nd Quadrant, 30mm above HP, 20mm behind VP): Elevation b' is 30mm above XY; Plan b is 20mm above XY.
  4. Plot Point C (3rd Quadrant, 25mm below HP, 40mm behind VP): Elevation c' is 25mm below XY; Plan c is 40mm above XY.
  5. Plot Point D (4th Quadrant, 40mm below HP, 30mm in front of VP): Elevation d' is 40mm below XY; Plan d is 30mm below XY.

Key rule

Elevations are always denoted with a prime symbol (a', b') and plans with plain letters (a, b). Elevation projections represent HP distances; plans represent VP distances.

Ex. II.2

Sectional Views of Objects

Outcome: Draw Front, Top, and a Sectional View of a hollow step-block from a given Isometric projection.

Procedure

  1. Analyze the 3D model, its dimensions, and the specified cutting plane.
  2. Draw the Front View showing external shapes and the cutting plane line.
  3. Draw the Sectional view (e.g. Sectional Front View) by hatching the solid regions cut by the plane with thin parallel lines at 45°.
  4. Draw the Top View showing the cutting plane indicator.
  5. Add overall and detail dimensions matching BIS SP 46 standards.

Common mistakes

Forgetting to hatch cut surfaces or hatching rib and web thin-walled structures, which should normally remain solid according to standards. Keep hatch spacing uniform (1mm to 3mm).

Concept handbook

CAD Coordinate Systems

CAD software uses three main coordinates: Absolute Coordinates (X,Y from origin 0,0), Relative Coordinates (@X,Y from the last point), and Polar Coordinates (@Distance<Angle from the last point). For example, a line 50mm long at 45° is drawn using `@50<45`.

Initial Setup Commands

Before drawing, set the units using `UNITS` (e.g. Millimeters, Decimal). Set the drawing boundaries using `LIMITS` (e.g. 0,0 to 297,210 for an A4 sheet), followed by `ZOOM` > `All` to refresh the workspace. Use `GRID` and `SNAP` for precision alignment.

Key Draw Commands

LINE (L): Creates individual straight segments.
CIRCLE (C): Creates circles (specify Center-Radius or Diameter).
RECTANGLE (REC): Draws a closed rectangular polyline.
HATCH (H): Fills an enclosed boundary with patterns like ANSI31 for sectioning.

Key Modify Commands

TRIM (TR): Cuts lines at intersections.
OFFSET (O): Creates parallel lines or concentric curves.
FILLET (F): Rounds corners with a specified radius.
MIRROR (MI): Creates a symmetric mirrored copy across an axis.

Recommended CAD setup sequence

  1. Open a new drawing and save with course code, exercise number, and name.
  2. Run UNITS and set Decimal + Millimeters.
  3. Run LIMITS for the sheet size, then ZOOM All.
  4. Create layers: OUTLINE, CONSTRUCTION, HIDDEN, CENTRE, DIMENSION, TEXT, HATCH.
  5. Turn on ORTHO or POLAR, and enable useful OSNAP modes.

Command choice guide

Use LINE for independent edges, POLYLINE when the shape must behave as one object, OFFSET for parallel edges and wall thickness, TRIM/EXTEND for cleaning intersections, MIRROR for symmetry, ARRAY for repeated holes or slots, HATCH for cut surfaces, and DIM commands only after final geometry is complete.

Concept handbook

Drafting Projections in CAD

Draw reference line XY. Create layers for construction lines (thin, light), outline (thick, dark), hidden lines (dashed), and dimension lines. Project lines vertically and horizontally using Ortho mode to keep views aligned.

Creating Sectional Views in CAD

Use `TRIM` to clean lines representing cut boundaries. Execute `HATCH` (`H`), select the ANSI31 pattern, set the scale (adjust to avoid too dense or too sparse lines), and pick points inside the closed boundaries representing cut metal surfaces.

Dimensioning & Text Standards

Configure dimension styles using `DIMSTYLE`. Set text heights, arrow sizes, and extension line gaps according to BIS SP 46 standards. Use `DIMLINEAR`, `DIMALIGNED`, `DIMRADIUS`, and `DIMDIAMETER` for measuring.

Plotting and Export

Draft title blocks containing Name, Roll Number, Course, Scale, and date. Use `PLOT` or `EXPORT` to generate high-resolution print-ready PDF files in layout mode.

Paper-to-CAD transfer method: Treat each view as a construction problem. Draw the front view first, project lines for the top and side views using construction layers, convert only the final visible edges to outline layer, add hidden/centre lines, hatch cut areas, and finally dimension the drawing.

Layer and plotting explanation

Layers make CAD drawings professional. For example, OUTLINE can be thick white/black continuous line, HIDDEN can be dashed, CENTRE can be chain line, CONSTRUCTION can be thin cyan, HATCH can be green or grey, and DIMENSION can be a separate readable layer. During plotting, lineweights and layer colors control print clarity.

Final checking before submission

  • All views aligned vertically/horizontally with projectors.
  • All object dimensions shown once, not repeated unnecessarily.
  • Hidden features and centre lines use correct linetypes.
  • Sectional surfaces are hatched uniformly.
  • Title block and scale are filled before exporting PDF.

Practical Centre / Lab Sheet

Ex. IV.1

2D CAD Drafting

Outcome: Draft the aligned Front View, Top View, and Sectional Side View of an engineering block in CAD with complete dimensions.

Procedure

  1. Initialize CAD. Run `UNITS` > decimal, millimeters. Run `LIMITS` > 0,0 to 297,210. `ZOOM` > `All`.
  2. Create layers: "OUTLINE" (color white, lineweight 0.4mm), "CONSTRUCTION" (color cyan, 0.15mm), "HATCH" (color green, 0.2mm), "DIM" (color yellow, 0.2mm).
  3. Draw the Front View on the OUTLINE layer.
  4. Project construction lines vertically down from the Front View vertices to align the Top View.
  5. Project lines horizontally to the side to create the Sectional Side View. Use the `HATCH` command with the ANSI31 pattern on the HATCH layer for the cut faces.
  6. Run `DIMLINEAR` on the DIM layer to place clear, non-overlapping dimensions. Add title block and export to PDF.

Pro Tip

Ensure that SNAP is on to easily catch endpoints, midpoints, and intersections. This guarantees absolute coordinate precision in CAD drawings.

Expected / Practice Questions

Module-wise answer bank

These are handbook practice questions, not official SITTTR questions. Use them to prepare for sheets viva and laboratory examinations.

Module I

1 Mark

What is the interior angle of a regular pentagon?

Answer / model points

The interior angle is 108° (calculated using formula: (n-2)*180/n, where n=5).

3 Marks

Explain the Concentric Circle Method for drawing an ellipse.

Answer / model points

1. Draw major and minor concentric circles.
2. Divide circles into 12 equal sectors (30° each).
3. From outer circle points, draw vertical lines. From inner circle points, draw horizontal lines to intersect and trace points on the ellipse.
4. Join with a smooth freehand curve.

7 Marks

Explain the step-by-step surface development of a cylinder of diameter 50mm and height 70mm.

Answer / model points

1. The development of a cylinder surface is a rectangle.
2. Calculate the circumference: πD = 3.1416 × 50mm ≈ 157.1mm.
3. Draw a rectangle of length 157.1mm (width of development) and height 70mm.
4. Draw the two circular bases of diameter 50mm attached to the top and bottom edges of the rectangle to form the complete development sheet.
5. Use thin outlines for fold/construction lines and thick outlines for boundaries.

Module II

1 Mark

Where is the top view located in First Angle Projection?

Answer / model points

The Top View is located directly below the Front View (below the XY reference line).

3 Marks

Define projections of points in all 4 quadrants.

Answer / model points

1st Quadrant: Point is above HP, in front of VP (elevation above XY, plan below XY).
2nd Quadrant: Point is above HP, behind VP (both elevation and plan above XY).
3rd Quadrant: Point is below HP, behind VP (elevation below XY, plan above XY).
4th Quadrant: Point is below HP, in front of VP (both elevation and plan below XY).

7 Marks

A line AB, 65mm long, is parallel to HP and inclined at 30° to VP. Its end A is 15mm above HP and 20mm in front of VP. Draw its projections.

Answer / model points

1. Draw reference line XY.
2. Mark elevation a' 15mm above XY, and plan a 20mm below XY.
3. Since the line is parallel to HP, its elevation a'b' is a horizontal line of length equal to the plan's horizontal span. Wait, since it is inclined to VP, the plan ab shows the true length of 65mm inclined at 30° to XY. Draw ab = 65mm at 30° to XY starting from a.
4. Draw projectors vertically up from b to find b' on the horizontal line starting from a'.
5. The line a'b' is a horizontal line of length equal to 65 × cos(30°) ≈ 56.3mm. Join a'b' and ab.

1 Mark

What is the keyboard shortcut for the TRIM command in CAD?

Answer / model points

The keyboard shortcut is TR.

3 Marks

Compare absolute, relative, and polar coordinate systems in CAD.

Answer / model points

1. Absolute coordinates use `X,Y` referencing the 0,0 origin.
2. Relative coordinates use `@X,Y` referencing the last entered point as 0,0.
3. Polar coordinates use `@Distance<Angle` referencing the last point at a specific distance and inclination angle.

Quick Revision

Final checklist and common mistakes

One-look rules

  • Pentagon interior angle: 108°. Hexagon interior angle: 120°.
  • First angle: object is in front of the projection plane. Front View is above XY, Top View below.
  • Hatching lines: thin, drawn at 45° angle, spaced 1-3mm apart.
  • Before drawing in CAD: always set `UNITS` and `LIMITS` first, then run `ZOOM` > `All`.
  • Keep separate layers for outline, hidden lines, construction lines, and dimensions.
  • Use SNAP and Ortho mode for absolute coordinate accuracy.

Common drawing mistakes

  • Confusing elevations (primed letters like a') and plans (plain letters like a).
  • Joining ellipse points with rigid straight line segments instead of a smooth curve.
  • Hatching solid ribs, webs, or shafts in sectional views (not allowed by standards).
  • Retaining draft/construction lines with the same thick weight as the final outline.
  • Forgetting to run `ZOOM` > `All` after setting `LIMITS` in CAD, leading to grid confusion.
