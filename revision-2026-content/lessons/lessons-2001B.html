Module I

Complex Numbers

14 hours (L:10 + T:4) CO1 Bloom: Apply

🎯 Learning Goals

  • Understand the complex number system
  • Perform fundamental operations
  • Represent complex numbers graphically
  • Find modulus, amplitude, and polar form
  • Solve engineering problems

🔑 Key Facts

  • i = √(−1)
  • z = a + ib
  • |z| = √(a² + b²)
  • arg(z) = tan⁻¹(b/a)
  • Polar: z = r(cos θ + i sin θ)
The Complex Number System

A complex number is a number of the form z = a + ib, where a and b are real numbers, and i is the imaginary unit defined by i² = −1.

  • Real part: Re(z) = a
  • Imaginary part: Im(z) = b
z = a + ib  ·  a,b ∈ ℝ  ·  i² = −1

Example: z = 3 + 4i has Re = 3 and Im = 4.

Malayalam support: സങ്കീർണ്ണ സംഖ്യകൾ (complex numbers) എന്നത് a + ib രൂപത്തിലുള്ള സംഖ്യകളാണ്. ഇവിടെ 'i' എന്നത് √(−1) ആണ്. ഇത് ഇലക്ട്രിക്കൽ എഞ്ചിനീയറിംഗിലും സിഗ്നൽ പ്രോസസ്സിംഗിലും വ്യാപകമായി ഉപയോഗിക്കുന്നു.
💡 Tip: The real and imaginary parts are always real numbers. The imaginary part includes the coefficient of i, not i itself.
Conjugate and Equality

Conjugate: The conjugate of z = a + ib is z̄ = a − ib.

z̄ = a − ib

Equality: Two complex numbers z₁ = a₁ + ib₁ and z₂ = a₂ + ib₂ are equal iff a₁ = a₂ and b₁ = b₂.

Example: If 2x + 3iy = 4 + 6i, then 2x = 4 ⇒ x = 2, and 3y = 6 ⇒ y = 2.
💡 Tip: The conjugate is used to rationalise denominators and find modulus.
Fundamental Operations

For z₁ = a + ib and z₂ = c + id:

  • Addition: z₁ + z₂ = (a+c) + i(b+d)
  • Subtraction: z₁ − z₂ = (a−c) + i(b−d)
  • Multiplication: z₁ · z₂ = (ac − bd) + i(ad + bc)
  • Division: z₁ / z₂ = (a+ib)/(c+id) × (c−id)/(c−id) = [(ac+bd) + i(bc−ad)] / (c² + d²)
Example (Multiplication): (2+3i)(1−4i) = 2 − 8i + 3i − 12i² = 2 − 5i + 12 = 14 − 5i
Example (Division): (3+4i)/(1−2i) = (3+4i)(1+2i)/5 = (3+6i+4i+8i²)/5 = (−5+10i)/5 = −1 + 2i
⚠️ Common mistake: In division, always multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator.
Graphical Representation — Argand Plane

Complex numbers are represented on the Argand plane (complex plane) with the real part on the horizontal axis and the imaginary part on the vertical axis.

Malayalam support: Argand plane-ൽ complex numbers-നെ points ആയി represent ചെയ്യുന്നു. Horizontal axis real part-um vertical axis imaginary part-um ആണ്.
Modulus and Amplitude

Modulus (Absolute value): |z| = √(a² + b²). It is the distance from the origin to the point (a, b).

Amplitude (Argument): θ = arg(z) = tan⁻¹(b/a), measured in degrees (or radians).

|z| = √(a² + b²)  ·  θ = tan⁻¹(b/a)
Example: For z = 3 + 4i: |z| = √(9+16) = 5, θ = tan⁻¹(4/3) ≈ 53.13°
⚠️ Common mistake: The argument depends on the quadrant. Always check the signs of a and b. In QII, θ = 180° − tan⁻¹(b/|a|).
Polar Form

A complex number can be expressed in polar form as:

z = r(cos θ + i sin θ)  ·  r = |z|, θ = arg(z)

This is also written as z = r ∠ θ or z = r cis θ.

Example: For z = 3 + 4i: r = 5, θ = 53.13° ⇒ z = 5(cos 53.13° + i sin 53.13°)
💡 Tip: Polar form is especially useful for multiplication and division — multiply moduli and add arguments.
Solved Problems
Problem 1: Find the modulus and argument of z = −1 + i.
Solution: a = −1, b = 1. r = √(1+1) = √2. θ = 180° − tan⁻¹(1/1) = 180° − 45° = 135°.
Problem 2: Express z = 2 − 2i in polar form.
Solution: r = √(4+4) = 2√2. θ = −tan⁻¹(2/2) = −45°. z = 2√2(cos(−45°) + i sin(−45°)).
Problem 3: If z₁ = 1+2i and z₂ = 3−i, find z₁ × z₂.
Solution: (1+2i)(3−i) = 3 − i + 6i − 2i² = 3 + 5i + 2 = 5 + 5i.
💡 Engineering Application: Complex numbers model AC circuits — impedance Z = R + iX, with magnitude giving total opposition and phase angle giving phase shift.

Module II

Vector Algebra

15 hours (L:12 + T:3) CO2 Bloom: Apply

🎯 Learning Goals

  • Distinguish scalars and vectors
  • Perform addition, subtraction, scalar multiplication
  • Compute dot and cross products
  • Apply vectors to engineering problems: area, work, moment

🔑 Key Facts

  • a·b = |a||b| cos θ
  • a×b = |a||b| sin θ · n̂
  • Work = F·d
  • Moment = r×F
  • Area of parallelogram = |a×b|
Scalars, Vectors, and Unit Vectors
  • Scalar: A quantity with only magnitude (e.g., mass, temperature, speed).
  • Vector: A quantity with both magnitude and direction (e.g., velocity, force, displacement).

Unit vectors: î, ĵ, k̂ along x, y, z axes respectively. Each has magnitude 1.

Position vector: r = x î + y ĵ + z k̂.

Malayalam support: Scalar-ന് magnitude മാത്രമേ ഉള്ളൂ (ഉദാ: mass). Vector-ന് magnitude-ഉം direction-ഉം ഉണ്ട് (ഉദാ: velocity). Unit vectors î, ĵ, k̂ axes-നെ സൂചിപ്പിക്കുന്നു.
Magnitude and Unit Vector

Magnitude: |v| = √(x² + y² + z²) for v = x î + y ĵ + z k̂.

Unit vector: v̂ = v / |v| (direction of v).

Example: v = 3î + 4ĵ. |v| = 5. v̂ = (3/5)î + (4/5)ĵ.
Vector Addition, Subtraction, Scalar Multiplication
  • Addition: v + w = (v₁+w₁)î + (v₂+w₂)ĵ + (v₃+w₃)k̂
  • Subtraction: v − w = (v₁−w₁)î + (v₂−w₂)ĵ + (v₃−w₃)k̂
  • Scalar multiplication: c·v = (c v₁)î + (c v₂)ĵ + (c v₃)k̂
Example: v = 2î+3ĵ, w = 1î−2ĵ. v+w = 3î+1ĵ, v−w = 1î+5ĵ, 3v = 6î+9ĵ.
Dot Product
v·w = |v||w| cos θ  ·  v·w = v₁w₁ + v₂w₂ + v₃w₃
  • Angle: cos θ = (v·w)/(|v||w|)
  • Perpendicular: v⊥w ⇔ v·w = 0
  • Work done: W = F·d (force × displacement)
Example: v = 2î+3ĵ, w = 1î−2ĵ. v·w = 2(1)+3(−2) = 2−6 = −4. |v|=√13, |w|=√5, cos θ = −4/(√13·√5) ≈ −0.496.
Cross Product
v×w = |v||w| sin θ · n̂  ·  v×w = (v₂w₃−v₃w₂)î − (v₁w₃−v₃w₁)ĵ + (v₁w₂−v₂w₁)k̂
  • Parallel: v ∥ w ⇔ v×w = 0
  • Area of parallelogram: |v×w|
  • Area of triangle: ½ |v×w|
  • Moment (torque): τ = r×F
Example: v = 2î+3ĵ, w = 1î−2ĵ. v×w = (3·0−0·(−2))î − (2·0−0·1)ĵ + (2·(−2)−3·1)k̂ = −7k̂.
Applications: Area, Work, Moment
Area of parallelogram: Vectors a = 2î+3ĵ, b = 1î−2ĵ. |a×b| = |−7k̂| = 7 sq. units.
Area of triangle: ½ × 7 = 3.5 sq. units.
Work done: Force F = 3î+4ĵ N, displacement d = 2î+1ĵ m. W = F·d = 3(2)+4(1) = 10 J.
Moment: Position r = 2î+3ĵ, Force F = 1î−2ĵ. τ = r×F = (3·0−0·(−2))î − (2·0−0·1)ĵ + (2·(−2)−3·1)k̂ = −7k̂ N·m.
💡 Engineering Application: Vectors are essential in electromagnetics (electric/magnetic fields), mechanics (forces, torques), and structural analysis.

Module III

Integral Calculus

15 hours (L:11 + T:4) CO3 Bloom: Apply

🎯 Learning Goals

  • Understand integration as reverse of differentiation
  • Recall standard integrals
  • Apply substitution and integration by parts
  • Evaluate definite integrals
  • Find area bounded by a curve and x-axis

🔑 Key Facts

  • ∫ xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n+1) + C (n≠−1)
  • ∫ eˣ dx = eˣ + C
  • ∫ 1/x dx = ln|x| + C
  • ∫ sin x dx = −cos x + C
  • ∫ cos x dx = sin x + C
Integration as Reverse of Differentiation

Integration is the inverse process of differentiation. If d/dx [F(x)] = f(x), then ∫ f(x) dx = F(x) + C, where C is the constant of integration.

∫ f(x) dx = F(x) + C  ·  d/dx [F(x)] = f(x)
Example: d/dx (x²) = 2x ⇒ ∫ 2x dx = x² + C.
💡 Tip: The constant C accounts for all antiderivatives. Always include it in indefinite integrals.
Standard Integrals
∫ xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n+1) + C
n ≠ −1
∫ 1/x dx = ln|x| + C
x ≠ 0
∫ eˣ dx = eˣ + C
∫ sin x dx = −cos x + C
∫ cos x dx = sin x + C
∫ sec² x dx = tan x + C
∫ cosec² x dx = −cot x + C
∫ sec x tan x dx = sec x + C
∫ cosec x cot x dx = −cosec x + C
Rules of Integration & Substitution

Rules:

  • ∫ k·f(x) dx = k·∫ f(x) dx
  • ∫ [f(x) ± g(x)] dx = ∫ f(x) dx ± ∫ g(x) dx

Substitution:

  • ∫ f(ax+b) dx = (1/a) F(ax+b) + C
  • ∫ f(x)·f'(x) dx = [f(x)]²/2 + C
  • ∫ f'(x)/f(x) dx = ln|f(x)| + C
Example (substitution): ∫ (2x+3)⁵ dx. Let u = 2x+3, du = 2dx ⇒ dx = du/2. = 1/2 ∫ u⁵ du = u⁶/12 + C = (2x+3)⁶/12 + C.
Example (f'/f): ∫ (2x)/(x²+1) dx = ln|x²+1| + C.
Integration by Parts

For product of two functions:

∫ u dv = uv − ∫ v du

LIATE rule (choose u as the function that comes first): Logarithmic, Inverse trigonometric, Algebraic, Trigonometric, Exponential.

Example: ∫ x sin x dx. Let u = x (algebraic), dv = sin x dx ⇒ du = dx, v = −cos x. = x(−cos x) − ∫ (−cos x) dx = −x cos x + sin x + C.
⚠️ Common mistake: Choosing the wrong u can make the integral more complex. Use LIATE.
Definite Integrals

A definite integral has limits of integration:

∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx = [F(x)]ₐᵇ = F(b) − F(a)
Example: ∫₀¹ x² dx = [x³/3]₀¹ = 1/3 − 0 = 1/3.
Area Bounded by a Curve and the x-axis

The area enclosed by the curve y = f(x), the x-axis, and the vertical lines x = a and x = b is:

Area = ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx  ·  (if f(x) ≥ 0 on [a,b])
Example: Area under y = x² from x=0 to x=2 is ∫₀² x² dx = [x³/3]₀² = 8/3 sq. units.
💡 Engineering Application: Definite integrals compute areas, volumes, work done, centre of mass, and signal energy in electrical engineering.

Module IV

Differential Equations & Laplace Transforms

16 hours (L:12 + T:4) CO4 Bloom: Apply

🎯 Learning Goals

  • Identify order and degree of differential equations
  • Solve first-order variable separable and linear D.E.
  • Understand Laplace transforms of elementary functions
  • Apply shifting property
  • Evaluate inverse Laplace transforms

🔑 Key Facts

  • L{1} = 1/s
  • L{tⁿ} = n!/sⁿ⁺¹
  • L{e^{at}} = 1/(s−a)
  • L{sin at} = a/(s²+a²)
  • L{cos at} = s/(s²+a²)
Differential Equations — Order and Degree

A differential equation is an equation involving derivatives of a function.

  • Order: The highest derivative present.
  • Degree: The exponent of the highest derivative (after removing radicals and fractions).
Example: d²y/dx² + 3(dy/dx) + 2y = 0 → Order 2, Degree 1.
Example: (d²y/dx²)³ + (dy/dx)² + y = 0 → Order 2, Degree 3.
⚠️ Common mistake: Degree is only defined when the equation is polynomial in derivatives. If derivatives appear in denominators or under radicals, rationalise first.
First-Order Differential Equations — Variable Separable

Form: dy/dx = f(x)g(y) ⇒ ∫ dy/g(y) = ∫ f(x) dx

Example: dy/dx = xy ⇒ dy/y = x dx ⇒ ln|y| = x²/2 + C ⇒ y = C e^(x²/2).
💡 Tip: Separate variables so that all y terms are on one side and all x terms on the other.
First-Order Linear Differential Equations

Form: dy/dx + P(x)y = Q(x)

Integrating factor: I.F. = e^(∫ P dx)  ·  y·I.F. = ∫ Q·I.F. dx + C
Example: dy/dx + 2y = x. P = 2, I.F. = e^(∫2 dx) = e^(2x). y e^(2x) = ∫ x e^(2x) dx + C. Using integration by parts: ∫ x e^(2x) dx = (x/2)e^(2x) − (1/4)e^(2x). So y = (x/2) − (1/4) + C e^(−2x).
Introduction to Laplace Transforms

The Laplace transform of a function f(t) is defined as:

L{f(t)} = ∫₀^∞ e^{−st} f(t) dt = F(s)

It transforms a function of time t into a function of complex frequency s.

Malayalam support: Laplace transform എന്നത് time domain-ൽ നിന്ന് s-domain (frequency domain) ലേക്ക് മാറ്റുന്ന ഒരു ഗണിത ഉപകരണമാണ്. ഇത് differential equations-നെ algebraic equations-ആക്കി മാറ്റുന്നു.
💡 Engineering Application: Laplace transforms are fundamental in control systems, circuit analysis, and signal processing.
Laplace Transforms of Elementary Functions
L{1} = 1/s
(s > 0)
L{tⁿ} = n! / sⁿ⁺¹
n = 0,1,2,…
L{e^{at}} = 1/(s−a)
(s > a)
L{sin at} = a/(s²+a²)
L{cos at} = s/(s²+a²)
L{sinh at} = a/(s²−a²)
L{cosh at} = s/(s²−a²)

Linearity property: L{a f(t) + b g(t)} = a L{f(t)} + b L{g(t)}

Example: L{3 + 2e^{4t}} = 3/s + 2/(s−4).
Shifting Property (First Shifting Theorem)
L{e^{at} f(t)} = F(s−a)

where F(s) = L{f(t)}.

Example: L{e^{3t} sin 2t} = 2 / ((s−3)² + 4).
Example: L{e^{−2t} cos 5t} = (s+2) / ((s+2)² + 25).
⚠️ Common mistake: The shifting property shifts s by −a for L{e^{at} f(t)}. Remember the sign: e^{at} → F(s−a).
Inverse Laplace Transforms

The inverse Laplace transform recovers f(t) from F(s). Common inverses:

L⁻¹{1/s} = 1
L⁻¹{1/s²} = t
L⁻¹{1/(s−a)} = e^{at}
L⁻¹{1/(s²+a²)} = (1/a) sin at
L⁻¹{s/(s²+a²)} = cos at
Example: L⁻¹{1/(s²+9)} = (1/3) sin 3t.
Example: L⁻¹{s/(s²+16)} = cos 4t.
💡 Tip: Use partial fractions to simplify F(s) before finding inverse transforms.

Q2: Compute the dot product of a = (2,3) and b = (1,−2).
a·b = 2(1)+3(−2) = 2−6 = −4.
Q3: Find the cross product of a = 2î+3ĵ and b = 1î−2ĵ.
a×b = (3·0−0·(−2))î − (2·0−0·1)ĵ + (2·(−2)−3·1)k̂ = −7k̂.
Q4: What is the area of a parallelogram formed by vectors a and b from Q3?
Area = |a×b| = 7 sq. units.

Module III — Integral Calculus

Q1: Evaluate ∫ 3x² dx.
∫ 3x² dx = 3·(x³/3) + C = x³ + C.
Q2: Evaluate ∫₀¹ x dx.
[x²/2]₀¹ = 1/2 − 0 = 1/2.
Q3: Find the area under y = x² from x=0 to x=2.
∫₀² x² dx = [x³/3]₀² = 8/3 sq. units.

Module IV — Differential Equations & Laplace

Q1: Find the order and degree of d²y/dx² + 3(dy/dx) + 2y = 0.
Order = 2, Degree = 1.
Q2: Solve dy/dx = 2xy.
dy/y = 2x dx ⇒ ln|y| = x² + C ⇒ y = C e^(x²).
Q3: Find L{sin 3t}.
L{sin 3t} = 3/(s²+9).
Q4: Find L⁻¹{1/(s²+25)}.
L⁻¹{1/(s²+25)} = (1/5) sin 5t.

⚡ Quick Revision

Module I — Complex Numbers

  • z = a + ib, i² = −1
  • |z| = √(a²+b²), θ = tan⁻¹(b/a)
  • Polar: z = r(cos θ + i sin θ)
  • Operations: add real/imag separately

Module II — Vector Algebra

  • a·b = |a||b| cos θ = a₁b₁+a₂b₂+a₃b₃
  • a×b = |a||b| sin θ·n̂
  • Area = |a×b|, Work = F·d, Moment = r×F

Module III — Integral Calculus

  • ∫ xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n+1) + C
  • ∫ u dv = uv − ∫ v du
  • Definite: ∫ₐᵇ = F(b)−F(a)
  • Area = ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx

Module IV — D.E. & Laplace

  • I.F. = e^(∫P dx) for linear D.E.
  • L{1}=1/s, L{e^{at}}=1/(s−a)
  • L{sin at}=a/(s²+a²), L{cos at}=s/(s²+a²)
  • L{e^{at}f(t)} = F(s−a)

⚠️ Common Mistakes — Exam Checklist

  • ✔️ In complex division, multiply by conjugate of denominator
  • ✔️ In vectors, cross product gives a vector, dot product gives a scalar
  • ✔️ In integration, always add +C for indefinite integrals
  • ✔️ In Laplace, shifting property: L{e^{at}f(t)} = F(s−a) (note the sign)
  • ✔️ In differential equations, identify order and degree correctly
  • ✔️ In inverse Laplace, use partial fractions when needed
