Module I — Introduction to Automobile (10 L + 10 P)

An automobile is an integrated system in which structure, power source, transmission, steering, suspension, brakes and controls work together to transport people or goods safely.

Evolution and Indian manufacturers

Development progressed from steam vehicles to petrol/diesel IC-engine vehicles, mass production, electronic control, hybridisation and electric mobility. In India, the syllabus highlights Hero and Bajaj (2W/3W), Maruti Suzuki and Tata Motors (passenger/commercial), Ashok Leyland (heavy commercial) and Mahindra (utility, tractor and commercial products). Plant locations and product ranges must be checked against current manufacturer information during class because they can change.

Malayalam support: Automobile evolution എന്നത് engine മാത്രം മാറിയ കഥയല്ല. Manufacturing, safety, electronics, emission control, materials, fuel and user needs എല്ലാം ചേർന്നുള്ള മാറ്റമാണ്.
Chassis, body and vehicle layouts

Chassis is the load-carrying framework with major running units. Body encloses occupants or load. A conventional HMV commonly uses a separate frame; many passenger cars use monocoque/unibody construction. Layout is described by engine position and driven wheels: front-engine rear-wheel drive, front-engine front-wheel drive, rear-engine rear-wheel drive and all-wheel drive.

Power flow example: Engine → clutch/torque converter → gearbox → propeller shaft or transaxle → differential → axle shafts → wheels.
Dimensions, weights and centre of gravity
  • Wheelbase: distance between front and rear axle centre lines.
  • Wheel track: distance between left and right wheel centre lines on the same axle.
  • Overhang: vehicle length beyond the nearest axle centre.
  • Ground clearance: minimum distance between ground and lowest specified vehicle point.
  • Kerb weight: vehicle ready for normal road use without payload.
  • GVW: maximum permitted total operating weight.

A lower centre of gravity generally improves resistance to rollover, but suspension travel, ground clearance and load distribution also matter.

Common error: Wheelbase is not total vehicle length; wheel track is not tyre width.
Engine mounts

Mounts locate the powertrain, carry static and dynamic loads, isolate vibration and control torque reaction. Rigid mounts transmit vibration; rubber/flexible mounts isolate better; hydraulic mounts use fluid damping for wider-frequency control. Selection depends on engine mass, excitation frequency, torque, packaging, durability, heat and chemical exposure.

Module II — Basics of Thermodynamics (12 L + 12 P)

System, surroundings, boundary and universe

A thermodynamic system is the chosen matter or region. Everything outside is the surroundings; the separating surface is the boundary. Open systems exchange mass and energy (engine cylinder during intake/exhaust, radiator coolant passage). Closed systems exchange energy but not mass for the selected interval (sealed gas in a piston-cylinder). An isolated system ideally exchanges neither.

Malayalam support: System type component name നോക്കി തീരുമാനിക്കരുത്. പഠിക്കുന്ന time interval-ലും chosen boundary-യിലും mass കടക്കുമോ എന്ന് ആദ്യം നോക്കണം.
Properties and equilibrium

Intensive properties do not depend on amount: temperature, pressure, density, viscosity. Extensive properties depend on amount: mass, volume, total internal energy. Equilibrium means no unbalanced thermal, mechanical or chemical tendency within the system.

Laws of thermodynamics

Zeroth: establishes temperature through thermal equilibrium. First: energy is conserved.

For a closed system: Q − W = ΔU. For steady-flow devices: heat + inlet energy = work + outlet energy.

Second: heat naturally flows from high to low temperature and no heat engine can convert all supplied heat into work. Third: entropy approaches a minimum as temperature approaches absolute zero; absolute zero cannot be reached by a finite process.

Engineering implication: An engine requires radiator, exhaust and lubrication because rejected heat and friction cannot be eliminated.
Processes and ideal cycles
Isochoric: constant volume.
Isobaric: constant pressure.
Isothermal: constant temperature.
Adiabatic: no heat transfer.
Otto cycle: ideal SI-engine cycle with constant-volume heat addition.
Diesel cycle: ideal CI-engine cycle with constant-pressure heat addition.
Carnot cycle: ideal reversible benchmark, not a practical vehicle engine cycle.
Worked energy example: An engine receives 1200 kJ heat and delivers 360 kJ work per cycle. Heat rejected = 1200 − 360 = 840 kJ. Thermal efficiency = 360/1200 × 100 = 30%. The remaining energy is not “lost”; it leaves mainly through exhaust, coolant and friction-related heating.

Module III — Engine Basics (12 L + 12 P)

Fixed parts

Cylinder block, cylinder head, crankcase and manifolds provide structure and passages.

Moving parts

Piston, rings, connecting rod, crankshaft, camshaft and valves transmit and control motion.

Support systems

Fuel, ignition/injection, lubrication, cooling, intake and exhaust enable controlled operation.
Engine terminology and geometry

TDC and BDC are piston extreme positions. Stroke is the distance between them; bore is cylinder diameter. Swept volume is displaced by the piston; clearance volume remains above the piston at TDC.

Swept volume per cylinder, Vs = (π/4)D²L. Compression ratio, r = (Vs + Vc)/Vc.

Example: D = 80 mm, L = 90 mm. Vs = π/4 × 0.08² × 0.09 = 0.000452 m³ ≈ 452 cm³.

Four-stroke petrol and diesel operation
  1. Intake: piston moves TDC→BDC. SI draws air-fuel mixture; CI draws air.
  2. Compression: both valves closed; piston BDC→TDC.
  3. Power: SI uses spark ignition; CI fuel is injected into hot compressed air and self-ignites.
  4. Exhaust: exhaust valve opens; piston expels products.
Malayalam support: Petrol engine-ൽ spark plug ignition timing നിർണായകം. Diesel engine-ൽ compression മൂലം air temperature ഉയരുകയും injector fuel spray നൽകുകയും ചെയ്യുന്നു.
Two-stroke petrol engine and scavenging

A two-stroke completes a cycle in one crankshaft revolution. Ports replace or supplement valves. Fresh charge entering through the transfer port pushes exhaust gas toward the exhaust port; this is scavenging. Simplicity and high power-to-weight are advantages, but short-circuiting of charge, lubrication and emissions are major limitations.

Module IV — Fuels (11 L + 11 P)

Origin, refining and fuel journey

Crude oil is extracted, transported to a refinery, separated by fractional distillation according to boiling range, further converted and treated, blended to specification, stored at terminals, transported by approved tankers and dispensed at retail outlets.

Flow: Crude extraction → transport → desalting → fractional distillation → conversion/treatment → blending and quality test → terminal storage → tanker → underground retail tank → dispenser → vehicle tank.
Storage, handling and filling-station safety

Fuel stations use underground tanks, fill points, vents, dispensers, emergency isolation, earthing/bonding, fire extinguishers and controlled traffic paths. The vehicle system includes cap, filler neck, tank, vent/EVAP arrangement, pump, lines and level sender.

Safety: No smoking or open flame. Switch off ignition. Avoid static discharge and spills. Do not taste or deliberately inhale fuel. Use institution-approved samples and PPE for demonstrations. Flash/fire-point tests must be performed only with approved apparatus and instructor supervision.
Fuel specifications and grades
  • Octane number: resistance of petrol to knock.
  • Cetane number: ignition quality of diesel; higher generally shortens ignition delay.
  • Volatility: tendency to vaporise; affects starting, mixture formation and vapour lock.
  • Viscosity: resistance to flow; important for diesel injection and lubrication.
  • Calorific value: heat released per unit mass or volume.
  • Flash point: lowest temperature at which vapour flashes momentarily; fire point is where sustained burning occurs.
  • Sulfur content: controlled to reduce harmful emissions and protect after-treatment.
BS-VI and biofuels

BS-VI is India’s stringent vehicle-emission and fuel-quality stage. It requires low-sulfur fuel and compatible engine/after-treatment systems. Ethanol blending can reduce petroleum dependence and alter octane, vapour behaviour, material compatibility and water sensitivity; always follow the vehicle manufacturer’s approved blend.

Expected / Practice Questions with Answers

1. Differentiate wheelbase and wheel track.
Wheelbase is longitudinal distance between front and rear axle centre lines. Wheel track is lateral distance between left and right wheel centre lines on one axle.
2. Why are hydraulic engine mounts used?
They combine elastic support with fluid damping, reducing vibration across a wider frequency range while controlling powertrain movement.
3. Classify a radiator as a thermodynamic system.
With coolant flowing through the selected control volume it is an open system because mass and energy cross the boundary.
4. State the First Law for a closed system.
Q − W = ΔU, using the convention that heat supplied to the system and work done by the system are positive.
5. Calculate swept volume for bore 70 mm and stroke 80 mm.
V = π/4 × 0.07² × 0.08 = 0.0003079 m³ ≈ 308 cm³ per cylinder.
6. Explain the main difference between petrol and diesel four-stroke power initiation.
A petrol/SI engine uses a spark plug to ignite a compressed mixture. A diesel/CI engine injects fuel into highly compressed hot air and it self-ignites.
7. What is scavenging?
The process of removing burnt gases and replacing them with fresh charge, especially important in two-stroke engines.
8. Distinguish octane and cetane numbers.
Octane indicates petrol knock resistance; cetane indicates diesel ignition quality and tendency for shorter ignition delay.
9. Why is flash point lower than fire point?
At flash point enough vapour exists for momentary ignition; sustained burning requires a higher temperature and continuing vapour generation.
10. Does BS-VI fuel alone make a vehicle BS-VI compliant?
No. Compliance also requires compatible engine design, calibration, sensors and emission after-treatment.
