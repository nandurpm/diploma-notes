Module 1

Fundamentals of Building Materials

Material selection begins with origin, manufacture, properties, defects, service conditions and sustainability.

Official hours: 7 L + 4 TMapped CO: CO1Bloom level: Understand

Learning goals

  • Classify materials as natural, artificial, engineered and green.
  • Compare stone, timber and bamboo.
  • Recognize eco-friendly masonry blocks and their uses.

Key facts

  • Seasoning reduces timber moisture before use.
  • A defect is a departure from required quality.
  • Green materials should be assessed by life-cycle impact, not a label alone.

Engineering relevance

Use the concepts to interpret technical information, communicate decisions accurately, and solve appropriately scoped problems.

Classification of building materials

Natural materials occur with limited processing, artificial materials are manufactured from constituents, engineered materials are designed for a target performance, and green materials emphasize reduced environmental impact or resource use. A classification chart should include origin, processing, properties and example applications.

Stone: types, properties, defects and uses

Stone selection considers strength, durability, porosity, texture, weathering resistance and appearance. Common defects may arise from cracks, laminations, cavities or weathering. Use stone appropriately in foundations, walls, flooring, cladding or landscape work based on performance.

Timber: properties, defects, seasoning and uses

Timber is valued for workability, strength-to-weight ratio and appearance. Defects include knots, shakes, checks, warping and decay. Seasoning reduces moisture to improve dimensional stability and service performance. Protect it from moisture, fungi, insects and fire as required.

Bamboo and eco-friendly masonry blocks

Bamboo is renewable and can be used in structural, enclosure and finishing applications when properly selected, treated and detailed. Compare clay bricks, fly ash bricks, AAC blocks and CSEB by density, strength, thermal behavior, water absorption, manufacturing and appropriate applications.

Module 2

Concrete Materials and Building Finishes

Concrete performance depends on constituent quality, proportions, water-cement ratio, placement, compaction and curing; finishes complete the building surface.

Official hours: 7 L + 4 TMapped CO: CO2Bloom level: Understand

Learning goals

  • Differentiate OPC, rapid-hardening, quick-setting and white cement.
  • Explain ingredients, PCC/RCC, grades and process.
  • Select flooring, finishing, composite, metal, insulation and coating materials.

Key facts

  • Water-cement ratio influences workability and strength.
  • Compaction reduces voids.
  • Curing maintains moisture and supports hydration.

Engineering relevance

Use the concepts to interpret technical information, communicate decisions accurately, and solve appropriately scoped problems.

Cement types and concrete ingredients

OPC is a general-purpose cement; rapid-hardening cement develops strength faster; quick-setting cement sets quickly; white cement is selected where colour is important. Concrete combines cement, water, fine aggregate and coarse aggregate. Each ingredient influences workability, strength, durability and finish.

PCC, RCC, grades, mix proportions and water-cement ratio

PCC is plain cement concrete without reinforcing steel; RCC includes reinforcement to resist tensile effects in a composite system. The syllabus lists M15, M20 and M25 grades and simple mix proportions. Do not confuse a grade designation with a complete structural mix design.

w/c = water mass ÷ cement mass.
Given: 25 kg water and 50 kg cement.
Required: w/c ratio.
Calculation: w/c=25/50.
Answer: w/c=0.50.
Mixing, placing, compaction, curing and defects

Concreting proceeds through batching/mixing, transporting, placing, compaction and finishing, followed by curing. Compaction removes entrapped air. Curing preserves moisture and temperature conditions for hydration. Defects can include honeycombing, cracks, segregation and bleeding; identify causes and preventive precautions.

Flooring, finishing, composites, metals, insulation and coatings

Stone, tile, cement and terrazzo flooring are selected by use, wear, moisture, appearance and maintenance. Plaster, putty and surface finishes prepare/protect surfaces. Plywood, MDF and particle board are composites with different fibre/particle construction. Steel and aluminium serve different structural, enclosure and corrosion contexts. Thermal and acoustic insulation, coatings and paints address comfort, protection and appearance.

Module 3

Building Components & Foundation Systems

A building is read as a coordinated set of substructure, superstructure, structural elements, openings, circulation and finishes.

Official hours: 8 L + 4 TMapped CO: CO3Bloom level: Apply

Learning goals

  • Identify substructure and superstructure.
  • Differentiate load-bearing and framed systems.
  • Explain walls, columns, beams, slabs and building elements.
  • Classify shallow and deep foundations.

Key facts

  • A foundation transfers load to soil.
  • Openings must meet functional requirements.
  • Foundation choice depends on load and soil conditions.

Engineering relevance

Use the concepts to interpret technical information, communicate decisions accurately, and solve appropriately scoped problems.

Basic components: substructure and superstructure

Substructure lies below or near ground level and includes foundations; superstructure rises above it and includes walls, columns, beams, slabs, roofs and enclosures. Draw a labelled section to show the relationship rather than presenting isolated definitions.

Load-bearing and non-load-bearing systems

In a load-bearing system, walls carry significant vertical loads. In a framed system, columns and beams form the main load path and partitions may be non-load-bearing. Compare load path, flexibility, openings, construction sequence and suitability without assuming one system is universally better.

Walls, columns, beams, slabs, doors, windows, staircases, roofs and finishes

Walls enclose or divide; columns carry axial and combined actions; beams transfer loads; slabs form floors/roofs. Doors provide controlled access, windows provide light/ventilation/views, staircases provide vertical movement, roofs shelter and finishes protect/complete surfaces. State functional requirements when describing each element.

Shallow and deep foundations

Shallow foundations distribute loads near the surface: strip, isolated, combined footing and raft are listed. Deep foundations transfer load to deeper strata or develop resistance through piles or piers when near-surface soil or loading conditions require it. Selection is a site and design decision, not a visual preference.

Module 4

Masonry, Roofing & Construction Technology

Construction technology connects material properties with repeatable site methods, safe temporary works, circulation and roof form.

Official hours: 7 L + 4 TMapped CO: CO4Bloom level: Apply

Learning goals

  • Differentiate stone, brick and block masonry.
  • Recall brick dimensions/properties and identify bonds.
  • Explain formwork and scaffolding.
  • Select basic roof forms and vertical-circulation systems.

Key facts

  • Bond controls joint arrangement and continuity.
  • Formwork is temporary but safety-critical.
  • Roof selection depends on form, material, climate, span and drainage.

Engineering relevance

Use the concepts to interpret technical information, communicate decisions accurately, and solve appropriately scoped problems.

Masonry, bricks and brick bonds

Stone, brick and block masonry differ in unit shape, jointing, material and construction technique. The syllabus requires standard brick sizes, properties and characteristics, plus English, Flemish, rat-trap, header and stretcher bonds. Draw bonds with clear bed and vertical joints.

Formwork and scaffolding

Formwork supports fresh concrete until it gains adequate strength. Materials may include timber, steel or proprietary systems; compare reuse, accuracy, cost, erection and stripping. Scaffolding provides temporary access and working platforms; describe purpose and types and include safe loading, bracing and access principles.

Safety note: Temporary works must be designed, inspected and used under competent supervision; never treat a sketch as a construction approval.
Vertical circulation and roofing systems

Lifts, stairs and escalators serve different movement patterns, capacities and accessibility needs. Roofs may be classified by shape, material and structural system. Flat, pitched, curved and trussed roofs require different drainage, span, covering and support considerations.

Rapid calculation reference

Formula bank

Water–cement ratio

w/c = mass of water ÷ mass of cement

Use consistent mass units; the ratio affects workability and strength.

Concrete grade

M20 indicates characteristic compressive-strength class conventionally associated with 20 N/mm² at the specified age

The syllabus lists M15, M20 and M25; do not treat the grade as a mix-proportion recipe by itself.

Area of rectangular slab

A = L×B

Use consistent length units and report square units.

Load path

Roof → slab/beam → wall/column → foundation → soil

This is a conceptual structural sequence; actual design requires qualified engineering analysis.

Module-wise question bank

Questions and answers

Answers are deliberately matched to the likely mark value: definitions are concise, while longer answers use structure, comparison, formulae or diagrams.

Module I — Materials

What is a green building material?
A material selected or designed to reduce environmental impact while meeting required performance and service needs.
State two timber defects.
Knots and warping; checks, shakes and decay are other examples.
Name four eco-friendly masonry blocks in the syllabus.
Clay bricks, fly ash bricks, AAC blocks and CSEB.

Module II — Concrete and finishes

What is PCC?
Plain cement concrete, without reinforcing steel as the defining distinction in this course context.
What does water-cement ratio mean?
The mass of water divided by the mass of cement in a concrete mixture.
List the main concreting stages.
Mixing, placing, compaction and curing, with appropriate transport and finishing.

Module III — Components and foundations

Differentiate substructure and superstructure.
Substructure includes foundations below/near ground; superstructure includes the building above the foundation level.
What is a load-bearing structure?
A system in which walls or other primary elements carry significant building loads to the foundation.
Name two deep foundation types.
Pile and pier foundations.

Module IV — Construction technology

Name two brick bonds.
English bond and Flemish bond; header, stretcher and rat-trap are also listed.
Why is formwork used?
To support and shape fresh concrete until it becomes self-supporting.
Name four roof forms.
Flat, pitched, curved and trussed roofs.
