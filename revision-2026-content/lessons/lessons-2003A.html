Official source declaration

Source identity and verified inconsistencies

This handbook is an original educational expansion of the supplied official SITTTR Kerala Diploma Curriculum Revision 2026 syllabus for Course 2003A — Chemistry for Engineering Practices. The suffix A is part of the course code and is preserved everywhere.

Semester-source inconsistency: the supplied course-description PDF prints “Semester I”, while the current Revision 2026 programme catalogue places code 2003A in Semester II. Website navigation follows the verified catalogue placement; the PDF wording is reproduced here without alteration.
Hour-allocation note: the detailed module table totals 45 theory hours and 39 practical hours (84), while the course description states 90 contact hours and the weekly scheme is 3:0:3:0. These official values are retained in their own contexts.
Assessment-table inconsistency: the course-description summary shows CIE 40 and ESE 60. The teaching-and-assessment table separately shows theory CIE 20 + ESE 60 and practical CIE 20 + ESE 0, while the detailed practicum methodology presents its own converted internal components and a 60-mark end-semester component. Some assessment values differ between the summary and detailed tables of the official syllabus. The values are reproduced in their respective contexts without alteration.

Official PDF course-description wording: “Chemistry for engineering practices is a foundational course designed to understand the basic concepts of Chemistry and their practical applications in engineering and daily life. This course enables students to develop problem-solving skills and gain knowledge necessary for selecting various engineering materials and addressing real-world industrial challenges.”

Four official objectives

  1. Build knowledge of atoms, molecules, solutions and thermodynamics and relate it to engineering.
  2. Apply analytical chemistry and water-treatment methods to domestic and industrial problems.
  3. Build electrochemistry and corrosion knowledge for engineering systems.
  4. Select engineering materials and understand green-chemistry principles.

Prerequisite

Basic knowledge of Chemistry from secondary education.

How to use this handbook

Study each theory chapter, inspect its diagram, attempt solved and practice problems, then complete at least two laboratory experiments from each module. Use the Formula Bank and Practical Record Centre before assessments.

Laboratory safety centre

Work safely before working quickly

NEVER PIPETTE BY MOUTH. Use a pipette filler. Formaldehyde, phenol, concentrated acids, concentrated bases and heated vapours require faculty supervision, ventilation and correct PPE. None of the listed experiments is a home activity.
CorrosiveAcids and strong bases can burn skin and eyes. Add acid to water, not water to acid.
ToxicAvoid inhalation, ingestion and skin contact. Use a fume hood where directed.
IrritantWear goggles and gloves; wash exposed skin immediately.
FlammableKeep away from flames, sparks and hot surfaces.
OxidisingKeep away from reducing agents and combustible material.
Environmental hazardDo not discharge metal-ion or resin waste into the sink.

Personal protection

  • Lab coat, splash goggles, suitable gloves and closed footwear.
  • Tie back long hair; secure loose clothing and jewellery.
  • Read chemical labels and Safety Data Sheets before use.

Glassware and instruments

  • Inspect burettes, pipettes and flasks for cracks.
  • Use a funnel only for filling, then remove it before titration readings.
  • Rinse pH and conductivity probes with distilled water; blot gently.
  • Never heat a closed system.

Emergency response

  • Alert faculty, isolate the area and use the correct spill kit.
  • Eyes: rinse at an eyewash for at least 15 minutes and obtain medical help.
  • Skin: remove contaminated clothing and rinse with water.
  • Broken glass: use brush and pan, never bare hands.
