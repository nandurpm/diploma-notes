Module I

Electrical energy consumption and conservation

15CO1
Power ഒരു സമയത്ത് ഉപയോഗിക്കുന്ന rate ആണ്; Energy ആ power എത്ര സമയം ഉപയോഗിച്ചു എന്നതിന്റെ ഫലമാണ്. Electricity bill kWh അടിസ്ഥാനത്തിലാണ്; watt മാത്രം നോക്കി monthly charge കണക്കാക്കാൻ കഴിയില്ല.

Power, energy and units

Define power and energy and relate them by E = Pt. Use watt, kilowatt, joule, watt-hour and kilowatt-hour correctly.

Daily and monthly consumption

For each appliance multiply rated power by operating hours; add energy of all loads and convert Wh to kWh.

Electricity-bill calculation

Apply tariff slabs, fixed charge, energy charge and other given components exactly as stated. Do not invent current tariff values; use the supplied tariff table.

Importance of conservation

Lower energy consumption reduces cost, peak demand, fuel use and environmental impact without reducing required service.

Domestic conservation practices

Avoid standby loss, choose correct appliance capacity, maintain refrigerator seals, use efficient lighting and operate loads during suitable periods.

BEE star rating

Interpret star level, annual energy consumption and label conditions. Compare appliances using comparable size and service requirements, not stars alone.

Selecting efficient appliances

Consider efficiency, annual energy, lifecycle cost, capacity, usage pattern, maintenance and safety certification.

Rules / common mistakes

  • Convert W to kW before multiplying by hours for kWh.
  • Use actual operating time, not 24 hours by default.
  • Compare appliances of similar capacity.
  • Tariff is an input to the problem and may change.

Practice and self-learning

  • Prepare a one-day and one-month household load schedule.
  • Calculate a sample bill from a supplied tariff table.
  • Compare two appliances using BEE label data and lifecycle energy.

Module II

Applications and conversion of electrical energy

15CO2
Electrical energy light, heat, chemical, mechanical energy ആയി convert ചെയ്യാം. ഓരോ application-ലും conversion principle, losses, efficiency, control, safety എന്നിവ വേർതിരിച്ച് മനസ്സിലാക്കണം.

Lighting conversion

Explain construction and working of incandescent, fluorescent and LED lamps and compare efficacy, life, starting, control and energy use.

Resistance heating

Heat is produced by I²Rt. Explain ovens, irons and water heaters, heating-element materials and temperature control.

Arc heating

An electric arc produces very high temperature; describe furnaces and welding applications with safety precautions.

Induction heating

Alternating magnetic field induces eddy currents in conductive workpieces. Explain rapid, clean and localised heating applications.

Dielectric heating

High-frequency electric field produces dielectric loss in insulating materials; describe industrial drying and bonding uses.

Electrolysis and Faraday laws

Explain electrolyte, electrodes and ion movement. State Faraday’s laws and applications in electroplating, metal refining and battery charging.

Solenoid and relay

Current in a coil creates magnetic force. Explain construction, working, contacts, coil rating and applications.

DC motor

Explain force on a current-carrying conductor, commutation at an elementary level and common applications.

Rules / common mistakes

  • Select conversion method based on material and required temperature.
  • Heating equipment requires insulation, earthing and temperature protection.
  • Relay coil and contact ratings are different.
  • Observe polarity and ventilation in electrochemical work.

Practice and self-learning

  • Compare incandescent, fluorescent and LED lamps.
  • Prepare a table comparing four electric-heating methods.
  • Draw working diagrams of solenoid, relay and elementary DC motor.

Module III

Magnetism, magnetic circuits and B–H characteristics

15CO3
Electric circuit-ൽ current flow-നെ resistance oppose ചെയ്യുന്നതുപോലെ magnetic circuit-ൽ flux-നെ reluctance oppose ചെയ്യുന്നു. Air gap ചെറിയതായാലും reluctance വളരെ കൂടാം, കാരണം air permeability കുറവാണ്.

Magnetic quantities

Define magnetic field, flux Φ, flux density B, field strength H, absolute permeability μ and relative permeability μr with units.

Magnetic materials

Differentiate diamagnetic, paramagnetic and ferromagnetic materials and identify engineering applications.

MMF and reluctance

Magnetomotive force F = NI drives flux; reluctance ℜ = l/(μA) opposes it. Use Φ = F/ℜ as the magnetic-circuit analogue of Ohm’s law.

Electric versus magnetic circuits

Compare emf/MMF, current/flux, resistance/reluctance and conductivity/permeability while noting that magnetic circuits are nonlinear.

Series magnetic circuits

For a common flux path, add reluctances and compute required ampere-turns. Handle different materials and cross-sections.

Air-gap circuits

Include gap reluctance using μ0; neglect fringing only when allowed. Air gap usually dominates required MMF.

Parallel magnetic circuits

Common MMF appears across parallel branches and total flux is the sum of branch fluxes.

B–H curve and hysteresis

Explain initial magnetisation, saturation, retentivity, coercivity and loop area. Relate loop area to hysteresis loss and material selection.

Rules / common mistakes

  • Use metres and square metres in reluctance calculations.
  • Check whether flux is common or divides.
  • Use correct permeability for each section.
  • Do not assume constant μ in saturation unless the problem permits it.

Practice and self-learning

  • Identify magnetic materials in transformers, motors and relays.
  • Solve series and air-gap magnetic circuits.
  • Solve one parallel magnetic circuit and interpret a hysteresis loop.

Module IV

Bipolar junction transistor and biasing

15CO4
BJT active region-ൽ amplifier ആയി, cut-off/saturation regions-ൽ switch ആയി ഉപയോഗിക്കാം. Biasing Q-point സ്ഥിരമായി നിലനിർത്താൻ വേണ്ടതാണ്; β മാത്രം ആശ്രയിക്കുന്ന fixed bias stability കുറവായിരിക്കും.

Operating regions

Explain cutoff, active and saturation regions using junction bias conditions and their use in switching/amplification.

Current gains α and β

Define α = IC/IE and β = IC/IB. Use β = α/(1−α) and α = β/(β+1), with IE = IC + IB.

BJT characteristics

Explain input, output and transfer characteristics and locate cutoff, active, saturation and breakdown regions.

Common-base configuration

Low input resistance, high output resistance, current gain less than one and good high-frequency response.

Common-emitter configuration

Moderate input/output resistance, high current/voltage/power gain and 180° phase reversal; widely used amplifier configuration.

Common-collector configuration

High input resistance, low output resistance, voltage gain near one; emitter follower used for buffering.

Need for biasing and stability

Set a suitable Q-point and prevent large shift with temperature, leakage current and β variation.

Fixed and collector-to-base bias

Compute resistor values for a specified operating point and compare feedback/stability.

Voltage-divider bias

Use divider voltage and emitter resistance to reduce dependence on β; calculate base, emitter and collector quantities.

Stability factor

Explain the concept and why a lower stability factor is preferred.

Rules / common mistakes

  • State the assumed silicon VBE when solving.
  • Check that calculated Q-point lies in active region.
  • Do not treat β as a precise constant.
  • Include emitter resistor voltage when analysing divider bias.

Practice and self-learning

  • Plot a qualitative CE characteristic and identify operating regions.
  • Compare CB, CE and CC in a table.
  • Design fixed-bias, collector-feedback and voltage-divider-bias resistors for a given Q-point.

B = Φ/A

Rule / Formula 6

H = NI/l

Rule / Formula 7

MMF F = NI

Rule / Formula 8

Reluctance ℜ = l/(μA)

Rule / Formula 9

Flux Φ = NI/ℜ

Rule / Formula 10

β = IC/IB; α = IC/IE

Rule / Formula 11

β = α/(1−α); α = β/(β+1)

Rule / Formula 12

IE = IC + IB

Rule / Formula 13

Fixed bias: RB ≈ (VCC−VBE)/IB

Rule / Formula 14

Voltage-divider approximation: VB = VCC R2/(R1+R2); VE = VB−VBE

Worked checkpoints

Worked checkpoint 1

A 1.5 kW heater used 2 h/day for 30 days consumes 90 kWh.

Worked checkpoint 2

A magnetic path with NI = 800 At and reluctance 2×10⁶ At/Wb has flux 0.4 mWb.

Worked checkpoint 3

For β = 99, α = 99/100 = 0.99.

Worked checkpoint 4

For VCC 12 V, desired IC 2 mA and VCE 6 V with emitter at 1 V, RC ≈ (12−6−1)/2 mA = 2.5 kΩ.
