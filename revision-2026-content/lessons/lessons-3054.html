Objectives, Course Outcomes and CO–PO Information

Official course objectives

  1. To understand the different fuel systems and components in Petrol and Diesel engines.
  2. To impart basic knowledge on the necessity of lubrication and cooling systems in automobiles.
  3. To familiarize with intake and exhaust systems.

Course Outcomes

  1. Identify fuel systems employed in petrol vehicles.
  2. Illustrate the working of fuel system employed in diesel vehicles.
  3. Explain the working of Engine lubrication and cooling systems.
  4. Outline the details of components in Intake and Exhaust systems used in engines.

M1.01 — Conventional petrol fuel system (3 hours)

Concept and explanation

A conventional petrol system uses tank, feed pump, filter, air cleaner and carburettor to prepare a combustible mixture. The air–fuel ratio must vary between starting, idling, acceleration and cruising.

M1.02 — Carburettor components and circuits (5 hours)

Concept and explanation

A simple carburettor meters fuel using pressure difference. Circuits for float control, idle, progression, main operation, acceleration and enrichment support different engine conditions.

M1.03 — Electronic fuel injection systems (3 hours)

Concept and explanation

EFI meters fuel electronically using sensors, an electronic control unit and injectors. Single-point, multi-point and direct injection differ in injector location and control strategy.

M1.04 — Gasoline direct injection and diagnostics (4 hours)

Fuel-injection pumpsJerk and distributor pumps create and distribute high-pressure fuel. Inline pumps use separate pumping elements, whereas distributor pumps use one pumping element with distribution.
Common-rail diesel systemA common-rail system separates pressure generation from injection timing. Tank, delivery pump, high-pressure pump, rail, pressure sensor, electronic injectors, relief valve, glow plugs and emission controls operate as a coordinated system.
Lubricating oil properties and systemsViscosity, viscosity index, flash point and additives influence oil performance. Petroil, splash, pressure, wet-sump and dry-sump systems deliver oil to moving parts in different ways.
Oil pumps, filters and coolersGear, vane and rotor pumps create flow; filters remove particles; coolers control oil temperature; relief valves protect the circuit from excessive pressure. Full-flow and bypass filtration have different arrangements.
Air and water coolingAir cooling transfers heat through fins, whereas water cooling circulates coolant through jackets, pump, radiator and hoses. The system must reject heat without excessive temperature variation.
Thermostat, radiator and antifreezeA thermostat controls warm-up and operating temperature. Radiators, expansion tanks, pressure caps and antifreeze solutions manage heat transfer, volume change, boiling protection and corrosion.
Valve timing and intake technologiesIntake manifolds and air cleaners guide and condition air. Variable valve timing and variable intake systems alter breathing with speed and load; electronic throttle controls air demand.
Mufflers and exhaust treatmentExhaust manifolds collect gases; resonators and mufflers reduce noise; catalytic converters assist emission control. Absorber, baffle, wave-cancellation and resonance types use different acoustic principles.
Turbocharging systemA turbocharger uses exhaust energy to drive a turbine connected to a compressor. Compressed intake air increases charge density, but heat, lubrication, control and response must be managed.
Turbocharger types and turbo lagNormal, waste-gate and variable-geometry turbochargers control boost differently. Turbo lag is the delay before useful boost develops after a change in engine demand.
