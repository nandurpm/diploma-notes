Course introduction

Electronic Instrumentation studies the principles, components and application of electronic measurement systems. It develops the ability to analyze transducer characteristics, evaluate signal conditioning circuits, understand the operation of digital measuring instruments and oscilloscopes, and coordinate industrial instrumentation solutions while respecting the technical and safety boundaries of electronic and measurement engineering.

Module 3 — Data Acquisition and Signal Processing

Explain Data Acquisition Systems (DAS) and state one correct method, application or precaution.

DAS collects data from multiple sensors, processes it, and stores or displays it. A typical system includes transducers, signal conditioners, multiplexers, Sample-and-Hold (S/H) circuits, and A/D converters.

Answer framework: Sketch the block diagram of a 'Multi-channel Data Acquisition System'; explain the role of the 'Sample and Hold' circuit.

Important point: Aliasing can occur if the sampling rate is too low. Do not design DAS without authorised adherence to the Nyquist sampling theorem.

Explain Signal Processing and Virtual Instrumentation and state one correct method, application or precaution.

Active filters (Low-pass, High-pass) are used to remove noise from sensor signals. Virtual instrumentation uses software (like LabVIEW) and modular hardware to create user-defined measurement systems, offering more flexibility than traditional instruments.

Answer framework: Explain the purpose of an 'Instrumentation Amplifier' in signal conditioning; describe the concept of 'Virtual Instrumentation'.

Important point: Software-based measurement can have latency issues. Do not use virtual instruments for high-speed real-time control without authorised real-time operating system (RTOS) support.

Module 4 — Electronic Measuring Instruments

Explain Digital Measuring Instruments and state one correct method, application or precaution.

Digital instruments provide high accuracy and resolution. DVMs use various A/D conversion techniques like the 'Dual Slope' method for noise rejection. DMMs integrate multiple measurement functions (V, I, R) into a single handheld device.

Answer framework: Describe the working of a 'Dual Slope DVM' conceptually; list three advantages of 'Digital Multimeters' over analog types.

Important point: Improper range selection in DMMs can damage the instrument or cause injury. Always follow authorised safety procedures and start from the highest range when measuring unknown values.

Explain Oscilloscopes and Analyzers and state one correct method, application or precaution.

The CRO is a versatile tool for visualizing waveforms. The DSO (Digital Storage Oscilloscope) digitizes and stores the waveform, allowing for advanced analysis and pre-trigger viewing. Spectrum Analyzers are used to view signals in the frequency domain.

Answer framework: Sketch the block diagram of a 'CRO'; explain the 'Storage' principle in a DSO.

Important point: High-voltage measurements on oscilloscopes require specialized probes. Do not connect standard probes to mains or high-voltage circuits without authorised high-voltage differential probes.
