Course Overview

A complete mathematics handbook, not a formula sheet

Course 1002 develops the exact mathematical tools needed to organise data, solve simultaneous relations, analyse angles and geometry, and calculate limits and rates of change. The emphasis is correct method selection, clear working and verification.

Module I

Matrices and Determinants

Represent data in rectangular form, perform operations, evaluate Determinants and solve systems of Linear Equations.

11 Lecture Hours4 Tutorial HoursCO1
1.1 Matrix, elements, order and types

A Matrix is a rectangular arrangement of numbers or quantities in rows and columns. If it has m rows and n columns, its Order is m × n. The entry in row i and column j is written aij.

Malayalam explanation: Matrix എന്നത് numbers അല്ലെങ്കിൽ quantities rows-ലും columns-ലും ക്രമീകരിച്ച rectangular arrangement ആണ്. Matrix-ന്റെ size അതിന്റെ Order ഉപയോഗിച്ച് എഴുതുന്നു.
Required Matrix types
TypeRecognition ruleExample
Row MatrixExactly one row[2 −1 5]
Column MatrixExactly one column[3; 0; −4]
Rectangular MatrixRows and columns are unequal2 × 3
Square MatrixRows = columns2 × 2, 3 × 3
Diagonal MatrixAll non-diagonal entries are zerodiag(4, −2, 7)
Identity MatrixDiagonal entries 1; all others 0I₂
Upper TriangularEntries below main diagonal are zero[[2,1,3],[0,5,4],[0,0,6]]
Lower TriangularEntries above main diagonal are zero[[2,0,0],[1,5,0],[3,4,6]]
Zero MatrixEvery entry is zeroO2×3
Engineering application: Matrices organise current measurements, simultaneous circuit relations, CAD coordinates, production data and computer transformations.
1.2 Equality, Transpose, scalar multiplication, addition and subtraction

Two Matrices are equal only when they have the same Order and all corresponding entries are equal. The Transpose AT is formed by converting rows into columns.

If A = [[a,b,c],[d,e,f]], then AT = [[a,d],[b,e],[c,f]].
Malayalam explanation: Transpose എടുക്കുമ്പോൾ Matrix-ന്റെ rows columns ആയും columns rows ആയും മാറുന്നു.
  • Scalar multiplication: multiply every entry by the scalar.
  • Addition/Subtraction: Matrices must have the same Order; operate on corresponding entries.
Common mistake: never add Matrices of different Orders.
1.3 Matrix multiplication

AB exists only when the number of columns of A equals the number of rows of B. Each entry is obtained by a row-by-column product.

If A is m × n and B is n × p, then AB is m × p.
Select one row from A.
Select one column from B.
Multiply matching entries.
Add the products to obtain one entry of AB.
Malayalam explanation: Matrix Multiplication element-by-element multiplication അല്ല. First Matrix-ന്റെ row, second Matrix-ന്റെ column എന്നിവ ഉപയോഗിച്ചാണ് ഓരോ answer entry കണ്ടെത്തുന്നത്.
Remember: generally AB ≠ BA. One product may exist while the reverse product does not.
1.4 Determinants of order 2 and 3

A Determinant is a scalar value associated with a Square Matrix.

For [[a,b],[c,d]], |A| = ad − bc.
For a 3 × 3 Determinant expanded along row 1:
|A| = a₁₁M₁₁ − a₁₂M₁₂ + a₁₃M₁₃.
Malayalam explanation: Determinant Matrix അല്ല; Square Matrix-നോട് ബന്ധപ്പെട്ട ഒരു single value ആണ്. 3 × 3 expansion-ൽ sign pattern ശ്രദ്ധിക്കണം.
Choose a row or column containing zeros when expansion choice is available.
1.5 Cramer’s Rule for two and three unknowns

Cramer’s Rule solves a square system when the coefficient Determinant Δ is non-zero.

x = Δx/Δ, y = Δy/Δ, and for three unknowns z = Δz/Δ.
Write the coefficient Determinant Δ.
Replace one coefficient column by the constants to form Δx, Δy and Δz.
Evaluate each Determinant.
Divide by Δ and verify in the original equations.
Malayalam explanation: Cramer’s Rule-ൽ unknown കണ്ടെത്താൻ അതിന്റെ coefficient column constant terms ഉപയോഗിച്ച് replace ചെയ്ത് പുതിയ Determinant calculate ചെയ്യുന്നു.
If Δ = 0, the direct Cramer formula cannot give a unique solution.
1.6 Minors, Cofactors, Adjoint and Inverse of a 2 × 2 Matrix

The Minor Mij is obtained by deleting row i and column j. The Cofactor is Cij = (−1)i+jMij.

Cofactor sign pattern
+−+
−+−
+−+
For A=[[a,b],[c,d]], adj A=[[d,−b],[−c,a]] and A−1=(1/(ad−bc))[[d,−b],[−c,a]], provided ad−bc ≠ 0.
Find Minors.
Apply Cofactor signs.
Form the Cofactor Matrix.
Transpose it to obtain the Adjoint.
Divide by the Determinant to obtain the Inverse.
Malayalam explanation: Determinant zero ആണെങ്കിൽ Matrix-ന് Inverse ഉണ്ടായിരിക്കില്ല. Inverse കിട്ടിയ ശേഷം AA−1 = I ഉപയോഗിച്ച് verify ചെയ്യാം.

Module I worked examples

Example 1 — Order and Transpose

For A=[[3,−1,2],[0,4,5]], state the Order and find AT.

A has 2 rows and 3 columns, so its Order is 2 × 3.
Rows become columns.
AT=[[3,0],[−1,4],[2,5]].

Example 2 — Matrix multiplication

A=[[1,2],[3,4]], B=[[2,0],[−1,5]]. Find AB.

(AB)₁₁=1(2)+2(−1)=0; (AB)₁₂=1(0)+2(5)=10.
(AB)₂₁=3(2)+4(−1)=2; (AB)₂₂=3(0)+4(5)=20.
AB=[[0,10],[2,20]].

Example 3 — 3 × 3 Determinant

Evaluate |A| for A=[[1,2,3],[0,4,5],[1,0,6]].

Expand row 1: 1(24) − 2(−5) + 3(−4).
24 + 10 − 12 = 22.
|A| = 22.

Example 4 — Cramer’s Rule, two unknowns

Solve 2x+y=7 and x−y=2.

Δ=|2 1;1 −1|=−3.
Δx=|7 1;2 −1|=−9, Δy=|2 7;1 2|=−3.
x=3, y=1.
x=3, y=1. Verification: 2(3)+1=7 and 3−1=2.

Example 5 — Cramer’s Rule, three unknowns

Solve x+y+z=6, 2x−y+z=3, x+2y−z=2.

Δ=7.
Δx=7, Δy=14, Δz=21.
x=1, y=2, z=3.
x=1, y=2, z=3.

Example 6 — Inverse

Find the Inverse of A=[[2,1],[5,3]].

|A|=6−5=1.
adj A=[[3,−1],[−5,2]].
A−1=[[3,−1],[−5,2]].

Module II

Trigonometry

Use angles, Trigonometric Ratios, identities, ASTC, Reduction Formulae and Compound Angles.

11 Lecture Hours4 Tutorial HoursCO2
2.1 Angles and standard position

An Angle is formed by rotating the Terminal Side from the Initial Side about the vertex. Anticlockwise rotation is positive; clockwise rotation is negative.

Angle classification
TypeRange
Acute0° < θ < 90°
Right90°
Obtuse90° < θ < 180°
Straight180°
Reflex180° < θ < 360°
Complete360°
Malayalam explanation: Positive Angle സാധാരണ anticlockwise direction-ൽ measure ചെയ്യുന്നു. Negative Angle clockwise direction-ൽ measure ചെയ്യുന്നു.
2.2 Six Trigonometric Ratios and standard angles
sinθ=Opposite/Hypotenuse, cosθ=Adjacent/Hypotenuse, tanθ=Opposite/Adjacent.
cosecθ=1/sinθ, secθ=1/cosθ, cotθ=1/tanθ.
Standard-angle values

sinθ01/21/√2√3/21

Malayalam explanation: Trigonometric Ratios right triangle-ന്റെ side ratios ആണ്. Technical terms English-ൽ തന്നെ ഉപയോഗിച്ച് side relation മനസ്സിലാക്കുക.
2.3 Reciprocal, quotient and Pythagorean identities
tanθ=sinθ/cosθ, cotθ=cosθ/sinθ.
sin²θ+cos²θ=1; 1+tan²θ=sec²θ; 1+cot²θ=cosec²θ.

These identities allow unknown Ratios to be calculated from a given Ratio. For acute angles, all Ratios are positive.

2.4 Trigonometric Ratios of any angle and ASTC Rule

For any Angle, identify the Quadrant and Reference Angle. Use ASTC to determine the sign.

ASTC sign rule
QuadrantPositive Ratios
IAll
IISine and cosecant
IIITangent and cotangent
IVCosine and secant
Malayalam explanation: ASTC Rule ഉപയോഗിച്ച് ഓരോ Quadrant-ലും ഏത് Trigonometric Ratios positive ആണെന്ന് കണ്ടെത്താം.
2.5 Reduction Formulae

Reduction Formulae simplify Ratios of 90°±θ, 180°±θ, 270°±θ, 360°±θ and −θ.

Identify the Quadrant.
Find the sign using ASTC.
For 90° or 270°, change sin↔cos, tan↔cot, sec↔cosec.
For 180° or 360°, retain the function.
Write the Reference Angle and sign.
Do not decide the sign from memory alone. Identify the Quadrant first.
2.6 Compound Angles
sin(A±B)=sinA cosB ± cosA sinB
cos(A±B)=cosA cosB ∓ sinA sinB
tan(A±B)=(tanA±tanB)/(1∓tanA tanB)

Split a non-standard Angle into standard Angles such as 45°±30°.

Malayalam explanation: Compound Angle Formula ഉപയോഗിക്കുമ്പോൾ A, B ആയി convenient standard angles തിരഞ്ഞെടുക്കുക. cos formula-യിലെ sign പ്രത്യേകമായി ശ്രദ്ധിക്കുക.

Module II worked examples

Example 1 — Ratios from one Ratio

If sinθ=3/5 for an Acute Angle, find cosθ and tanθ.

Opposite=3, Hypotenuse=5.
Adjacent=√(25−9)=4.
cosθ=4/5, tanθ=3/4.

Example 2 — Pythagorean identity

If tanθ=5/12, find secθ.

1+tan²θ=sec²θ.
sec²θ=1+25/144=169/144.
secθ=13/12 for an Acute Angle.

Example 3 — ASTC

Evaluate sin150°.

150° lies in Quadrant II, where sine is positive.
Reference Angle=180°−150°=30°.
sin150°=sin30°=1/2.

Example 4 — Reduction Formula

Evaluate cos(270°−30°).

Angle=240°, Quadrant III, cosine negative.
270° changes cos to −sin for this form.
cos240°=−1/2.

Example 5 — Compound Angle

Find sin75°.

75°=45°+30°.
sin75°=sin45°cos30°+cos45°sin30°.
sin75°=(√6+√2)/4.

Example 6 — Compound cosine

Find cos15°.

15°=45°−30°.
cos15°=cos45°cos30°+sin45°sin30°.
cos15°=(√6+√2)/4.

Module III

Coordinate Geometry

Interpret Straight Lines and Circles using Slope, equations and geometric relationships.

11 Lecture Hours4 Tutorial HoursCO3
3.1 Cartesian plane and Slope

The Cartesian plane contains the x-axis, y-axis, Origin and four Quadrants. A point is written as an ordered pair (x,y).

Slope m=(y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁)=tanθ.
  • Positive Slope: line rises from left to right.
  • Negative Slope: line falls from left to right.
  • Zero Slope: horizontal line.
  • Undefined Slope: vertical line.
Malayalam explanation: Slope ഒരു Straight Line എത്ര steep ആണെന്നും ഏത് direction-ലാണ് ഉയരുന്നത് അല്ലെങ്കിൽ താഴുന്നത് എന്നും കാണിക്കുന്നു.
3.2 Slope–Point Form
y−y₁=m(x−x₁)
Write the given point (x₁,y₁).
Write the given or calculated Slope m.
Substitute in Slope–Point Form.
Simplify and verify the point.
3.3 Angle Between Lines, Parallel Lines and Perpendicular Lines
tanθ=|(m₂−m₁)/(1+m₁m₂)|
Parallel: m₁=m₂. Perpendicular: m₁m₂=−1.
Malayalam explanation: Parallel Lines-ന് same Slope ആയിരിക്കും. Perpendicular Lines-ന്റെ Slopes negative reciprocals ആയിരിക്കും.
For a vertical line, Slope is undefined; use the geometric condition directly.
3.4 Circle with Centre and Radius
(x−h)²+(y−k)²=r², Centre=(h,k), Radius=r.

When the Centre is the Origin, the equation becomes x²+y²=r².

3.5 General Equation of a Circle
x²+y²+2gx+2fy+c=0
Centre=(−g,−f), Radius=√(g²+f²−c).
Identify 2g, 2f and c.
Calculate g and f.
Change their signs for the Centre.
Calculate the Radius.
Malayalam explanation: General Equation-ൽ x coefficient 2g, y coefficient 2f ആണ്. Centre കണ്ടെത്തുമ്പോൾ signs മാറ്റണം.

Module III worked examples

Example 1 — Slope from two points

Find the Slope through (2,3) and (6,11).

m=(11−3)/(6−2)=8/4.
m=2.

Example 2 — Line through a point

Find the equation through (2,−1) with Slope 3.

y+1=3(x−2).
y=3x−7.
3x−y−7=0.

Example 3 — Angle Between Lines

Find the acute Angle between lines of Slopes 1 and 3.

tanθ=|(3−1)/(1+3)|=1/2.
θ=tan−1(1/2)≈26.565°.

Example 4 — Parallel line

Find the line through (1,4) parallel to y=2x+3.

Parallel Slope=2.
y−4=2(x−1).
y=2x+2.

Example 5 — Circle from Centre and Radius

Centre (2,−3), Radius 4.

(x−2)²+(y+3)²=16.

Example 6 — Centre and Radius

Find Centre and Radius of x²+y²−6x+4y−12=0.

2g=−6→g=−3; 2f=4→f=2; c=−12.
Centre=(3,−2).
r=√(9+4+12)=5.
Centre=(3,−2), Radius=5.

Module IV

Differential Calculus

Evaluate Limits and Derivatives using direct substitution, factorisation and standard differentiation rules.

12 Lecture Hours3 Tutorial HoursCO4
4.1 Concept of a Limit

A Limit describes the value approached by f(x) when x approaches a specified value. The Limit may exist even when direct substitution initially gives an indeterminate form.

Malayalam explanation: Limit ഒരു function-ന്റെ input ഒരു value-നോട് അടുത്തുവരുമ്പോൾ output ഏത് value-നോട് അടുത്തുവരുന്നു എന്ന് കാണിക്കുന്നു.
4.2 Direct Substitution and Factorisation
  • Direct Substitution: use when the denominator does not become zero.
  • Factorisation: for 0/0, factorise, cancel the common factor and substitute again.
Never cancel terms across addition or subtraction. Cancel only common factors.
4.3 Standard Limits
limx→a(xⁿ−aⁿ)/(x−a)=naⁿ⁻¹
limx→0sinx/x=1, where x is in radians.
4.4 Meaning and notation of Differentiation

The Derivative measures the rate of change of a function and the Slope of its Tangent. Common notations are dy/dx and f′(x).

Malayalam explanation: Differentiation ഒരു quantity മറ്റൊരു quantity-നോട് എത്ര വേഗത്തിൽ മാറുന്നു എന്ന് കണ്ടെത്താൻ ഉപയോഗിക്കുന്നു. Graph-ൽ ഇത് Tangent-ന്റെ Slope ആയി കാണാം.

d[af(x)+bg(x)]/dx = af′(x)+bg′(x)

4.7 Second Derivative

Differentiate the first Derivative again. Notations are d²y/dx² and f″(x).

Malayalam explanation: Second Derivative ചോദിച്ചാൽ first Derivative കിട്ടിയ ശേഷം വീണ്ടും Differentiate ചെയ്യണം.

Quick Revision

Last-day checklist and common mistakes

Module I

  • Check Matrix Orders first.
  • Use row-by-column multiplication.
  • Track Cofactor signs.
  • For Inverse, verify |A|≠0.

Module II

  • Find Quadrant before sign.
  • 90°/270° changes function.
  • 180°/360° retains function.
  • Watch signs in cos(A±B).

Module III

  • Keep point order consistent.
  • Parallel: same Slope.
  • Perpendicular: negative reciprocal.
  • Change signs for Circle Centre.

Module IV

  • Substitute before factorising.
  • sinx/x uses radians.
  • Product Rule has two terms.
  • Second Derivative means differentiate again.
