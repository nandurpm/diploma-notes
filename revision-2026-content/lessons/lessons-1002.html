Course Overview

What Course 1002 expects

Engineering Mathematics is not a proof-heavy university text. The target is correct selection and application of standard methods.

Course purpose

The course supplies the mathematical tools required to represent quantities, solve simultaneous relations, analyse angles and geometry, and calculate limits and rates of change in engineering situations.

Prerequisites

  • Linear and quadratic equations
  • Rectangular coordinates
  • Angles and basic arithmetic
  • Secondary-education-level algebra

Teaching and assessment

60 contact hours with L:T:P:R = 3:1:0:0. Continuous Internal Evaluation carries 40 marks and the End Semester Examination carries 60 marks.

Read the idea, note the formula, follow the method, solve the worked example, then complete the short practice without looking at the answer.

Study Method

How to use this handbook

1. Learn

Read one topic at a time. Copy the formula and understand what each symbol means.

2. Practise

Cover the solution and repeat the numbered steps. Do not jump directly to the final answer.

3. Check

Matrices and Determinants

Represent data in rectangular form, perform matrix operations, evaluate determinants and solve simultaneous equations.

1.1 Matrix, order and special types

A matrix is a rectangular arrangement of numbers in rows and columns. If a matrix has m rows and n columns, its order is m × n. The element in row i, column j is written aij.

TypeRecognition ruleExample
Row matrixOne row[2   −1   5]

Square matrixRows = columns2 × 2 or 3 × 3
Diagonal matrixAll off-diagonal elements are zerodiag(4, −2, 7)

Order first: two matrices can be equal, added or subtracted only when their orders are the same.
1.2 Transpose and algebra of matrices

The transpose AT is formed by changing rows into columns. If A is m × n, then AT is n × m.

If A = [[a,b,c],[d,e,f]], then AT = [[a,d],[b,e],[c,f]].
OperationCondition / ruleResult order
Scalar multiplicationMultiply every element by the scalar kSame as A
EqualitySame order and corresponding elements equalStatement A = B

ABColumns of A = rows of B(rows of A) × (columns of B)
Common mistake: matrix multiplication is not element-by-element multiplication. Use row-by-column products. In general, AB ≠ BA.
1.3 Determinants, minors and cofactors

A determinant is a number associated with a square matrix. For A = [[a,b],[c,d]], |A| = ad − bc.

For a 3 × 3 determinant expanded along row 1:
|A| = a₁₁M₁₁ − a₁₂M₁₂ + a₁₃M₁₃.

The minor Mij is the determinant remaining after deleting row i and column j. The cofactor is Cij = (−1)i+jMij.

Cofactor sign pattern
+−+
−+−
+−+
Expansion may be taken along any row or column. Choose one containing zeros when possible.
1.4 Adjoint and inverse of a 2 × 2 matrix

For A = [[a,b],[c,d]], interchange the main diagonal elements and change the signs of the off-diagonal elements.

adj A = [[d,−b],[−c,a]]
A−1 = 1/(ad−bc) [[d,−b],[−c,a]], provided ad−bc ≠ 0.
Determinant zero ആണെങ്കിൽ matrix-ന് inverse ഉണ്ടായിരിക്കില്ല.
Check the determinant before dividing. Writing an inverse when |A| = 0 is mathematically invalid.
1.5 Cramer’s rule for two and three unknowns

Cramer’s rule applies to a square system when the coefficient determinant Δ is non-zero.

Write the coefficient determinant Δ.
Replace one coefficient column at a time by the constant column to obtain Δx, Δy and, where required, Δz.
Calculate x = Δx/Δ, y = Δy/Δ and z = Δz/Δ.
Substitute the answers in the original equations to check.
Engineering mini-application: simultaneous equations occur in current balance, force balance, production mixing and calibration problems. Cramer’s rule converts the coefficient data into determinant calculations.

Worked examples

Solved Example 1.1: Order, type and transpose

Question. For A = [[3, −1, 2],[0, 4, 5]], state the order and find Aᵀ.

Rule. Rows become columns.
A has 2 rows and 3 columns, so its order is 2 × 3.
The first row [3, −1, 2] becomes the first column.
The second row [0, 4, 5] becomes the second column.
Final answer: Aᵀ = [[3,0],[−1,4],[2,5]].

Check: The order changes from 2 × 3 to 3 × 2.

Solved Example 1.2: Addition and multiplication

Question. Let A=[[1,2],[3,4]] and B=[[2,0],[−1,5]]. Find A+B and AB.

Rule. For AB, multiply each row of A by each column of B.
A+B = [[1+2, 2+0],[3−1, 4+5]] = [[3,2],[2,9]].
(AB)₁₁ = 1(2)+2(−1)=0; (AB)₁₂=1(0)+2(5)=10.
(AB)₂₁ = 3(2)+4(−1)=2; (AB)₂₂=3(0)+4(5)=20.
Final answer: A+B=[[3,2],[2,9]] and AB=[[0,10],[2,20]].

Check: BA is different, which confirms multiplication is order-sensitive.

Solved Example 1.3: Determinant, minor and cofactor

Question. Evaluate |A| for A=[[1,2,3],[0,4,5],[1,0,6]]. Also find M₁₂ and C₁₂.

Rule. Expand along the first row; C₁₂=−M₁₂.
|A| = 1(4·6−5·0) − 2(0·6−5·1) + 3(0·0−4·1).
|A| = 24 − 2(−5) + 3(−4) = 24+10−12 = 22.
Delete row 1 and column 2: M₁₂ = |0 5; 1 6| = 0−5 = −5.
C₁₂ = −M₁₂ = 5.
Final answer: |A|=22, M₁₂=−5 and C₁₂=5.

Solved Example 1.4: Inverse and a two-variable system

Question. Find A⁻¹ for A=[[2,1],[5,3]], then solve 2x+y=7 and 5x+3y=19 by Cramer’s rule.

Rule. A⁻¹=(1/|A|)adj A; x=Δx/Δ and y=Δy/Δ.
|A| = 2·3−1·5 = 1, so A⁻¹=[[3,−1],[−5,2]].
Δ = |2 1; 5 3| = 1.
Δx = |7 1; 19 3| = 21−19=2.
Δy = |2 7; 5 19| = 38−35=3.
Final answer: A⁻¹=[[3,−1],[−5,2]], x=2 and y=3.

Check: 2(2)+3=7 and 5(2)+3(3)=19.

Solved Example 1.5: Three-variable Cramer’s rule

Question. Solve x+y+z=6, 2x−y+z=3, and x+2y−z=2.

Rule. Calculate Δ, then replace each coefficient column by [6,3,2]ᵀ.
Δ = |1 1 1; 2 −1 1; 1 2 −1| = 7.
Δx = |6 1 1; 3 −1 1; 2 2 −1| = 7.
Δy = |1 6 1; 2 3 1; 1 2 −1| = 14.
Δz = |1 1 6; 2 −1 3; 1 2 2| = 21.
x=7/7=1, y=14/7=2, z=21/7=3.
Final answer: x=1, y=2, z=3.

Check: All three original equations are satisfied.

Worked examples

Solved Example 2.1: Standard-angle value

Question. Evaluate sin 60° cos 30° + cos 60° sin 30°.

Rule. Recognise sin(A+B).
sin 60° cos 30° + cos 60° sin 30° = sin(60°+30°).
sin 90° = 1.
Final answer: 1.

Solved Example 2.2: One acute ratio given

Question. If tan θ=3/4 and θ is acute, find sin θ and cos θ.

Rule. Use a right triangle with opposite 3 and adjacent 4.
Let opposite=3 and adjacent=4.
Hypotenuse=√(3²+4²)=5.
sin θ=3/5 and cos θ=4/5.
Final answer: sin θ=3/5, cos θ=4/5.

Solved Example 2.3: ASTC and reduction

Question. Find sin 150°, cos 210° and tan 330°.

Rule. Use reference angles and quadrant signs.
150°=180°−30° lies in quadrant II: sin positive, so sin150°=sin30°=1/2.
210°=180°+30° lies in quadrant III: cos negative, so cos210°=−cos30°=−√3/2.
330°=360°−30° lies in quadrant IV: tan negative, so tan330°=−tan30°=−1/√3.
Final answer: 1/2, −√3/2 and −1/√3.

Solved Example 2.4: Exact compound angle

Question. Find cos 75° exactly.

Rule. 75°=45°+30° and cos(A+B)=cosAcosB−sinAsinB.
cos75°=cos45°cos30°−sin45°sin30°.
= (1/√2)(√3/2) − (1/√2)(1/2).
= (√3−1)/(2√2) = (√6−√2)/4.
Final answer: cos75°=(√6−√2)/4.

Solved Example 2.5: Tangent difference

Question. Evaluate tan 15° exactly.

Rule. 15°=45°−30°.
tan15°=(tan45°−tan30°)/(1+tan45°tan30°).
=(1−1/√3)/(1+1/√3).
Multiply numerator and denominator by √3: (√3−1)/(√3+1).
Rationalise to obtain 2−√3.
Final answer: tan15°=2−√3.

Module III

Coordinate Geometry

Use coordinates to describe straight lines, their relative orientation, and circles.

11 Lecture Hours4 Tutorial HoursCO3
3.1 Slope and equation of a straight line

The slope measures vertical change per unit horizontal change. Through two points P(x₁,y₁) and Q(x₂,y₂):

m=(y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁), x₂≠x₁
m=tan θ, where θ is the inclination from the positive x-axis.
Point-slope form: y−y₁=m(x−x₁).
For a vertical line, x₂−x₁=0, so the slope is not defined. Do not write m=0.
3.2 Angle, parallel and perpendicular lines
Angle between lines of slopes m₁ and m₂:
tan θ = |(m₂−m₁)/(1+m₁m₂)|.

Parallel lines: m₁=m₂. Perpendicular lines: m₁m₂=−1, provided both slopes are finite.

Convert the given line to a form from which its slope can be read.
Use the same slope for a parallel line or the negative reciprocal for a perpendicular line.
Substitute the given point in y−y₁=m(x−x₁).
Simplify to the required form.
Engineering mini-application: line equations describe rails, cable routes, ramps and edges in a coordinate drawing. Parallel and perpendicular conditions help maintain alignment.
3.3 Equation of a circle

A circle is the set of points at a fixed distance r from centre C(h,k).

(x−h)²+(y−k)²=r².

After expansion the general form is:

x²+y²+2gx+2fy+c=0,
centre=(−g,−f), radius=√(g²+f²−c).
In the general equation, compare 2g and 2f carefully. For example, coefficient −6x means 2g=−6, hence g=−3 and centre x-coordinate is −g=3.

Worked examples

Solved Example 3.1: Slope and point-slope form

Question. Find the equation of the line through (2,3) and (6,11).

Rule. m=(y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁); y−y₁=m(x−x₁).
m=(11−3)/(6−2)=8/4=2.
Using (2,3): y−3=2(x−2).
Simplify: y−3=2x−4, so y=2x−1.
Final answer: y=2x−1.

Check: At x=6, y=11.

Solved Example 3.2: Angle between lines

Question. Find the acute angle between lines with slopes 1 and −1/3.

Rule. tanθ=|(m₂−m₁)/(1+m₁m₂)|.
tanθ=|((−1/3)−1)/(1+1(−1/3))|.
=|−4/3 ÷ 2/3|=2.
Therefore θ=tan⁻¹2, approximately 63.435°.
Final answer: θ=tan⁻¹2≈63.435°.

Solved Example 3.3: Parallel and perpendicular lines

Question. Through (1,−2), find lines parallel and perpendicular to 3x−2y+5=0.

Rule. Write the given line as y=(3/2)x+5/2, so m=3/2.
Parallel: y+2=(3/2)(x−1). Multiply by 2: 2y+4=3x−3.
Parallel line: 3x−2y−7=0.
Perpendicular slope = −2/3.
Perpendicular: y+2=(−2/3)(x−1). Multiply by 3 and simplify.
Final answer: Parallel: 3x−2y−7=0; perpendicular: 2x+3y+4=0.

Solved Example 3.4: Circle from centre and radius

Question. Form the equation of the circle with centre (3,−2) and radius 5.

Rule. (x−h)²+(y−k)²=r².
Substitute h=3, k=−2, r=5.
(x−3)²+(y+2)²=25.
Expanded form: x²+y²−6x+4y−12=0.
Final answer: (x−3)²+(y+2)²=25.

Solved Example 3.5: Centre and radius from general form

Question. Find the centre and radius of x²+y²−6x+8y−11=0.

Rule. Compare with x²+y²+2gx+2fy+c=0.
2g=−6, so g=−3. 2f=8, so f=4. c=−11.
Centre=(−g,−f)=(3,−4).
r=√(g²+f²−c)=√(9+16+11)=√36=6.
Final answer: Centre (3,−4), radius 6.

Check: Substitution gives the standard form (x−3)²+(y+4)²=36.

Module IV

Differential Calculus

4.1 Concept of a limit

A limit describes the value approached by a function as x approaches a specified number. The function need not be evaluated exactly at that point.

x എന്ന value-ൽ എത്തേണ്ടതില്ല; അതിനോട് അടുത്തപ്പോൾ function ഏത് value-ലേക്ക് പോകുന്നു എന്നതാണ് limit.
4.2 Limits by substitution and factorisation

Use direct substitution first. If it produces a finite value, stop. If it produces 0/0, simplify by factorisation and then substitute.

Substitute the limiting value.
If the form is 0/0, factorise numerator and denominator.
Cancel only a common factor, not separate terms.
Substitute again after simplification.
limx→a (xⁿ−aⁿ)/(x−a) = naⁿ⁻¹.
limx→0 sin x/x = 1, where x is measured in radians.
Do not cancel across addition or subtraction. For example, x cannot be cancelled from (x+2)/x.

4.4 Linearity, product, quotient and second derivatives
Linearity: d[af(x)+bg(x)]/dx = af'(x)+bg'(x).
Product rule: d(uv)/dx = u(dv/dx)+v(du/dx).
Quotient rule: d(u/v)/dx = [v(du/dx)−u(dv/dx)]/v².
Second derivative: d²y/dx² = d/dx(dy/dx).

Worked examples

Solved Example 4.1: Direct substitution and factorisation

Question. Evaluate (a) lim x→2 (x²+3x−1), (b) lim x→3 (x²−9)/(x−3).

Rule. Use substitution; for 0/0 factorise.
(a) Substitute x=2: 4+6−1=9.
(b) Direct substitution gives 0/0.
x²−9=(x−3)(x+3); cancel x−3 for x≠3.
Now substitute x=3 in x+3.
Final answer: (a) 9, (b) 6.

Solved Example 4.2: Limit of xⁿ−aⁿ form

Question. Evaluate lim x→2 (x⁵−32)/(x−2).

Rule. lim(xⁿ−aⁿ)/(x−a)=naⁿ⁻¹.
Here n=5 and a=2.
Limit = 5·2⁴ = 5·16.
Final answer: 80.

Solved Example 4.3: Standard trigonometric limit

Question. Evaluate lim x→0 sin 4x/x.

Rule. Create the standard form sin u/u.
Write sin4x/x = 4[sin4x/(4x)].
As x→0, 4x→0 and sin4x/(4x)→1.
Therefore the limit is 4·1.
Final answer: 4.

Check: The angle is treated in radians.

Solved Example 4.4: Linearity and standard results

Question. Differentiate y=4x⁵−3x²+2eˣ−5log x+7.

Rule. Differentiate each term separately.
d(4x⁵)/dx=20x⁴; d(−3x²)/dx=−6x.
d(2eˣ)/dx=2eˣ; d(−5log x)/dx=−5/x.
d(7)/dx=0.
Final answer: dy/dx=20x⁴−6x+2eˣ−5/x.

Solved Example 4.5: Product and quotient rules

Question. Differentiate (a) y=x²sin x, (b) y=(x²+1)/x.

Rule. Product: u'v+uv'. Quotient: (vu'−uv')/v².
(a) u=x², v=sinx. y'=2xsinx+x²cosx.
(b) u=x²+1, v=x.
y'=[x(2x)−(x²+1)(1)]/x².
Simplify: y'=(x²−1)/x².
Final answer: (a) 2x sinx+x² cosx; (b) (x²−1)/x².

Solved Example 4.6: Second derivative

Question. If y=3x⁴−2x³+5x−7, find d²y/dx².

Rule. Differentiate twice.
First derivative: dy/dx=12x³−6x²+5.
Differentiate again: d²y/dx²=36x²−12x.
Final answer: d²y/dx²=36x²−12x.

Method plan for the eight seven-mark questions

1. Three-variable Cramer

Write Δ and all three replacement determinants clearly. A full answer must include x, y and z and a quick substitution check.

2. Matrix and two-variable system

Show determinant, four minors, signed cofactors, adjoint and inverse. State the inverse condition before solving the equations.

3. Ratios in quadrants

For each angle, write its reference angle and quadrant. Award the sign using ASTC before inserting the standard value.

4. Compound angles

Choose a useful decomposition such as 75°=45°+30° or 15°=45°−30°. Write the complete formula before substitution.

5. Line relationships

Read the given slope, obtain the parallel or negative-reciprocal slope, use point-slope form, and simplify. Use the angle formula separately.

6. Circle conversion

Start with (x−h)²+(y−k)²=r². After expansion, compare 2g, 2f and c and recover the same centre and radius.

7. Mixed limits

Classify each limit: direct substitution, factorisation, xⁿ−aⁿ form or sinx/x form. Do not use a method that is not required.

8. Mixed derivatives

Mark u and v before applying product or quotient rule. Simplify the first derivative and differentiate again only when the second derivative is requested.

Four engineering-oriented application checks

Simultaneous electrical quantities

Two measured relations are 2I₁+I₂=11 and I₁−I₂=1. Cramer’s rule gives Δ=−3, Δ₁=−12, Δ₂=−9, hence I₁=4 and I₂=3. Substitute both values to verify the readings.

Inclined component check

A 100 N force acts at 30° to the horizontal. The horizontal component is 100cos30°=50√3 N and the vertical component is 100sin30°=50 N. This uses only the basic ratios of an acute angle.

Coordinate route check

A cable route through (1,2) and (5,10) has slope 2 and equation y−2=2(x−1), or y=2x. A second route with slope −1/2 through the same point is perpendicular.

Rate-of-change check

If a displacement model is s=2t⁴−3t²+5t, then velocity is ds/dt=8t³−6t+5 and acceleration is d²s/dt²=24t²−6. The operation is standard first and second differentiation.

Verification habit: matrix answers can be checked by substitution, trigonometric answers by quadrant signs and approximate values, line/circle answers by point substitution, and derivatives by differentiating each term independently.
