Course introduction

Applied Electronics develops the semiconductor-device and circuit skills used to analyse and build rectifiers, transistor/FET amplifiers, feedback circuits and oscillators. The course connects device characteristics to biasing, stability, frequency response and practical testing.

Basic circuit theory, semiconductor fundamentals, Ohm’s law and safe low-voltage laboratory practice.

Doping produces N-type and P-type semiconductor material. A PN junction forms a depletion region and barrier potential; forward and reverse bias alter carrier flow and produce the diode characteristic.

Study and application method

Exam practice

Module-wise Questions with Answers

Module 1 — Semiconductor Devices and Diode Applications

Explain Semiconductor and PN-junction behaviour and state one correct method, application or precaution.

Doping produces N-type and P-type semiconductor material. A PN junction forms a depletion region and barrier potential; forward and reverse bias alter carrier flow and produce the diode characteristic.

Answer framework: Draw energy/carrier or junction-bias diagram, label polarity/current direction and relate operating point to the I–V characteristic.

Important point: A silicon diode is not an ideal switch; threshold, resistance, current limit and temperature matter.

Explain Rectifiers, filters and regulation and state one correct method, application or precaution.

Rectifiers convert AC to pulsating DC; filters reduce ripple; a Zener-based circuit can regulate a limited output range when current and power ratings are respected.

Answer framework: Trace AC input through rectifier/filter/load, identify ripple path and calculate or estimate required rating before construction.

Important point: Capacitors and diodes have voltage/current/polarity limits—observe ratings and discharge capacitors safely.

Explain Special-purpose diodes and state one correct method, application or precaution.

Zener, photodiode, varactor, tunnel and SCR-related devices use specific junction properties for regulation, sensing, tuning or controlled switching.

Answer framework: State device symbol, controlled quantity, normal bias mode and one application; compare it with an ordinary rectifier diode.

Important point: Do not substitute devices simply because symbols look similar; breakdown/trigger and rating behaviour differ.

Module 2 — BJT and FET Amplifier Foundations

Explain BJT configurations and amplifier action and state one correct method, application or precaution.

BJT common-base, common-emitter and common-collector configurations offer different gain, impedance and phase properties. Small input changes control larger output variations when correctly biased.

Answer framework: Identify terminals/configuration, draw DC and AC paths, set the operating point and compare gain/input/output impedance qualitatively.

Important point: A transistor amplifier cannot be designed from gain alone; bias point and load determine distortion and usable swing.

Explain Biasing, load lines and thermal stability and state one correct method, application or precaution.

Bias networks establish a quiescent operating point. DC/AC load lines visualise allowed voltage/current swing; feedback/compensation techniques reduce sensitivity to device and temperature variation.

Answer framework: Draw the output characteristic with load line, choose a stable Q-point, then check cutoff/saturation margins and component power ratings.

Important point: Thermal runaway is a real device-risk mechanism; do not omit stabilisation or heat-management considerations.

Explain FET and MOSFET circuits and state one correct method, application or precaution.

JFET and MOSFET devices use electric-field control and have high input impedance. Depletion/enhancement behaviour and biasing differ from BJT practice.

Answer framework: Identify device type/symbol, state control variable and operating region, then explain source/common-drain or source-follower use with a labelled circuit.

Important point: MOSFET gates can be damaged by static electricity; use correct handling and protection.

Module 3 — Frequency Response and Feedback

Explain Frequency response of amplifiers and state one correct method, application or precaution.

Amplifier gain varies with frequency because coupling/bypass capacitors and device capacitances affect low/high-frequency behaviour. Bandwidth describes the useful frequency range.

Answer framework: Plot/interpret gain versus frequency on suitable axes, identify lower/upper cutoff causes and relate component change to expected response shift.

Important point: Do not quote mid-band gain as valid across the entire frequency range.

Explain Multistage coupling and state one correct method, application or precaution.

Direct, RC, transformer and LC coupling methods connect stages with different impedance, bandwidth and application implications. Overall gain and bandwidth depend on the combined stages.

Answer framework: Compare coupling method using DC isolation, impedance matching, frequency response and practical application criteria.

Important point: Cascade stages carefully; loading from the next stage can reduce expected gain.

Explain Feedback effects and state one correct method, application or precaution.

Negative feedback can improve stability, bandwidth and distortion performance at the cost of gain; positive feedback supports oscillator action when phase/gain conditions are satisfied.

Answer framework: Draw forward and feedback paths, identify sign of feedback and state effect on gain, impedance, noise or bandwidth.

Important point: Feedback sign depends on circuit connection and phase—not on a label alone.

Module 4 — Oscillators and Waveform Generation

Explain Oscillation condition and state one correct method, application or precaution.

An oscillator generates a periodic signal without an external periodic input by using positive feedback and a frequency-selective network. Sustained oscillation is described by loop-gain and phase conditions.

Answer framework: Identify active device, feedback network and frequency-setting elements; write the condition conceptually before selecting component values.

Important point: A circuit may start but distort or stop if amplitude stabilisation and biasing are ignored.

Explain LC, RC and crystal oscillators and state one correct method, application or precaution.

LC oscillators suit higher-frequency sine generation; RC phase-shift/Wien circuits suit lower/audio ranges; crystal oscillators provide high frequency stability through piezoelectric resonance.

Answer framework: Compare frequency-setting element, typical range, stability and application; draw the feedback path and label reactive components.

Important point: Do not expect one oscillator topology to deliver every frequency, waveform or stability requirement.

Explain UJT relaxation oscillator and state one correct method, application or precaution.

A UJT relaxation oscillator charges a capacitor until a triggering level causes rapid discharge, producing non-sinusoidal timing waveforms useful in triggering/timing contexts.

Answer framework: Trace capacitor charge/discharge phases, mark threshold event and relate component values to timing qualitatively.

Important point: Observe supply and device ratings; pulse circuits can produce unexpected peak currents.
