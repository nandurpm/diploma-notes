Module 1: History and Classification

Duration: 10 Hours | CO1
1.1 History and Development

The automobile has evolved from steam-powered "road locomotives" to modern electric and autonomous vehicles. Key milestones include:

  • Abroad: Nicholas Cugnot (1769 steam carriage), Karl Benz (1886 - first practical petrol car), Henry Ford (Mass production via Model T).
  • India: Pre-independence saw imports. Post-independence (1947) led to the establishment of Hindustan Motors (Ambassador) and Premier Automobiles (Padmini). The 1980s revolution started with Maruti Suzuki.

1.2 Classification of Vehicles

Automobiles are classified based on several criteria:

  1. Purpose: Passenger (Car, Bus), Goods (Truck, Lorry).
  2. Capacity: LMV (Light Motor Vehicle), HTV (Heavy Transport Vehicle).
  3. Fuel: Petrol, Diesel, CNG, Electric, Hybrid.
  4. Axles: Two-wheeler, Three-wheeler, Four-wheeler, Multi-axle.
  5. Drive: Left-hand drive, Right-hand drive.
1.3 Chassis Terminology & Layouts

The Chassis is the skeleton of the vehicle, carrying all major systems like the engine, transmission, and suspension.

Module 2: Engine Terminology & Working

Duration: 11 Hours | CO2
2.1 Heat Engines: IC vs EC

A Heat Engine converts thermal energy into mechanical work.

  • External Combustion (EC): Fuel burns outside the cylinder (e.g., Steam Engine).
  • Internal Combustion (IC): Fuel burns inside the cylinder (e.g., Petrol/Diesel Engine).
2.2 Engine Terminology
  • Bore: Inner diameter of the cylinder.
  • Stroke: Distance traveled by the piston from TDC to BDC.
  • TDC (Top Dead Center): Highest point the piston reaches.
  • BDC (Bottom Dead Center): Lowest point the piston reaches.
  • Swept Volume ($V_s$): Volume displaced by the piston in one stroke.
  • Clearance Volume ($V_c$): Volume above the piston at TDC.
2.3 4-Stroke vs 2-Stroke Engines

In a 4-stroke engine, one power stroke occurs every two revolutions of the crankshaft (Suction, Compression, Power, Exhaust). In a 2-stroke engine, one power stroke occurs every single revolution.

Formula Bank & Calculations

Compression Ratio (CR) = (Vs + Vc) / Vc
Example Calculation:
Given: Swept Volume (Vs) = 450cc, Clearance Volume (Vc) = 50cc.
Calculation: CR = (450 + 50) / 50 = 500 / 50 = 10.
Answer: The Compression Ratio is 10:1.

Module 3: Engine Construction Details - I

Duration: 11 Hours | CO3
3.1 Cylinder Block and Head

Cylinder Block: The main body of the engine. Usually made of Cast Iron or Aluminum Alloy. It contains the cylinders and water jackets for cooling.

Cylinder Head: Closes the top of the cylinder. Houses valves, spark plugs (SI), or injectors (CI).

3.2 Cylinder Liners

Liners are sleeves inserted into the block to provide a wear-resistant surface.

  • Dry Liner: Not in direct contact with coolant. Easy to replace but poor heat transfer.
  • Wet Liner: Direct contact with coolant. Better cooling but requires sealing to prevent leaks.
3.3 Piston and Rings

The piston transmits the force of combustion to the connecting rod. Made of Aluminum alloy for lightness.

Piston Rings: 1. Compression Rings (Seal the combustion chamber). 2. Oil Control Rings (Scrape excess oil from cylinder walls).

Module 4: Engine Construction Details - II

Duration: 11 Hours | CO4
4.1 Firing Order

The sequence in which power strokes occur in different cylinders. It ensures engine balance and smooth torque.

  • 3-Cylinder: 1-2-3 or 1-3-2
  • 4-Cylinder: 1-3-4-2 or 1-2-4-3
  • 6-Cylinder: 1-5-3-6-2-4
4.2 Valve Operating Mechanism

Valves control the flow of air/fuel and exhaust. Most modern engines use Poppet Valves.

  • SOHC (Single Overhead Camshaft): One cam operates both intake and exhaust valves.
  • DOHC (Double Overhead Camshaft): Separate cams for intake and exhaust, allowing better performance at high RPM.

Q3: Explain the function of Piston Rings. (3 Marks)
Piston rings serve three main purposes: sealing the combustion chamber to prevent gas leakage, transferring heat from the piston to the cylinder wall, and controlling the oil film on the cylinder wall.

Quick Revision

  • Chassis: The backbone of the vehicle.
  • IC Engine: Combustion happens inside (Petrol/Diesel).
  • CR: Ratio of total volume to clearance volume.
  • Liners: Dry (no water contact) vs Wet (water contact).
  • DOHC: Better for high-speed performance.

Glossary

Bore
Diameter of the engine cylinder.
Stroke
Distance moved by piston between TDC and BDC.
Payload
The weight of passengers and cargo a vehicle can carry.
Gasket
A seal used between two surfaces (like block and head) to prevent leaks.
