Objectives, Course Outcomes and CO–PO Information

Official course objectives

  1. Gain a comprehensive understanding of catering establishments within the travel and tourism industry, including their classification and career opportunities, develop proficiency in the organization and staffing of food and beverage service departments, acquire practical knowledge of various service areas and equipment, demonstrate competence in menu planning and the sequence of courses for different types of meals, and develop skills in planning and preparing both Western and Indian menus, including proper table setting and service techniques.

Course Outcomes

  1. Gain a comprehensive understanding of the role of catering establishments in the travel and tourism industry, including commercial and welfare classifications and career opportunities.
  2. Develop proficiency in departmental organization and staffing within food and beverage service departments, including staff responsibilities, waiter attributes, and interdepartmental relationships.
  3. Acquire practical knowledge of service areas, ancillary departments, and selection, classification, care, and maintenance of service equipment.
  4. Demonstrate competence in menu planning, types of meals, menu terminology, and sequence of courses in French classical, table d’hôtel, and à la carte menus, Indian and Continental.
  5. Develop skills in planning Western and Indian menus, covers and accompaniments, mise-en-scene, mise-en-place, table laying, tablecloth folding, and serviette arrangement.

Official module 1

Catering Establishments and F&B Department Staffing

Food and beverage service is the guest-facing part of hospitality that converts prepared food and beverages into a planned service experience. Understanding the establishment, department, staff, and relationships is the foundation for later practical work.

Hours: 12
CO1 · Bloom level: Understanding

Learning goals

  • Classify commercial and welfare catering establishments.
  • Relate F&B service to travel and tourism.
  • Explain staff duties, waiter attributes, and departmental relationships.

Module study tip

Read every topic in sequence, reproduce its example or procedure, and write a short explanation before attempting the module questions.

M1.01 — Introduction to hotel industry, F&B service and catering establishments (3 hours)

Concept and explanation

The hotel industry provides accommodation and related services; food and beverage operations may serve residents, visitors, staff, events, and travellers. F&B service includes receiving the guest, presenting the menu, taking the order, coordinating with production, serving correctly, clearing, billing, and closing the service. Catering establishments are shaped by purpose, clientele, location, scale, and service style.

Malayalam support: Hotel industry-യിൽ accommodation കൂടാതെ food and beverage service പ്രധാന revenue/service area ആണ്. Guest-നെ welcome ചെയ്യുന്നതിൽ നിന്ന് billing വരെ ഒരു coordinated process ആണ്.

Study points

  • Distinguish service from production; identify guest expectations; link service style to market and outlet type.
Engineering / workplace application: Hotels, restaurants, banquets, cafés, transport catering, and institutional dining all require organised service.
Illustrative example: A banquet service plans for a fixed group and menu, while an à la carte restaurant handles individual orders.
Common mistake: Calling every F&B outlet a restaurant ignores differences in guest need, service speed, and staffing.
M1.02 — Classification of catering establishments and career opportunities (3 hours)

Concept and explanation

The syllabus classifies catering establishments as commercial—residential and non-residential—and welfare—industrial, institutional, and transport. Commercial outlets operate primarily as a business; welfare outlets provide food as part of an organisation or service. Career opportunities include waiter, captain, supervisor, restaurant manager, banquet staff, room-service staff, and specialised beverage roles.

Malayalam support: Commercial outlet profit ലക്ഷ്യത്തോടെ പ്രവർത്തിക്കും; welfare outlet institution/transport/industry ആവശ്യത്തിനായി food നൽകും. ഓരോ മേഖലയിലും career roles വ്യത്യസ്തമാണ്.

Study points

  • Use the classification exactly; compare clientele, pricing, service pattern, and staffing; relate training to career progression.
Engineering / workplace application: Airline catering, hospital food service, factory canteens, hotels, and independent restaurants illustrate the categories.
Illustrative example: A hotel restaurant is commercial and residential in context; an employee canteen is typically welfare and industrial.
Common mistake: Confusing an institutional dining facility with a commercial restaurant leads to wrong assumptions about menu and service.
M1.03 — Departmental organisation, staffing, duties and waiter attributes (3 hours)

Concept and explanation

An F&B department commonly has a manager or head, supervisors, captains, waiters, commis, sommeliers or beverage specialists, and support staff depending on the operation. A good waiter needs grooming, courtesy, observation, communication, product knowledge, memory, punctuality, teamwork, and safe handling. Duties should be assigned through a clear hierarchy.

Malayalam support: Good waiter-ന് courtesy, grooming, communication, observation, speed, product knowledge എന്നിവ ആവശ്യമാണ്. Hierarchy വഴി responsibility വ്യക്തമാകും.

Study points

  • Separate authority from responsibility; identify reporting lines; explain how behaviour affects guest confidence; learn French and American staff terms without replacing practical understanding.
Engineering / workplace application: Staff organisation controls service quality during breakfast, banquet, and peak restaurant hours.
Illustrative example: Before opening, a waiter checks sideboard, table settings, reservations, menus, and station readiness.
Common mistake: Speed without accuracy or courtesy is not good service.
M1.04 — Relationships within F&B and with other departments (3 hours)

Concept and explanation

F&B service depends on kitchen, front office, housekeeping, maintenance, stores, accounts, security, and personnel. A service request must move through the right channel: front office may communicate room-service needs, stores supplies equipment, kitchen produces food, accounts handles billing, and maintenance resolves faults. Communication must be timely and documented.

Malayalam support: F&B department ഒറ്റയ്ക്ക് പ്രവർത്തിക്കില്ല. Kitchen, front office, stores, accounts, maintenance എന്നിവയുമായി coordination ആവശ്യമാണ്.

Study points

  • Map a guest request across departments; use standard communication; escalate safety or quality issues immediately.
Engineering / workplace application: A room-service order links guest information, kitchen timing, service delivery, and billing.
Illustrative example: A broken refrigerator requires a maintenance report and safe food-control action, not only a verbal complaint.
Common mistake: Blaming another department without passing complete information delays service and harms the guest experience.
Module summary: Good service begins with the right establishment, role, skill, and interdepartmental communication.
Official module 2

Service Areas and Ancillary Departments

Service areas determine where guests are served and where equipment, food, linen, and staff move. Ancillary departments support the visible service and must be planned for hygiene, speed, and control.

Hours: 12
CO2 · Bloom level: Understanding

Learning goals

  • Describe the listed F&B outlets and their service characteristics.
  • Explain pantry, food pickup, stores, linen room, plate room, wash-up, and kitchen stewarding.
  • Relate layout and support areas to efficient service.

Module study tip

Read every topic in sequence, reproduce its example or procedure, and write a short explanation before attempting the module questions.

M2.01 — Food and beverage outlets (6 hours)

Concept and explanation

The syllabus lists specialty restaurant, coffee shop, banquets/functions, room service, cafeteria, grill room, discotheques, night clubs, bar, outdoor catering, garden café, and poolside service. Each outlet differs in menu, atmosphere, operating hours, equipment, staffing, and guest expectation. A specialty restaurant emphasises a theme; a coffee shop emphasises quick accessible service; banquets serve planned functions.

Malayalam support: ഓരോ outlet-നും menu, atmosphere, timing, staffing, equipment എന്നിവ വ്യത്യസ്തമാണ്. Service design guest expectation-നോട് match ചെയ്യണം.

Study points

  • Compare outlet purpose and service speed; identify fixed versus mobile service; note safety and guest-flow requirements.
Engineering / workplace application: A hotel may use a coffee shop for all-day service and a specialty restaurant for a themed dining experience.
Illustrative example: A banquet mise-en-place is completed before guests arrive; an à la carte station must remain flexible for orders.
Common mistake: Applying the same service sequence to a buffet, banquet, and à la carte outlet creates delays.
M2.02 — Ancillary departments and outdoor catering support (6 hours)

Concept and explanation

Ancillary areas include pantry, food pickup area, stores, linen room, plate room, wash-up, and kitchen stewarding. They receive, hold, issue, clean, and control resources. Outdoor catering adds transport, temporary setup, food safety, service logistics, and recovery of equipment. The syllabus repeats outdoor catering and garden café/poolside under this section; both are retained as part of the coverage.

Malayalam support: Visible service smooth ആകാൻ pantry, stores, wash-up, linen room തുടങ്ങിയ support areas ശരിയായി പ്രവർത്തിക്കണം. Outdoor catering-ൽ transportയും food safetyയും അധികം പ്രധാനമാണ്.

Study points

  • Trace equipment from store to service and back; separate clean and soiled flow; plan outdoor power, water, shade, transport, and waste control.
Engineering / workplace application: Weddings, poolside buffets, and garden events rely on temporary service areas and controlled equipment movement.
Illustrative example: For an outdoor event, prepare a checklist for food containers, service equipment, linen, waste bins, water, and emergency contacts.
Common mistake: Sending clean plates through a soiled-wash route creates hygiene risk.
Module summary: Service areas are a coordinated system of guest-facing outlets and controlled support spaces.
Official module 3

F&B Service Equipment

Equipment influences speed, presentation, hygiene, and cost. Selection must balance function, durability, safety, style, maintenance, and the type of service offered.

Hours: 12
CO3 · Bloom level: Understanding

Learning goals

  • Classify cutlery, crockery, glassware, flatware, hollowware, and other equipment.
  • State selection factors and French terms where relevant.
  • Explain care, maintenance, and silver cleaning.

Module study tip

Read every topic in sequence, reproduce its example or procedure, and write a short explanation before attempting the module questions.

M3.01 — Familiarisation and selection of service equipment (6 hours)

Concept and explanation

Cutlery includes knives, forks, spoons, and special items; crockery includes plates, cups, saucers, and bowls; glassware is selected according to beverage and service; flatware and hollowware support table service and presentation. Selection factors include suitability, design, size, weight, durability, ease of cleaning, replacement, cost, storage, safety, and compatibility with the menu.

Malayalam support: Equipment തിരഞ്ഞെടുക്കുമ്പോൾ appearance മാത്രം നോക്കരുത്; function, durability, cleaning, safety, storage, cost എന്നിവയും നോക്കണം.

Study points

  • Identify each item by name and use; match equipment to menu; handle glass and sharp items safely; maintain par stock.
Engineering / workplace application: A soup service needs suitable bowl, spoon, underliner, and service equipment; beverage glass shape affects presentation and portion control.
Illustrative example: Before service, count required items, inspect for chips or stains, polish, and arrange them at the station.
Common mistake: Using an attractive but unsuitable glass or oversized plate reduces service quality.
M3.02 — Care and maintenance, including silver cleaning (3 hours)

Concept and explanation

Care includes correct handling, washing, drying, polishing, inspection, and storage. Silver or silver-plated articles require a suitable cleaning method, soft materials, and careful rinsing to avoid scratches or residue. Cleaning should follow manufacturer or institutional procedure and use PPE where chemicals are involved.

Malayalam support: Equipment care-ൽ handling, washing, drying, polishing, inspection, storage എല്ലാം ഉൾപ്പെടും. Silver item-ുകൾ scratch വരാതെ clean ചെയ്യണം.

Study points

  • Separate metals; follow product instructions; keep chemicals labelled; dry fully; report damage; store clean items to prevent recontamination.
Engineering / workplace application: Regular maintenance extends the service life of costly crockery, glassware, cutlery, and hollowware.
Illustrative example: After polishing, inspect an EPNS item under good light, remove residue, and store it away from moisture.
Common mistake: Abrasive cleaning on plated items can remove the finish.
M3.03 — Classification of food and beverage equipment (3 hours)

Concept and explanation

Equipment can be classified by material, function, location, portability, or service stage. A useful working classification is table equipment, service equipment, preparation/support equipment, cleaning equipment, and storage equipment. Classification helps inventory, issue control, and training.

Malayalam support: Equipment-നെ function/material/location അനുസരിച്ച് classify ചെയ്യാം. Classification inventory control എളുപ്പമാക്കും.

Study points

  • Use one consistent classification; label storage; distinguish guest-use items from back-of-house equipment; record losses.
Engineering / workplace application: A department can identify missing items faster when the stock list is grouped by crockery, glassware, cutlery, linen, and service tools.
Illustrative example: A par-stock sheet may list dinner plates, soup bowls, wine glasses, service spoons, and linen under separate headings.
Common mistake: Mixing consumables, durable equipment, and chemicals in one stock list causes control problems.
Module summary: Equipment knowledge connects guest presentation with hygiene, safety, cost, and workflow.
Official module 4

Meals and Menu Planning

A menu is a communication and planning document. It tells the guest what is offered and guides kitchen production, service equipment, staffing, purchasing, and timing.

Hours: 12
CO4 · Bloom level: Understanding

Learning goals

  • Classify meals and plan Western and Indian menus.
  • Explain covers, accompaniments, menu terminology, and menu objectives.
  • Describe table d’hôtel, à la carte, and French classical course sequence.

Module study tip

Read every topic in sequence, reproduce its example or procedure, and write a short explanation before attempting the module questions.

M4.01 — Types of meals and breakfast menus (3 hours)

Concept and explanation

The syllabus lists breakfast, lunch, dinner, supper, brunch, high tea, and afternoon tea. A menu should reflect time of day, guest expectation, nutrition, service style, local preference, cost, and available production. Breakfast may be English, American, Continental, or Indian, each with a different pattern of dishes and accompaniments.

Malayalam support: Meal type സമയം, menu pattern, service speed, guest expectation എന്നിവ നിശ്ചയിക്കുന്നു. English, American, Continental, Indian breakfast-ുകൾക്ക് വ്യത്യസ്ത accompaniments ഉണ്ട്.

Study points

  • Compare meal timing and course structure; identify covers and accompaniments; plan a balanced menu within resources.
Engineering / workplace application: Hotels use breakfast type and meal period to forecast production and staffing.
Illustrative example: A Continental breakfast is generally lighter and faster to serve than a full English breakfast; the exact menu should follow the establishment.
Common mistake: Copying a breakfast menu without considering service time and kitchen capacity causes delay.
M4.02 — Menu terminology and menu types (3 hours)

Concept and explanation

The syllabus includes basic menu types, table d’hôtel (Indian and Continental), and à la carte (Indian, Continental, and Chinese). Table d’hôtel offers a fixed sequence or selection at a set price; à la carte lists individually priced dishes prepared to order. Menu terms should be used consistently so the guest and kitchen understand the offer.

Malayalam support: Table d’hôtel fixed/limited choice set-price menu ആയിരിക്കും; à la carte-ൽ ഓരോ dish-നും individual priceയും order preparationയും ഉണ്ടാകും.

Study points

  • Compare price, choice, preparation, service time, and control; identify Indian, Continental, and Chinese menu contexts.
Engineering / workplace application: Menu design supports forecasting, purchasing, and guest decision-making.
Illustrative example: A banquet menu may be table d’hôtel; a restaurant menu with separately priced dishes is à la carte.
Common mistake: Calling a fixed menu à la carte creates incorrect expectations about choice and price.
M4.03 — Origin and objectives of menu planning (3 hours)

Concept and explanation

Menu planning begins with the purpose of the meal and the guest group. Objectives include satisfaction, nutrition, variety, balance, cost control, use of seasonal supplies, kitchen capability, service equipment, and profitability. The origin of the menu may be the chef, hotel policy, event requirement, cultural tradition, or guest profile.

Malayalam support: Menu planning-ന്റെ ലക്ഷ്യം guest satisfaction മാത്രം അല്ല; nutrition, cost, variety, season, kitchen capacity എന്നിവയും ഉൾപ്പെടും.

Study points

  • State target guest and occasion; balance colour, texture, flavour, and method; check cost and equipment; avoid repetition.
Engineering / workplace application: A hotel adapts menus for conference guests, children, local festivals, and international travellers.
Illustrative example: A balanced menu avoids repeating fried, creamy, or starch-heavy items in every course.
Common mistake: Choosing several dishes with the same colour, texture, or cooking method produces a monotonous menu.
M4.04 — Sequence of courses in the French classical menu (3 hours)

Concept and explanation

A classical sequence moves from lighter or stimulating items toward substantial courses and finally dessert, with variations by menu and service. Students should learn the order and purpose of the courses as a framework, while recognising that modern menus may simplify it. The sequence guides cutlery, crockery, timing, and clearing.

Malayalam support: French classical menu sequence മനസ്സിലാക്കിയാൽ table setting, service order, clearing order എന്നിവ ശരിയായി plan ചെയ്യാം.

Study points

  • Learn the official course names from class notes; connect each course to equipment and accompaniment; avoid presenting a heavy course before an appropriate starter.
Engineering / workplace application: Formal banquets use course sequencing to coordinate kitchen and service timing.
Illustrative example: The server checks the planned sequence so the correct plate and cutlery are ready before each course.
Common mistake: Using every classical course in a modern short meal is unnecessary; sequence should suit the menu.
Module summary: Menu planning is a coordination tool linking guest needs, food production, equipment, cost, and service.

Western and Indian Menus, Mise-en-Place and Table Laying

Hours: 12
CO5 · Bloom level: Understanding

  • Explain mise-en-scene and mise-en-place, including aide-board arrangement.
  • Lay tables, fold tablecloths, and arrange serviettes for different meals.

Module study tip

Read every topic in sequence, reproduce its example or procedure, and write a short explanation before attempting the module questions.

M4.01 / CO5 — Mise-en-scene, mise-en-place and laying tables (12 hours)

Concept and explanation

Mise-en-scene is the overall environment and setting presented to the guest; mise-en-place is the preparation and placement of everything required before service. Table laying depends on meal, menu, cover, service style, crockery, cutlery, glassware, linen, condiments, and accompaniments. The aide-board is stocked to support the station without clutter.

Malayalam support: Mise-en-place എന്നത് service തുടങ്ങുന്നതിന് മുമ്പുള്ള preparation ആണ്; mise-en-scene guest കാണുന്ന overall environment ആണ്. Table layout meal/menu അനുസരിച്ച് മാറും.

Study points

  • Prepare a cover plan; align tablecloth and place settings; keep service distances consistent; fold linen neatly; stock aide-board; inspect before guests arrive.

Suggested procedure

  1. List menu and number of covers.
  2. Select linen, crockery, cutlery, glassware, and accompaniments.
  3. Clean and position the table and cloth.
  4. Lay the cover symmetrically and check spacing.
  5. Fold and place serviette according to the meal.
  6. Stock the aide-board and inspect the complete mise-en-scene.
Engineering / workplace application: Proper table laying reduces unnecessary movement, improves appearance, and supports hygienic service.
Illustrative example: For a three-course meal, place the outer cutlery for the first course and arrange glassware consistently above the knife area; exact cover follows the menu and institution demonstration.
Common mistake: Setting every piece of cutlery for every meal creates confusion; only the planned menu equipment should be laid.
Module summary: Professional table laying is the visible result of planning, equipment knowledge, and disciplined mise-en-place.

14. Explain Mise-en-scene, mise-en-place and laying tables. [3 marks]
