Module 1

Basic Accounting Terms & Principles

Duration: 13 Hours | Cognitive Level: Understanding

Meaning and Definition of Accounting

Accounting is the process of identifying, measuring, recording, and communicating economic information to permit informed judgments and decisions by users of the information.

Basic Accounting Terms

  • Entity: A business unit that has a separate existence (e.g., a company).
  • Transaction: An exchange of goods or services for money or money's worth.
  • Assets: Economic resources owned by the business (e.g., Cash, Building).
  • Liabilities: Debts or obligations the business owes to outsiders.
  • Capital (Equity): The owner's investment in the business.
  • Revenue: Income earned from the sale of goods or services.
  • Expense: Costs incurred to earn revenue.

Accounting Principles (GAAP US Standard)

Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP) are the standard framework of guidelines for financial accounting. Key principles include:

  • Business Entity Concept: Business is treated as separate from its owner.
  • Money Measurement: Only transactions measurable in money are recorded.
  • Going Concern: Business will continue to operate for the foreseeable future.
  • Dual Aspect: Every transaction has two sides (Debit and Credit).

The Accounting Equation

Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity

This is the foundation of the Double Entry System.

Golden Rules of Accounting (Traditional Approach)

Account Type Debit Rule Credit Rule
Personal A/c Debit the Receiver Credit the Giver
Real A/c Debit what comes in Credit what goes out
Nominal A/c Debit all Expenses/Losses Credit all Incomes/Gains
Module 2

Journalizing and Ledger Posting

Duration: 13 Hours | Cognitive Level: Applying

Double Entry Bookkeeping

Every transaction affects at least two accounts. One account is debited and another is credited with an equal amount.

Books of Original Entry: The Journal

The Journal is a chronological record of all transactions. The process of recording in a journal is called Journalizing.

Example Transaction: Purchased furniture for cash $5,000.
Analysis: 1. Furniture A/c (Real A/c) - Comes in - Debit. 2. Cash A/c (Real A/c) - Goes out - Credit.
Journal Entry:
Furniture A/c ... Dr 5,000
  To Cash A/c ... 5,000
(Being furniture purchased for cash)

Ledger Posting

A Ledger is a book containing all accounts (Personal, Real, Nominal). Posting is the process of transferring entries from the Journal to the Ledger.

Banking Transactions

Recording transactions involving bank accounts, such as deposits, withdrawals, and payments by cheque.

  • Cash deposited into bank: Bank A/c Dr, Cash A/c Cr.
  • Cash withdrawn for office use: Cash A/c Dr, Bank A/c Cr.
Module 3

Special Journals and Trial Balance

Duration: 16 Hours | Cognitive Level: Applying

Special Journals (Subsidiary Books)

When transactions are numerous, the journal is subdivided into special journals:

  • Purchase Day Book: To record credit purchases of goods.
  • Sales Day Book: To record credit sales of goods.
  • Purchase Return Book: To record goods returned to suppliers.
  • Sales Return Book: To record goods returned by customers.
  • Journal Proper: For transactions that don't fit in other books (e.g., opening entries, adjustment entries).

Single Column Cash Book

Records only cash receipts (Debit side) and cash payments (Credit side). It serves both as a journal and a ledger account.

Trial Balance

A statement prepared with the debit and credit balances of ledger accounts to test the arithmetical accuracy of the books.

Classification of Errors

  1. Errors of Omission: Transaction completely or partially omitted.
  2. Errors of Commission: Wrong recording, wrong posting, or wrong totaling.
  3. Errors of Principle: Violating accounting principles (e.g., treating capital expense as revenue).
  4. Compensating Errors: One error cancels out another.
Module 4

Final Accounts and Adjustments

Duration: 16 Hours | Cognitive Level: Applying

Trading Account

Prepared to find out the Gross Profit or Gross Loss. It includes direct expenses and direct revenues.

Formula: Gross Profit = Net Sales - Cost of Goods Sold.

Profit and Loss Account

Prepared to find out the Net Profit or Net Loss. It includes indirect expenses (like rent, salary) and indirect incomes.

Balance Sheet

A statement of the financial position of a business on a specific date, showing Assets, Liabilities, and Capital.

Adjusting Entries

Entries made at the end of the accounting period to assign revenues and expenses to the period in which they occurred.

  • Closing Stock: The value of unsold goods at the end of the year. (Credited to Trading A/c, shown as Asset in Balance Sheet).
  • Depreciation: The permanent decrease in the value of fixed assets due to wear and tear. (Debited to P&L A/c, deducted from Asset in Balance Sheet).
Worked Example: A machine costs $10,000. Depreciation rate is 10% per annum.
Calculation: $10,000 × 10/100 = $1,000.
Adjustment Entry:
Depreciation A/c ... Dr 1,000
  To Machinery A/c ... 1,000

Module-wise Questions (Q&A)

Module 1

What is the Business Entity Concept?
It is an accounting principle which states that the business and its owner are two separate legal entities. Therefore, the owner's personal transactions are not recorded in the business books.

Module 2

Why is the Journal called the 'Book of Original Entry'?
Because it is the first place where a business transaction is recorded in a chronological order before being posted to the ledger.

Module 3

Differentiate between Error of Omission and Error of Commission.
Error of Omission occurs when a transaction is completely or partially left out of the books. Error of Commission occurs due to wrong recording, wrong posting, or wrong totaling of an entry.

Module 4

What is Depreciation?
Depreciation is the gradual and permanent decrease in the value of a fixed asset due to usage, wear and tear, or obsolescence. It is treated as an indirect expense.
