Module 1: Analysis of Acid Radicals (Anions)

9 Hours

Acid radicals are the negatively charged ions in an inorganic salt. The analysis involves systematic identification using preliminary and confirmatory tests.

Scope of Radicals

The syllabus requires identification of the following acid radicals:

  • Carbonate (CO₃²⁻)
  • Sulphide (S²⁻)
  • Sulphite (SO₃²⁻)
  • Sulphate (SO₄²⁻)
  • Thiosulphate (S₂O₃²⁻)
  • Nitrate (NO₃⁻)
  • Fluoride (F⁻)
  • Chloride (Cl⁻)
  • Bromide (Br⁻)
  • Iodide (I⁻)
  • Acetate (CH₃COO⁻)
  • Oxalate (C₂O₄²⁻)
  • Phosphate (PO₄³⁻)
  • Borate (BO₃³⁻)
  • Chromate (CrO₄²⁻)
Systematic Procedure for Acid Radicals
  1. Action of Dilute HCl: Carbonate (brisk effervescence), Sulphide (rotten egg smell), Sulphite (burning sulphur smell).
  2. Action of Conc. H₂SO₄: Chloride (pungent fumes), Nitrate (brown fumes with copper turnings), Acetate (vinegar smell), Oxalate (CO + CO₂ evolution).
  3. Special Tests: Sulphate (BaCl₂ test), Phosphate (Ammonium molybdate test), Borate (Ethyl borate test).
Malayalam support: ആസിഡ് റാഡിക്കലുകളെ തിരിച്ചറിയാൻ ആദ്യം നേർപ്പിച്ച HCl (Dilute HCl) ഉപയോഗിക്കുന്നു. ഇതിൽ മാറ്റം കണ്ടില്ലെങ്കിൽ ഗാഢ സൾഫ്യൂറിക് ആസിഡ് (Conc. H₂SO₄) ഉപയോഗിച്ച് പരിശോധിക്കുന്നു. സൾഫേറ്റ്, ഫോസ്ഫേറ്റ് എന്നിവയ്ക്ക് പ്രത്യേക ടെസ്റ്റുകൾ ആവശ്യമാണ്.

Module 2: Analysis of Basic Radicals (Cations)

10 Hours

Basic radicals are positively charged ions. They are classified into groups based on their solubility products and identified using group reagents.

Group Classification
Group Radicals Group Reagent
Group 0 Ammonium (NH₄⁺) NaOH
Group I Silver (Ag⁺), Lead (Pb²⁺), Mercury (Hg₂²⁺) Dilute HCl
Group II Copper (Cu²⁺), Cadmium (Cd²⁺), Bismuth (Bi³⁺), Arsenic (As³⁺), Antimony (Sb³⁺), Tin (Sn²⁺/Sn⁴⁺) H₂S gas in presence of Dil. HCl
Group III Iron (Fe³⁺), Aluminium (Al³⁺), Chromium (Cr³⁺) NH₄OH in presence of NH₄Cl
Group IV Zinc (Zn²⁺), Manganese (Mn²⁺), Cobalt (Co²⁺), Nickel (Ni²⁺) H₂S gas in presence of NH₄OH
Group V Barium (Ba²⁺), Strontium (Sr²⁺), Calcium (Ca²⁺) (NH₄)₂CO₃ in presence of NH₄Cl & NH₄OH
Group VI Magnesium (Mg²⁺), Sodium (Na⁺), Potassium (K⁺) Specific reagents (e.g., Na₂HPO₄ for Mg)

Module 3: Analysis of Simple Salts

12 Hours

A simple salt consists of one acid radical and one basic radical. The analysis must follow a systematic workflow:

  1. Preliminary Examination (Color, State, Solubility).
  2. Identification of Acid Radical.
  3. Confirmatory test for Acid Radical.
  4. Preparation of Original Solution (OS).
  5. Identification of Basic Radical Group.
  6. Identification of Basic Radical within the group.
  7. Confirmatory test for Basic Radical.
Syllabus Constraint: The simple salt should be soluble in water, dilute acids, or conc. HCl and should not involve interfering radicals (like Fluoride, Borate, Oxalate, Phosphate) that complicate group separation.

Module 4: Analysis of Binary Mixtures

14 Hours

Binary mixtures contain two simple salts, meaning two acid radicals and two basic radicals must be identified.

Strategy: In binary mixtures, one must check if the presence of one radical interferes with the test for another. For example, in a mixture of Chloride and Bromide, special separation is needed.

Open Ended Experiments (3 Hours)

Students are encouraged to apply their analytical skills to unknown samples or real-world materials like water hardness or soil minerals (qualitative aspect).

Formula Bank & Calculations

Solubility Product (Kₛₚ)

The principle behind group separation. A precipitate forms when the Ionic Product exceeds Kₛₚ.

Concept: For a salt AₓBᵧ,
Kₛₚ = [A]ˣ [B]ᵧ

Common Ion Effect

Used to suppress the ionization of weak electrolytes. Example: NH₄Cl suppresses NH₄OH ionization to ensure only Group III hydroxides precipitate.

Practice Questions & Answers

1. Why is NH₄Cl added before NH₄OH in Group III analysis?
To suppress the concentration of OH⁻ ions through the Common Ion Effect. This ensures that the ionic product of Group IV, V, and VI hydroxides does not exceed their solubility products, preventing them from precipitating along with Group III.
2. How do you identify the presence of Carbonate radical?
Treat the salt with dilute HCl. Brisk effervescence occurs due to the evolution of CO₂ gas. When this gas is passed through lime water, it turns milky due to the formation of Calcium Carbonate.
3. What is the confirmatory test for Nitrate?
Brown Ring Test: To the salt solution, add freshly prepared Ferrous Sulphate solution. Then, carefully add concentrated H₂SO₄ along the sides of the test tube. A brown ring forms at the junction of the two liquids.

Quick Revision

Group Reagents

  • Gr I: Dil. HCl
  • Gr II: H₂S + HCl
  • Gr III: NH₄Cl + NH₄OH

Flame Test Colors

  • Barium: Apple Green
  • Strontium: Crimson Red
  • Calcium: Brick Red

Common Mistakes

  • Not using freshly prepared FeSO₄ for Nitrate test.
  • Adding too much NH₄OH in Group III.

Glossary

  • Qualitative Analysis: Identification of the constituents of a substance.
  • Precipitate: An insoluble solid that emerges from a liquid solution.
  • Original Solution (OS): A clear solution of the salt in a suitable solvent (Water/HCl).
  • Radical: An atom or group of atoms carrying a charge.
