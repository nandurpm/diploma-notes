Course Overview

What this subject teaches

Electronic communication is the process of sending information such as voice, music, video or data from one place to another using electrical signals and electromagnetic waves.

Main purpose

Learn how low-frequency information is converted into a high-frequency signal suitable for transmission, and how the original information is recovered at the receiver.

Industrial value

Communication principles are used in radio, TV, mobile systems, wireless control, IoT modules, RF remote systems, telemetry, walkie-talkies, alarms and industrial monitoring.

Engineering connection

For electrical/electronics drawing work, this subject helps you understand block diagrams, RF symbols, antennas, receivers, transmitter cards, noise issues and signal-flow documentation.

Hard truth: Do not prepare this subject by memorizing only block names. You must understand signal flow: message → carrier modulation → transmission → antenna/channel → receiver → demodulation → output.
Study Method

How to study this handbook

Correct learning order

  1. Learn why modulation is needed.
  2. Master AM terms: carrier, sidebands, modulation index, bandwidth and power.
  3. Master FM terms: frequency deviation, modulation index, Carson bandwidth.
  4. Study pulse modulation and sampling theorem.
  5. Study transmitter and receiver block diagrams.
  6. Practise numerical problems until formulas are automatic.

Exam answer method

  1. Start with definition.
  2. Draw neat labelled diagram or waveform.
  3. Explain block-by-block or step-by-step.
  4. Write formula and substitute values carefully.
  5. Add comparison or application if asked.
  6. Finish with one-line conclusion.

CO1 • AM and FM • Calculations

Module 1 - Modulation, AM and FM

Solve AM/FM parameters from given signal values. Total syllabus load: 11 hours.

Core idea

Modulation is the process of changing a high-frequency carrier according to a low-frequency message signal. It is needed for practical antenna size, long-distance transmission, multiplexing and better noise performance.

Modulation എന്നത് low frequency message signal carrier signal-ന്റെ amplitude/frequency/phase മാറ്റി transmit ചെയ്യുന്നതാണ്. Carrier ഇല്ലെങ്കിൽ practical antenna size വളരെ വലിയതാകും.

Classification of modulation

Analog modulation includes AM, FM and PM. Pulse analog modulation includes PAM, PWM and PPM. Pulse digital modulation includes PCM.

Amplitude modulation

In AM, the amplitude of carrier varies according to message signal, while carrier frequency and phase remain constant.

AM spectrum

For single tone modulation, AM contains carrier at fc, lower sideband at fc - fm and upper sideband at fc + fm.

DSBSC, SSB, VSB

DSBSC suppresses carrier. SSB transmits one sideband. VSB transmits one full sideband and a small part of the other sideband.

Frequency modulation

In FM, carrier frequency varies according to message signal amplitude, while carrier amplitude remains constant.

AM vs FM

AM is simple and narrow bandwidth but more noise affected. FM has better noise immunity but needs larger bandwidth and more complex circuits.

AM formulas

m = Am / Ac
m = (Vmax - Vmin) / (Vmax + Vmin)
BWAM = 2fm
PT = PC(1 + m²/2)
Power in each sideband = PCm²/4

FM formulas

mf = δ / fm
BWFM ≈ 2(δ + fm) Carson's rule
Instantaneous frequency fi = fc + kf em(t)
FM has constant transmitted power if carrier amplitude is constant.

Module 2 - Sampling, Pulse Modulation and Antennas

Explain pulse modulation schemes and role of antennas. Total: 11 hours + 1 series test.

Core idea

Sampling converts continuous-time signals into a sequence of samples. Pulse modulation represents information using pulse amplitude, width, position or coded binary values. Antennas convert electrical signals into electromagnetic waves and vice versa.

Sampling theorem പ്രകാരം sampling frequency signal-ലെ maximum frequency-യുടെ ഇരട്ടിയിലും കൂടുതലായിരിക്കണം. അല്ലെങ്കിൽ aliasing ഉണ്ടാകും.

Sampling theorem

A band-limited signal can be reconstructed from samples if sampling frequency fs is at least twice the maximum signal frequency fm.

PAM

Pulse Amplitude Modulation varies pulse amplitude according to message amplitude. Pulse width and position remain constant.

PWM

Pulse Width Modulation varies pulse width according to message amplitude. Pulse amplitude and position remain constant.

PPM

Pulse Position Modulation varies pulse position according to message amplitude. Pulse amplitude and width remain constant.

PCM

Pulse Code Modulation samples, quantizes and encodes analog signal into binary code. It is widely used in digital communication.

Antenna role

Antenna provides efficient radiation/reception of electromagnetic waves. Important parameters include gain, directivity, bandwidth, radiation pattern and polarization.

Pulse modulation comparison

SchemeVaried quantityMain note
PAMPulse amplitudeSimple but noise sensitive
PWMPulse widthBetter noise immunity than PAM
PPMPulse positionGood noise immunity; needs synchronization
PCMBinary codeDigital; needs sampling, quantization and coding

Antenna terms

  • Radiation pattern: graphical field strength distribution.
  • Gain: concentration of radiated power in a direction.
  • Directivity: ability to focus radiation.
  • Bandwidth: frequency range of proper operation.
  • Polarization: orientation of electric field.
  • Microstrip antenna: compact PCB antenna used in mobiles, GPS, Wi-Fi and embedded systems.

CO3 • AM/FM Transmitters • Modulators • Noise

Module 3 - Transmitters and Communication Noise

Explain AM and FM transmitter operation, AM modulator circuits and noise. Total: 11 hours.

Core idea

A transmitter converts an information signal into a high-frequency, amplified and properly modulated RF signal suitable for radiation by antenna.

Transmitter block diagram പഠിക്കുമ്പോൾ audio/input amplifier, oscillator, modulator, RF amplifier, power amplifier, antenna എന്ന signal flow മനസ്സിലാക്കുക.

Low-level AM transmitter

Modulation is done at low power stage. Then RF amplifiers raise the power. It is efficient for small transmitters.

High-level AM transmitter

Modulation is done at final power amplifier stage. It is used for high-power broadcast transmitters.

Collector modulator

Collector voltage of RF amplifier is varied by audio signal, producing amplitude modulation.

Balanced modulator

Produces DSBSC output by suppressing carrier while retaining upper and lower sidebands.

Armstrong FM transmitter

Indirect FM generation method. It starts with phase modulation and uses frequency multipliers/mixers to obtain required FM frequency deviation.

Pre-emphasis and de-emphasis

Pre-emphasis boosts high audio frequencies at transmitter. De-emphasis restores them at receiver, reducing high-frequency noise effect.

Noise

Unwanted electrical disturbance added to signal. External noise includes atmospheric, extraterrestrial and man-made noise. Internal noise includes thermal, shot and flicker noise.

SNR

Signal-to-noise ratio compares useful signal power with noise power. Higher SNR means better communication quality.

Module 4 - AM and FM Receivers

Illustrate the working principle of AM and FM receivers. Total: 10 hours + 1 series test.

Core idea

A receiver selects the desired RF signal, amplifies it, converts it to IF if required, demodulates it and delivers the original information to the output device.

Receiver-ൽ tuning/selectivity important ആണ്. Superheterodyne receiver RF signal-നെ fixed IF ആക്കി process ചെയ്യുന്നതിനാൽ sensitivity/selectivity മെച്ചപ്പെടുന്നു.

Receiver characteristics

Selectivity, sensitivity, fidelity and noise figure decide receiver performance.

Superheterodyne receiver

Incoming RF signal is mixed with local oscillator output to produce fixed IF. Fixed IF amplification improves gain and selectivity.

Choice of IF

IF must balance image rejection and selectivity. AM broadcast commonly uses 455 kHz IF.

AM receiver

Uses RF amplifier, mixer, local oscillator, IF amplifier, detector, AF amplifier and speaker.

FM receiver

Uses RF stage, mixer, IF amplifier, limiter, FM detector/discriminator, de-emphasis and audio amplifier.

AM demodulator and AGC

Diode detector recovers audio from AM envelope. AGC adjusts receiver gain to keep output nearly constant for varying input strength.

AM receiver vs FM receiver

PointAM receiverFM receiver
DetectorEnvelope detectorFrequency discriminator / detector
LimiterUsually not requiredRequired to remove amplitude noise
BandwidthLowerHigher
Noise performancePoorerBetter

AGC types

  • Simple AGC: gain control voltage starts acting as soon as signal appears.
  • Delayed AGC: gain reduction starts only after signal exceeds threshold.
  • Delayed AGC gives better sensitivity for weak signals.

Solved Examples

Worked numerical problems

Example 1 - AM modulation index from envelope

Given Vmax = 15 V and Vmin = 5 V. m = (Vmax - Vmin)/(Vmax + Vmin) = (15 - 5)/(15 + 5) = 10/20 = 0.5 or 50%.

Example 2 - AM sideband frequencies and bandwidth

Given fc = 1 MHz and fm = 5 kHz. USB = 1000 + 5 = 1005 kHz. LSB = 1000 - 5 = 995 kHz. Bandwidth = 2fm = 10 kHz.

Example 3 - AM transmitted power

Carrier power PC = 100 W, m = 0.8. PT = PC(1 + m²/2) = 100(1 + 0.64/2) = 100(1.32) = 132 W.

Example 4 - FM modulation index

Frequency deviation δ = 75 kHz, modulating frequency fm = 15 kHz. mf = δ/fm = 75/15 = 5.

Example 5 - FM bandwidth by Carson rule

δ = 75 kHz, fm = 15 kHz. BW = 2(δ + fm) = 2(75 + 15) = 180 kHz.

Example 6 - Sampling theorem

Maximum audio frequency = 4 kHz. Minimum sampling frequency fs = 2fm = 8 kHz. Practically choose more than 8 kHz to avoid aliasing.

Answer Key

Complete model answers - condensed exam format

1. Modulation and need

Modulation is the process of varying a carrier parameter according to message signal. It is needed for practical antenna size, long-distance transmission, multiplexing and reduced interference.

2. AM modulation index

AM modulation index m is the ratio of modulating signal amplitude to carrier amplitude. m = Am/Ac = (Vmax - Vmin)/(Vmax + Vmin).

3. FM deviation and index

Frequency deviation is maximum change in carrier frequency from unmodulated value. FM modulation index mf = δ/fm.

4. Sampling theorem

A band-limited signal can be reconstructed from samples if sampling frequency is at least twice the maximum signal frequency: fs ≥ 2fm.

5. Pulse modulation expansions

PAM - Pulse Amplitude Modulation; PWM - Pulse Width Modulation; PPM - Pulse Position Modulation; PCM - Pulse Code Modulation.

6. Radiation pattern

Radiation pattern is the graphical representation of radiated field strength or power in different directions around antenna.

7. Pre-emphasis

Pre-emphasis boosts high-frequency audio components before FM transmission to improve noise performance. Receiver uses de-emphasis to restore original response.

8. SNR

Signal-to-noise ratio is ratio of useful signal power to noise power. SNR = S/N; in dB, SNR = 10 log10(S/N).

9. Receiver characteristics

Sensitivity, selectivity, fidelity and noise figure are important receiver characteristics.

10. AGC

Automatic Gain Control develops control voltage from received signal strength and adjusts receiver gain to keep output nearly constant.

11. Classification

Continuous wave modulation: AM, FM, PM. Pulse analog modulation: PAM, PWM, PPM. Pulse digital modulation: PCM.

12. AM expression

AM wave: s(t)=Ac[1+m cos ωmt]cos ωct. It contains carrier at fc and sidebands at fc+fm and fc-fm.

13. AM vs FM

AM varies amplitude, needs smaller bandwidth and is noise sensitive. FM varies frequency, has constant amplitude, better noise immunity and larger bandwidth.

14. Sampling and aliasing

If fs ≥ 2fm, original signal can be recovered. If fs is less, spectral overlap occurs and false low-frequency components appear; this is aliasing.

15. Pulse schemes

PAM varies amplitude, PWM varies width, PPM varies position, PCM converts samples into binary code after quantization.

16. AM transmitters

Low-level AM performs modulation at low power and amplifies later. High-level AM performs modulation at final RF power stage and is used in high-power broadcasting.

17. Noise types

External noise includes atmospheric, extraterrestrial and man-made noise. Internal noise includes thermal, shot and flicker noise.

18. Diode detector and AGC

Diode detector rectifies AM envelope and RC network filters RF, leaving audio. AGC uses detected DC to control gain of earlier stages.

19. Numerical answer

m=(18-6)/(18+6)=12/24=0.5. USB=1 MHz+10 kHz=1010 kHz. LSB=1 MHz-10 kHz=990 kHz. Bandwidth=2fm=20 kHz.

20. AM spectrum and variants

AM spectrum has carrier, LSB and USB. BW=2fm. PT=PC(1+m²/2). DSBSC suppresses carrier, SSB transmits one sideband, VSB transmits one sideband plus vestige.

21. FM answer model

FM varies carrier frequency according to message. mf=δ/fm. Carson bandwidth is 2(δ+fm). Example: δ=75 kHz, fm=15 kHz, BW=180 kHz.

22. Antennas

Antenna converts electrical energy to EM waves and vice versa. Parameters include radiation pattern, gain, directivity, bandwidth, polarization and impedance. Microstrip antennas are compact PCB antennas used in mobile, GPS, Wi-Fi and embedded systems.

23. Transmitters

AM transmitter contains audio/input amplifier, carrier oscillator, modulator, RF amplifier, power amplifier and antenna. Armstrong FM uses phase modulation and frequency multiplication to generate stable FM.

24. Receivers

Superheterodyne receiver converts RF to fixed IF using mixer and local oscillator. AM receiver uses envelope detector; FM receiver uses limiter and FM detector. FM receiver has better noise performance and wider bandwidth.
