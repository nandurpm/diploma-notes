Course overview

What this subject actually teaches

Electronic communication is the transfer of information such as speech, music, video, control data or measurements using electrical signals and electromagnetic waves.

Main purpose

Learn how low-frequency information is converted into a high-frequency carrier signal and then recovered at the receiver.

Industrial use

Radio, TV, walkie-talkies, mobile systems, RF remote controls, telemetry, wireless sensors, IoT gateways and industrial alarms use these principles.

Drawing-engineering value

This subject helps in reading signal-flow diagrams, transmitter cards, receiver blocks, antenna layouts, RF labels, noise notes and test documentation.

Blunt warning: If you memorize only block names, you will fail to write proper answers. You must know signal flow, purpose of each block, formula meaning and waveform change.
Study method

Correct way to use this handbook

Learning order

  1. Understand need of modulation and carrier.
  2. Study AM definition, spectrum, bandwidth and power.
  3. Practise AM numerical problems until formulas are automatic.
  4. Study FM deviation, modulation index and Carson rule.
  5. Study sampling theorem and pulse modulation comparison.
  6. Study antennas, transmitters, noise, receivers and demodulators.

Exam answer structure

  1. Write definition first.
  2. Draw neat labelled block diagram or waveform.
  3. Explain block-by-block operation.
  4. Write formula and substitute values carefully.
  5. Add comparison, merits, limitations or application.
  6. End with a short conclusion.

Module 1 - Modulation, AM and FM

Classify modulation techniques, interpret AM parameters, derive FM spectrum expression and solve modulation index, bandwidth and power problems.

Need of modulation

Direct transmission of audio-frequency signal is inefficient because antenna size becomes impractical, radiation is poor, signals mix together and long-distance transmission becomes weak. Modulation shifts the information to a high-frequency carrier.

Modulation ചെയ്യുന്നതിലൂടെ low frequency message high frequency carrier-ൽ കയറ്റി transmit ചെയ്യുന്നു. അതുകൊണ്ട് antenna size കുറയുന്നു, range കൂടുന്നു, multiplexing possible ആകുന്നു.

Modulation classification

Continuous-wave modulation: AM, FM, PM. Pulse analog modulation: PAM, PWM, PPM. Pulse digital modulation: PCM.

Amplitude Modulation

Carrier amplitude varies according to message amplitude; carrier frequency and phase remain constant.

AM spectrum

A single-tone AM wave has carrier fc, lower sideband fc - fm and upper sideband fc + fm.

DSBSC, SSB, VSB

DSBSC suppresses carrier. SSB transmits one sideband only. VSB transmits one full sideband and a small trace of the other sideband.

Frequency Modulation

Carrier frequency changes according to message amplitude; carrier amplitude remains constant.

Applications

AM is used in broadcast radio and aviation communication. FM is used in FM broadcast, TV sound, telemetry and high-quality radio links.

AM formula bank

Module 2 - Sampling, pulse modulation and antennas

Explain sampling theorem, compare PAM/PWM/PPM/PCM and understand how antennas radiate electromagnetic waves.

Sampling theorem

A band-limited signal with maximum frequency fm can be completely recovered from samples if sampling frequency fs is at least twice the maximum message frequency.

fs ≥ 2fm Nyquist sampling criterion
Sampling theorem-ൽ fs കുറഞ്ഞാൽ aliasing വരും. അതായത് original signal correct ആയി reconstruct ചെയ്യാൻ കഴിയില്ല.

Pulse modulation comparison

SchemeVaried parameterNote
PAMPulse amplitudeSimplest pulse modulation
PWMPulse widthUsed in control and switching systems
PPMPulse positionGood noise immunity compared to PAM
PCMDigital codeSampling + quantization + coding

PAM

The amplitude of each pulse follows the instantaneous value of the message signal. It is affected by amplitude noise.

PWM

The pulse width changes according to the message amplitude. Amplitude and position remain constant.

PPM

The pulse position changes according to the message signal. Pulse amplitude and width remain constant.

PCM

The analog signal is sampled, quantized and encoded into binary form. It is the base of many digital communication systems.

Antenna basics

An antenna converts electrical signals into electromagnetic waves during transmission and converts electromagnetic waves into electrical signals during reception.

  • Radiation pattern shows directional distribution of radiated energy.
  • Gain indicates concentration of radiated power in a direction.
  • Bandwidth is the frequency range over which antenna performs properly.
  • Polarization is orientation of electric field.
  • Directivity shows how concentrated the radiation is.

Microwave and microstrip antennas

Microwave antennas operate at high frequencies and are used in radar, satellite, point-to-point links and mobile communication. Microstrip antennas are low-profile printed antennas used in compact devices.

For exams, write structure, radiation pattern, parameters and applications. Do not write vague sentences like “antenna sends waves” only.

Module 3 - AM/FM transmitters and noise

Explain low-level and high-level AM transmitters, collector modulator, balanced modulator, Armstrong FM transmitter, pre-emphasis, de-emphasis and noise.

AM transmitter

An AM transmitter produces a high-frequency carrier, modulates it with audio/message signal, amplifies RF power and feeds it to the antenna.

  1. Audio signal is processed by AF amplifier.
  2. RF oscillator generates carrier.
  3. Modulator combines carrier and audio.
  4. RF power amplifier increases signal power.
  5. Antenna radiates the modulated wave.

FM transmitter

In Armstrong FM transmitter, stable phase modulation is first produced and then converted into wideband FM using frequency multipliers and mixers.

  1. Crystal oscillator provides stable carrier.
  2. Balanced modulator and phase shifter produce NBFM.
  3. Frequency multipliers increase deviation.
  4. Mixer adjusts final carrier frequency.
  5. Power amplifier drives antenna.

Low-level AM

Modulation happens at low power stage; then RF amplifiers boost power. Easier but requires linear RF power amplifiers.

High-level AM

Modulation happens at final power amplifier stage. More efficient for high-power broadcasting.

Balanced modulator

Produces DSBSC by suppressing carrier and passing sidebands. Important in SSB generation.

Noise in communication

Noise is an unwanted random signal that disturbs the desired signal. It reduces clarity, accuracy and reliability of communication.

Noise typeExamples
External noiseAtmospheric, extraterrestrial, man-made industrial noise
Internal noiseThermal noise, shot noise, transit-time noise

Signal-to-noise ratio

SNR compares signal power with noise power. Higher SNR means better signal quality.

SNR = Signal power / Noise power
SNR(dB) = 10 log10(S/N)
Pre-emphasis boosts high-frequency audio before FM transmission. De-emphasis at receiver restores original response and reduces high-frequency noise.

Module 4 - Receivers, demodulation and AGC

Study receiver characteristics, superheterodyne receiver, AM/FM receiver block diagrams, IF choice, diode detector and AGC.

Receiver characteristics

  • Sensitivity: ability to receive weak signals.
  • Selectivity: ability to separate desired station from nearby stations.
  • Fidelity: ability to reproduce original signal without distortion.
  • Noise figure: measure of noise added by receiver.

Superheterodyne principle

The incoming RF signal is mixed with local oscillator output to produce a fixed intermediate frequency. Most amplification and selectivity are then done at the IF stage.

IF = |fLO - fRF|

Fixed IF gives stable gain, sharp selectivity and easier filter design.

AM receiver block flow

  1. Antenna receives modulated RF signal.
  2. RF amplifier improves weak signal.
  3. Mixer combines RF with local oscillator.
  4. IF amplifier provides gain and selectivity.
  5. Diode detector recovers audio.
  6. AF amplifier drives speaker.

FM receiver block flow

  1. Antenna and RF amplifier select station.
  2. Mixer converts RF to IF.
  3. Limiter removes amplitude variations.
  4. FM detector recovers audio.
  5. De-emphasis reduces high-frequency noise.
  6. AF amplifier drives output.

Diode detector

A diode detector rectifies AM wave. The RC circuit follows the envelope of AM signal and recovers the original audio.

Correct RC selection is important. Too small RC causes ripple; too large RC causes diagonal clipping.

AGC

Automatic Gain Control changes receiver gain automatically according to received signal strength. Strong signal reduces gain; weak signal increases gain.

Simple AGC acts immediately. Delayed AGC starts after signal exceeds a set threshold.

Solved examples

Numerical practice with working

Example 1 - AM bandwidth

An AM signal is modulated by 5 kHz audio. Find bandwidth.

B = 2fm = 2 × 5 kHz = 10 kHz

Example 2 - Envelope modulation index

If Vmax = 50 V and Vmin = 10 V, find m.

m = (50-10)/(50+10) = 40/60 = 0.667

Example 3 - AM total power

Carrier power is 100 W and m = 0.6. Find total power.

Pt = 100(1+0.6²/2)=100(1.18)=118 W

Example 4 - FM bandwidth

Frequency deviation is 75 kHz and modulating frequency is 15 kHz.

B = 2(75+15)=180 kHz

Example 5 - Sampling frequency

Maximum message frequency is 4 kHz. Minimum sampling frequency?

fs = 2fm = 8 kHz minimum

Example 6 - Sideband frequencies

Carrier = 1 MHz and message = 5 kHz.

USB = 1005 kHz, LSB = 995 kHz

Answer key

Short guide answers

Part A key
  1. Modulation: process of varying carrier parameter according to message signal.
  2. Sampling theorem: fs must be at least 2fm for faithful reconstruction.
  3. Radiation pattern: graphical representation of radiated power in different directions.
  4. Pre-emphasis: boosting high-frequency audio components before FM transmission.
  5. Selectivity: ability of receiver to separate desired signal from adjacent signals.
Part B answer points

For every 6-mark answer: definition, diagram/table, 4 to 6 strong points and conclusion are enough. Do not write paragraph-only answers for block diagrams.

  • AM vs FM: changed parameter, bandwidth, noise, power, complexity and applications.
  • Pulse modulation: compare varied quantity, circuit complexity, noise effect and use.
  • Noise: definition, external noise, internal noise, SNR and reduction methods.
  • Diode detector: diode rectification, RC envelope following, recovered AF output.
Part C answer method

For 15-mark answers, use this structure: 2 marks definition, 4 marks diagram/waveform, 6 marks working explanation, 2 marks formulas/comparison, 1 mark conclusion/application.
