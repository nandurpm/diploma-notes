Module 1: Basic Concepts of Statistics

Duration: 13 Hours | CO1

1.1 Meaning and Functions of Statistics

Statistics is the science of collecting, organizing, analyzing, interpreting, and presenting data. In a business context, it provides a numerical basis for decision-making.

Functions of Statistics:

  • Simplification: It simplifies complex data into understandable forms (averages, percentages).
  • Comparison: It helps in comparing data across different time periods or regions.
  • Policy Formulation: Helps governments and businesses frame policies based on trends.
  • Forecasting: Predicting future trends based on past data.

1.2 Types of Data and Collection Methods

Primary Data

Data collected for the first time by the investigator for a specific purpose.

  • Direct Personal Interview
  • Mailed Questionnaires
  • Telephone Interviews

Secondary Data

Data which has already been collected by someone else and processed.

  • Government Publications (Census)
  • Journals and Reports
  • Websites and Newspapers
1.3 Classification and Tabulation

Classification: The process of arranging data into groups or classes according to common characteristics.

  • Geographical (Area-wise)
  • Chronological (Time-wise)
  • Qualitative (Attributes like gender, literacy)
  • Quantitative (Numerical values like height, weight)

Tabulation: Logical and systematic arrangement of statistical data in columns and rows.

1.4 Graphical Representation

Visual representation helps in quick understanding of data distribution.

  • Histogram: A set of rectangles with bases as class intervals and heights proportional to frequencies.
  • Frequency Polygon: Formed by joining the mid-points of the tops of the rectangles in a histogram.
  • Ogive (Cumulative Frequency Curve): Used to find the median. There are two types: "Less than" and "More than".

Module 2: Measures of Central Value & Dispersion

Duration: 19 Hours | CO2

2.1 Measures of Central Value (Averages)

An average is a single value that represents the entire set of data.

Arithmetic Mean

Sum of all observations divided by the number of observations.

Mean (x̄) = Σx / n

Median

The middle value of the data when arranged in ascending or descending order.

Mode

The value that occurs most frequently in the data set.

2.2 Measures of Dispersion

Dispersion measures the extent to which data items are scattered around the central value.

Standard Deviation (σ)

The square root of the arithmetic mean of the squares of deviations from the arithmetic mean.

σ = √[ Σ(x - x̄)² / n ]
Example: Calculate Mean and SD for 2, 4, 6.
Mean: (2+4+6)/3 = 4.
Deviations (x-x̄): -2, 0, 2.
Squares: 4, 0, 4. Sum = 8.
SD: √(8/3) ≈ 1.63.

Module 3: Correlation and Regression Analysis

Duration: 12 Hours | CO3

3.1 Correlation Analysis

Correlation studies the relationship between two variables. If change in one variable results in a change in the other, they are correlated.

  • Positive Correlation: Both move in the same direction (e.g., Price and Supply).
  • Negative Correlation: Move in opposite directions (e.g., Price and Demand).

Karl Pearson’s Coefficient of Correlation (r)

r = Σxy / √(Σx² * Σy²)

Where x = (X - X̄) and y = (Y - Ȳ).

3.2 Regression Analysis

Regression is used to estimate the value of one variable (dependent) based on the value of another (independent).

  • Regression Line of Y on X: Y - Ȳ = byx(X - X̄)
  • Regression Line of X on Y: X - X̄ = bxy(Y - Ȳ)

Module 4: Index Numbers

Duration: 14 Hours | CO4

4.1 Construction of Index Numbers

Index numbers are specialized averages used to measure changes in a variable (like price) over time.

Laspeyres' Index

Uses base year quantities (q₀) as weights.

P₀₁ = [Σp₁q₀ / Σp₀q₀] × 100

Paasche's Index

Uses current year quantities (q₁) as weights.

P₀₁ = [Σp₁q₁ / Σp₀q₁] × 100

Fisher's Ideal Index

Geometric mean of Laspeyres and Paasche index.

Fisher = √(Laspeyres × Paasche)
4.2 Tests of Adequacy
  • Time Reversal Test: P₀₁ × P₁₀ = 1
  • Factor Reversal Test: P₀₁ × Q₀₁ = Σp₁q₁ / Σp₀q₀

Fisher's Index satisfies both tests, hence it is called "Ideal".

Formula Bank

Central Tendency

  • Mean: Σx / n
  • Weighted Mean: Σwx / Σw
  • Combined Mean: (n₁x̄₁ + n₂x̄₂) / (n₁ + n₂)

Dispersion & Correlation

  • Standard Deviation: √[Σ(x-x̄)² / n]
  • Karl Pearson r: Σxy / √(Σx² * Σy²)
  • Spearman's Rank: 1 - [6ΣD² / n(n²-1)]

Module-wise Practice Questions

Q1: Distinguish between Primary and Secondary data. (5 Marks)
Primary data is original data collected for the first time, while secondary data is already existing data collected by others. Primary data is more accurate but expensive; secondary data is cheaper but may not fit the specific needs perfectly.
Q2: What are the requisites of a good average? (3 Marks)
1. Rigidly defined. 2. Easy to understand and calculate. 3. Based on all observations. 4. Least affected by fluctuations of sampling.
Q3: Explain the properties of Karl Pearson's coefficient of correlation. (5 Marks)
1. The value of 'r' lies between -1 and +1. 2. It is independent of change of origin and scale. 3. If r = +1, it is perfect positive correlation. 4. If r = -1, it is perfect negative correlation. 5. If r = 0, there is no linear correlation.

Quick Revision

  • Statistics: Collection, Analysis, Interpretation.
  • Mean: Average; Median: Middle; Mode: Most frequent.
  • Standard Deviation: Best measure of dispersion.
  • Correlation (r): Relationship strength (-1 to +1).
  • Fisher's Index: Ideal index (satisfies TRT and FRT).

Glossary

Data

Variable

Population
The entire group under study.
Sample
A part of the population selected for study.
