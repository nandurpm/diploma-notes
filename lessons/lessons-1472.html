M1.01 — Evolution, definition, features, importance, scope and objectives of business (7 hours)

Concept and explanation

Business evolved from simple exchange to organised production, services, digital platforms, and global networks. It involves regular economic activity, production or procurement, sale or exchange, risk, and an objective of value creation or return. Its scope includes industry, trade, commerce, services, entrepreneurship, and support systems. Objectives may include survival, profit, growth, customer value, employment, innovation, and social responsibility.

M1.02 — Classifications of economic activities, components and functions (6 hours)

Concept and explanation

Economic activities are broadly grouped into production/industry, trade, and services; business activities may also be classified by size, ownership, sector, or output. Components include resources, people, finance, technology, information, and processes. Functions include purchasing, production/operations, marketing, finance, accounting, human resources, and administration.

1. Explain Evolution, definition, features, importance, scope and objectives of business. [3 marks]

Business evolved from simple exchange to organised production, services, digital platforms, and global networks. It involves regular economic activity, production or procurement, sale or exchange, risk, and an objective of value creation or return. Its scope includes industry, trade, commerce, services, entrepreneurship, and support systems. Objectives may include survival, profit, growth, customer value, employment, innovation, and social responsibility.

4. Explain Business ownerships and combinations. [3 marks]

Sole proprietorship is owned and controlled by one person; partnership distributes ownership and responsibility among partners. A limited liability company separates the business entity and limits liability according to law. Collaborations, strategic alliances, joint ventures, mergers, acquisitions, and consolidations are ways organisations share capability, enter markets, combine operations, or change control. Economics studies how scarce resources are allocated to satisfy wants. Business decisions involve demand, supply, price, cost, revenue, competition, opportunity cost, and productivity. Managers use economic reasoning to decide what to produce, how much capacity to operate, and how to respond to changes.

Top or strategic management sets direction and long-term policy; middle management converts strategy into departmental plans; supervisory or first-line management guides daily work.
