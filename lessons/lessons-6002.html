M1.012 hours • Remembering

Constitution — definition and significance

A Constitution is the supreme framework of rules and principles that establishes government institutions, distributes powers, limits public authority and protects the relationship between the State and citizens. Its significance lies in providing legitimacy, stability, accountability and a common standard for governance.

മലയാളം സഹായക്കുറിപ്പ്: ഭരണഘടന ഒരു രാജ്യത്തിന്റെ പരമോന്നത നിയമചട്ടക്കൂടാണ്. സർക്കാർ സ്ഥാപനങ്ങളുടെ ഘടന, അധികാരവിതരണം, അധികാരപരിധി, പൗരന്മാരുടെ സംരക്ഷണം എന്നിവ ഇതിലൂടെ നിശ്ചയിക്കപ്പെടുന്നു.
Supreme lawGovernment structureLimited powerCitizen protection
M1.022 hours • Remembering

Evolution of the Indian Constitution

The syllabus traces constitutional evolution through the reforms of 1909, 1919 and 1935, followed by the Indian Independence Act 1947. Together, these stages show the gradual movement from colonial administration toward representative institutions and independent constitutional government.

മലയാളം സഹായക്കുറിപ്പ്: 1909, 1919, 1935 നിയമപരിഷ്കാരങ്ങളും 1947-ലെ Indian Independence Act-ഉം ഇന്ത്യയുടെ ഭരണഘടനാ വികാസത്തിലെ പ്രധാന ഘട്ടങ്ങളാണ്. കോളനി ഭരണത്തിൽ നിന്ന് സ്വതന്ത്ര ഭരണത്തിലേക്കുള്ള മാറ്റം ഇവ വ്യക്തമാക്കുന്നു.
Remember the four years in order: 1909 → 1919 → 1935 → 1947.
Explain each as a stage in constitutional and representative development.
Conclude with the transition to an independent constitutional system.
M1.032 hours • Understanding

Preamble, basic principles and basic-structure debate

The Preamble introduces the source, nature, objectives and guiding values of the Constitution. Its core values are Justice, Liberty, Equality and Fraternity. The basic-structure idea means that constitutional amendment cannot destroy the Constitution’s essential identity and foundational principles.

മലയാളം സഹായക്കുറിപ്പ്: Preamble ഭരണഘടനയുടെ ലക്ഷ്യവും ആത്മാവും വ്യക്തമാക്കുന്നു. Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity എന്നിവയാണ് പ്രധാന മൂല്യങ്ങൾ. ഭേദഗതി ചെയ്യുമ്പോഴും ഭരണഘടനയുടെ അടിസ്ഥാന സ്വഭാവം നശിപ്പിക്കരുത് എന്നതാണ് basic structure ആശയം.
M1.042 hours • Understanding

Salient features of the Indian Constitution

  • Written and detailed Constitution.
  • Combination of rigidity and flexibility in amendment.
  • Bicameral Legislature at the Union level.
  • Federal distribution of powers with unitary features.
  • Integrated judicial system.
  • Single citizenship.
  • Emergency provisions.
മലയാളം സഹായക്കുറിപ്പ്: ഇന്ത്യൻ ഭരണഘടന എഴുതപ്പെട്ടതും വിശദവുമായതാണ്. Federal-ഉം unitary-ഉം സവിശേഷതകൾ ചേർന്നിരിക്കുന്നു. Integrated judiciary, single citizenship, bicameral legislature, emergency provisions എന്നിവ പ്രധാന പ്രത്യേകതകളാണ്.
Module 1 answer pattern: Begin with a definition, arrange the historical stages in order, identify the Preamble values, and finish with a classified list of salient features.

Part III — Fundamental Rights, Articles 12 to 35

Fundamental Rights protect individual freedom, equality and dignity. They are enforceable through courts. For study, group them into six broad classes.

Right to Freedom19–22Basic civil freedoms and safeguards relating to life, liberty and arrest.സ്വാതന്ത്ര്യവും വ്യക്തിസുരക്ഷയും.

Right to Freedom of Religion25–28Freedom of conscience and religious practice subject to constitutional limits.മതവിശ്വാസ സ്വാതന്ത്ര്യം.
Cultural and Educational Rights29–30Protection of language, culture and minority educational interests.ഭാഷ, സംസ്കാരം, വിദ്യാഭ്യാസ അവകാശങ്ങൾ.

M2.021 hour • Understanding

Part IV — Directive Principles of State Policy

DPSP are constitutional directions to the State for building a just social order and guiding public policy. They are not directly enforceable by courts, but they are fundamental to governance and law-making.

മലയാളം സഹായക്കുറിപ്പ്: DPSP സർക്കാർ നയങ്ങൾ രൂപപ്പെടുത്താനുള്ള ഭരണഘടനാപരമായ മാർഗനിർദ്ദേശങ്ങളാണ്. ഇവ നേരിട്ട് കോടതിയിൽ enforce ചെയ്യാൻ കഴിയില്ലെങ്കിലും നല്ല ഭരണത്തിന് അടിസ്ഥാനമാണ്.
M2.031 hour • Understanding

Part IVA — Fundamental Duties, Article 51A

Fundamental Duties remind every citizen to respect the Constitution and national symbols, preserve unity, promote harmony, protect the environment, develop scientific temper, safeguard public property, strive for excellence and support education of children as constitutionally required.

മലയാളം സഹായക്കുറിപ്പ്: Fundamental Duties പൗരന്മാരുടെ ഉത്തരവാദിത്വങ്ങളാണ്. ഭരണഘടനയെ മാനിക്കുക, ഐക്യം സംരക്ഷിക്കുക, പരിസ്ഥിതി കാക്കുക, ശാസ്ത്രീയ മനോഭാവം വളർത്തുക, പൊതുസമ്പത്ത് സംരക്ഷിക്കുക എന്നിവ പ്രധാനമാണ്.

M3.013 hours • Understanding

Legislature — Union Parliament and State Legislature

Lok Sabha

House of the People. It represents the people directly and performs legislative, financial and accountability functions.

ജനങ്ങളെ നേരിട്ട് പ്രതിനിധീകരിക്കുന്ന സഭ. നിയമനിർമ്മാണം, ധനകാര്യ നിയന്ത്രണം, Executive-ന്റെ ഉത്തരവാദിത്വം ഉറപ്പാക്കൽ എന്നിവ പ്രധാനമാണ്.

Rajya Sabha

Council of States. It represents the States and participates in Union law-making and constitutional functions.

States-നെ പ്രതിനിധീകരിക്കുന്ന സഭ. Union law-making-ൽ പങ്കാളിയാകുന്നു.

Speaker

Presides over the Lok Sabha, maintains order, regulates proceedings and protects the authority of the House.

Lok Sabha നടപടികൾ നിയന്ത്രിക്കുകയും സഭയിലെ ക്രമവും അധികാരവും സംരക്ഷിക്കുകയും ചെയ്യുന്നു.

State Legislature: The syllabus includes the Legislative Assembly and, where applicable, the Legislative Council. Their core functions are State law-making, financial approval and accountability of the State Executive.

M3.033 hours • Understanding

Judiciary — Supreme Court, High Court, Judicial Review and Independence

Supreme Court

Highest court in the integrated judicial system, constitutional guardian and final judicial authority within its jurisdiction.

High Court

Highest court at the State level, exercising constitutional and appellate functions within its jurisdiction.

Judicial Review

Power of courts to examine whether laws and executive actions conform to the Constitution.

Independence of Judiciary: Judicial decisions must be free from improper pressure so that the Constitution, rights and rule of law are protected impartially.

മലയാളം സഹായക്കുറിപ്പ്: Judiciary നിയമങ്ങളെ വ്യാഖ്യാനിക്കുകയും ഭരണഘടനാ വിരുദ്ധമായ നിയമ/Executive നടപടികൾ പരിശോധിക്കുകയും ചെയ്യുന്നു. Judicial independence നിഷ്പക്ഷമായ നീതിന്യായത്തിനാവശ്യമാണ്.
High-score comparison: Legislature makes law, Executive administers and implements law, Judiciary interprets law and tests constitutional validity.

Local Self-Government — 73rd and 74th Amendments

73rd Amendment — Panchayat Raj

Strengthens rural local self-government and democratic participation through Panchayat institutions.

ഗ്രാമപ്രദേശങ്ങളിലെ local self-government ശക്തിപ്പെടുത്തുന്ന ഭരണഘടനാ മാറ്റമാണ് 73rd Amendment.

74th Amendment — Nagarapalika

Strengthens urban local self-government through municipal institutions.

നഗരപ്രദേശങ്ങളിലെ municipalities / Nagarapalika സ്ഥാപനങ്ങളെ ശക്തിപ്പെടുത്തുന്ന മാറ്റമാണ് 74th Amendment.

RTI supports transparency and accountability by enabling citizens to seek information from public authorities according to the Act.

Model Question Paper with Complete Answers

INDIAN CONSTITUTION • Practice Model • Maximum Marks: 75 • Time: 3 Hours

This is a fresh practice paper prepared from the syllabus topics. It is not an official question paper.

PART A — Answer all questions. Each carries 1 mark. [5 × 1 = 5]

  1. Define Constitution.
  2. Name any two values stated in the Preamble.
  3. In which Part of the Constitution are Fundamental Duties placed?
  4. Name the three organs of government.
  5. Which constitutional amendment is associated with urban local self-government?

PART B — Answer any five. Each carries 8 marks. [5 × 8 = 40]

  1. Explain the significance of a Constitution and outline the stages 1909, 1919, 1935 and 1947.
  2. Explain the Preamble values and salient features of the Indian Constitution.
  3. Classify Fundamental Rights and write the corresponding Article ranges.
  4. Compare Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles of State Policy.
  5. Explain the composition and functions of the Union Parliament and State Legislature.

Part B full answers

B1. Significance and evolution

Definition: The Constitution is the supreme framework of government. Significance: 1. Creates the organs and institutions of government. 2. Distributes powers among institutions and levels of government. 3. Limits public power and promotes accountability. 4. Protects constitutional values and citizens. 5. Provides stability and a lawful method of change. Evolutional outline: • 1909 — an early constitutional reform stage listed in the syllabus. • 1919 — a further stage in representative and administrative development. • 1935 — a major pre-Constitution framework influencing governmental structure. • 1947 — Indian Independence Act and the transition to independent government. Conclusion: These stages show a gradual movement from colonial administration to independent constitutional governance.

B2. Preamble and salient features

The Preamble expresses the Constitution’s source, nature, objectives and guiding values. Core values: 1. Justice — social, economic and political fairness. 2. Liberty — freedom of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship within constitutional limits. 3. Equality — equal status and opportunity. 4. Fraternity — unity, dignity and common belonging. Salient features: • Written and detailed Constitution. • Combination of rigidity and flexibility. • Bicameral Union Legislature. • Federal structure with unitary features. • Integrated Judiciary. • Single citizenship. • Emergency provisions. Basic-structure idea: amendment power cannot destroy the Constitution’s essential identity. Conclusion: The Preamble supplies the values, while the salient features show the working design of the Constitution.

B3. Classification of Fundamental Rights

Part III extends from Articles 12 to 35. The six principal classes are: 1. Right to Equality — Articles 14 to 18. 2. Right to Freedom — Articles 19 to 22. 3. Right against Exploitation — Articles 23 and 24. 4. Right to Freedom of Religion — Articles 25 to 28. 5. Cultural and Educational Rights — Articles 29 and 30. 6. Right to Constitutional Remedies — Article 32. Fundamental Rights are enforceable through courts and protect freedom, equality and dignity.

B4. Fundamental Rights versus DPSP

Fundamental Rights: • Located in Part III. • Protect individual freedom, equality and dignity. • Enforceable through courts. • Operate as legal guarantees. DPSP: • Located in Part IV. • Guide the State in social and economic policy. • Not directly enforceable through courts. • Operate as principles for governance and legislation. Relationship: Rights protect liberty; DPSP guides welfare-oriented governance. Constitutional balance requires attention to both.

B5. Union Parliament and State Legislature

Union Parliament: 1. Lok Sabha — House of the People; directly represents citizens. 2. Rajya Sabha — Council of States; represents States. 3. Main functions — law-making, financial control, discussion of public policy and accountability of the Executive. 4. Speaker — presides over Lok Sabha, regulates proceedings and maintains order. State Legislature: 1. Legislative Assembly is the principal elected House. 2. Legislative Council exists where constitutionally provided. 3. Functions include State law-making, financial approval and oversight of the State Executive. Conclusion: The Legislature represents the people and States and converts public policy into law.

B6. Union and State Executive

Union Executive: • President — constitutional head of the Union. • Vice-President — constitutional office with the Rajya Sabha role. • Prime Minister and Council of Ministers — real executive leadership, policy direction and administration. State Executive: • Governor — constitutional head of the State. • Chief Minister and Council of Ministers — real executive leadership and State administration. In the parliamentary system, the Council of Ministers exercises executive leadership while remaining responsible through the constitutional framework.

B7. Constitutional and statutory bodies

A constitutional body is created directly by the Constitution. A statutory body is created by legislation. Election Commission: • Constitutional body. • Administers and safeguards the electoral process. National Human Rights Commission: • Statutory body. • Promotes and protects human-rights concerns within its legal mandate. Lok Ayukta: • Statutory institution under applicable law. • Examines complaints relating to public administration and corruption. These bodies influence citizens through elections, rights protection and accountability mechanisms.

Part C full answers

C1. Three organs of government

Introduction: Constitutional government distributes major functions among Legislature, Executive and Judiciary. A. Legislature • Makes laws and represents citizens and States. • Union: Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha and the parliamentary structure. • State: Legislative Assembly and, where applicable, Legislative Council. • Functions: law-making, financial control, debate and executive accountability. B. Executive • Implements laws and administers public policy. • Union: President, Vice-President, Prime Minister and Council of Ministers. • State: Governor, Chief Minister and Council of Ministers. • The constitutional heads function within the parliamentary system; political executive leadership is exercised by the respective Councils of Ministers. C. Judiciary • Interprets law and resolves disputes. • Supreme Court is the highest court; High Courts operate at State level. • Judicial Review enables courts to test laws and executive action against the Constitution. • Independence of Judiciary protects impartiality, rights and rule of law. Conclusion: Functional separation with constitutional checks prevents concentration of power and supports accountable governance.

C2. Rights, DPSP and Duties

Introduction: Responsible citizenship combines protected rights, welfare-oriented State policy and citizen duties. Fundamental Rights: • Part III, Articles 12–35. • Six classes: Equality, Freedom, against Exploitation, Freedom of Religion, Cultural and Educational Rights, Constitutional Remedies. • Enforceable through courts. Directive Principles of State Policy: • Part IV. • Guide the State toward social and economic justice and good governance. • Not directly enforceable by courts. Fundamental Duties: • Part IVA, Article 51A. • Require respect for the Constitution and national symbols, unity, harmony, environment, scientific temper, public property, excellence and educational responsibility. Comparison and relationship: • Rights protect the citizen. • DPSP guides the State. • Duties guide citizen conduct. Conclusion: Together they form a balanced constitutional framework of liberty, welfare and responsibility.

C3. Local self-government, RTI and RTE

Local self-government brings democracy and administration closer to the people. 73rd Amendment: • Relates to Panchayat Raj institutions in rural areas. • Supports local participation and rural administration. 74th Amendment: • Relates to Nagarapalika / municipal institutions in urban areas. • Supports urban local administration and participation. RTI Act 2005: • Enables citizens to seek information from public authorities according to law. • Promotes transparency and accountability. RTE Act 2009: • Gives a legal framework for children’s right to elementary education as prescribed by law. Citizen impact: • Local bodies deliver and coordinate local public services. • RTI strengthens informed participation. • RTE advances access to elementary education. Conclusion: The amendments and Acts deepen democracy by combining local participation, transparent administration and educational rights.
