Module 1 • 10 Hours

Fundamental Concepts of Chemical Engineering

1.1 History and Professional Roles

Chemical Engineering emerged as a distinct discipline in the late 19th century, evolving from Industrial Chemistry. It focuses on the large-scale production of chemicals, materials, and energy.

Chemist vs. Chemical Engineer
Aspect Chemist Chemical Engineer
Scale Laboratory scale (Grams/Milliliters) Industrial scale (Tons/Kiloliters)
Focus Discovery of new reactions and molecules. Designing processes, equipment, and economics.
Environment Lab bench, fume hoods. Process plants, control rooms, design offices.

Module 2 • 14 Hours

Gas Laws and Gas Mixtures

2.1 Basic Gas Laws

  • Boyle's Law: At constant temperature, $P \propto 1/V$ ($P_1V_1 = P_2V_2$).
  • Charles' Law: At constant pressure, $V \propto T$ ($V_1/T_1 = V_2/T_2$).
  • Ideal Gas Equation: $PV = nRT$.
Problem: Calculate the volume of 2 moles of an ideal gas at 2 atm and 300 K. (R = 0.0821 L.atm/mol.K)

Given: n = 2, P = 2 atm, T = 300 K, R = 0.0821
Calculation: $V = nRT / P = (2 \times 0.0821 \times 300) / 2$
Answer: 24.63 Liters.

2.2 Gas Mixtures

Dalton's Law of Partial Pressures: The total pressure of a mixture of non-reacting gases is equal to the sum of the partial pressures of the individual gases.

P_total = p_A + p_B + p_C + ...
p_A = y_A \times P_total
Malayalam Support: ഡാൾട്ടന്റെ നിയമം അനുസരിച്ച്, ഒരു ഗ്യാസ് മിശ്രിതത്തിലെ ആകെ മർദ്ദം (Total Pressure) അതിലെ ഓരോ ഗ്യാസിന്റെയും ഭാഗിക മർദ്ദങ്ങളുടെ (Partial Pressure) തുകയ്ക്ക് തുല്യമായിരിക്കും. ഒരു ഗ്യാസിന്റെ Partial Pressure എന്നത് അതിന്റെ Mole Fraction-നെ ആകെ മർദ്ദം കൊണ്ട് ഗുണിക്കുന്നതിന് തുല്യമാണ്.

2.3 Average Molecular Weight ($M_{avg}$)

For a mixture of gases:

M_{avg} = \sum (y_i \times M_i) = y_1M_1 + y_2M_2 + ...

Where $y_i$ is the mole fraction and $M_i$ is the molecular weight of component $i$.

Module 3 • 12 Hours

Material Balance without Chemical Reaction

3.1 Law of Conservation of Mass

Mass can neither be created nor destroyed. In a steady-state process without reaction:

Input = Output

3.2 Procedure for Solving MB Problems

  1. Draw a clear Flowchart of the process.
  2. Label all streams (Input, Output) and their compositions.
  3. Select a suitable Basis (e.g., 100 kg of feed).
  4. Write the Overall Material Balance (OMB).
  5. Write Component Material Balances (CMB).
  6. Solve the equations.

3.3 Unit Operations Overview

Module 4 • 9 Hours

Material Balance with Chemical Reaction

4.1 Stoichiometry Basics

  • Stoichiometric Equation: A balanced chemical equation showing the mole ratios.
  • Limiting Reactant: The reactant that is completely consumed and limits the amount of product formed.
  • Excess Reactant: The reactant present in an amount greater than the stoichiometric requirement.
\% Excess = \frac{Actual - Stoichiometric}{Stoichiometric} \times 100

4.2 Conversion, Yield, and Selectivity

  • Conversion: Fraction of feed reactant converted to products.
  • Yield: Moles of desired product formed relative to moles of reactant fed.
  • Selectivity: Ratio of desired product formed to undesired product formed.

4.3 Recycle, Bypass, and Purge

Operation Purpose
Recycle To return unreacted reactants to the reactor to increase overall conversion.
Bypass To divert a portion of the feed around a unit to control product properties.
Purge To remove inert materials that would otherwise accumulate in a recycle loop.

Module-wise Practice Questions

Module 1: Define Molarity and Normality. (3 Marks)
Molarity: The number of moles of solute dissolved in one liter of solution. Unit: mol/L.
Normality: The number of gram equivalents of solute dissolved in one liter of solution. Unit: eq/L.
Module 2: State Dalton's Law of partial pressures. (2 Marks)
Dalton's Law states that the total pressure exerted by a mixture of non-reacting gases is equal to the sum of the partial pressures of individual component gases.
Module 3: Distinguish between Unit Operation and Unit Process. (5 Marks)
Unit Operation: Involves only physical changes like size reduction, separation, or phase change. Examples: Distillation, Filtration, Evaporation.
Unit Process: Involves chemical reactions where new products are formed. Examples: Oxidation, Nitration, Polymerization.
Module 4: Explain Limiting Reactant with an example. (5 Marks)
The limiting reactant is the substance that is totally consumed when the chemical reaction is complete. The amount of product formed is limited by this reactant.
Example: If 1 mole of $H_2$ reacts with 1 mole of $O_2$ to form water ($2H_2 + O_2 \to 2H_2O$), the stoichiometric requirement is 2 moles of $H_2$ for 1 mole of $O_2$. Here, $H_2$ is the limiting reactant because we only have 1 mole.

Quick Revision

  • STP: 0°C (273.15 K) and 1 atm pressure.
  • Molar Volume: 22.414 L at STP for 1 mole of ideal gas.
  • OMB: Total Mass In = Total Mass Out.
  • Recycle: Used to utilize unreacted feed.
  • Purge: Used to prevent accumulation of inerts.

Glossary

Stoichiometry
The study of quantitative relationships between reactants and products in chemical reactions.
Mole Fraction
Ratio of moles of one component to the total moles in a mixture.
Steady State
A condition where process variables do not change with time.
