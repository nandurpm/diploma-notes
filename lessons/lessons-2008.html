Module 1: Pronunciation and Accuracy (CO1)

Focus: I am all ears & Let me try

M1.01: Phonetics, Stress, and Accent

Correct pronunciation is the foundation of effective communication. In this module, students focus on the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA), word stress, and rhythm.

Key Concept: Word Stress
In English, one syllable in a word is pronounced more forcefully than others.
  • Nouns (2 syllables): Stress usually on 1st syllable (e.g., OBject, PREsent).
  • Verbs (2 syllables): Stress usually on 2nd syllable (e.g., obJECT, preSENT).

M1.02: Real-life Usage

Applying vocabulary and phrases in daily contexts. This includes greetings, introductions, and polite expressions.

  • Greeting: "Good morning, how can I help you today?"
  • Apologizing: "I sincerely apologize for the delay in the report."
  • Requesting: "Could you please clarify the technical specifications?"

Module 2: Listening Comprehension (CO2)

Focus: Listening to Narratives and Descriptions

M2.01: Active Listening

Listening is not just hearing; it is understanding the speaker's intent. Students practice listening to:

  • Technical descriptions of machinery.
  • Narratives of industrial accidents or success stories.
  • Casual conversations between colleagues.

M2.02: Gist and Discourse Conversion

After listening, the student must be able to extract the "Gist" (the main point) and convert the information into other forms like a written summary or a flow chart.

Listening Tip: Look for "Signpost Words" like Firstly, However, In conclusion, Therefore. These words tell you where the speaker is going.

Module 3: Language Functions & Role Play (CO3)

Focus: Workplace Situations and Functional English

M3.01: Workplace Role-play

Students simulate real-world engineering scenarios. Examples include:

  • Explaining a circuit diagram to a supervisor.
  • Reporting a fault in the production line.
  • Handling a customer complaint about a faulty device.

Module 4: Professional Speaking (CO4)

Focus: Debates, Discussions, and Interviews

M4.01: Narrating Experiences

Sharing personal or professional experiences using correct tense and grammar. Example: "During my internship, I encountered a major software bug..."

M4.02: Debates and Interviews

This is the peak of the course, focusing on confidence and clarity.

  • Group Discussion (GD): Practicing turn-taking, agreeing/disagreeing politely, and summarizing.
  • Interviews: Preparing for technical and HR interviews using the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result).

Patterns and Structures Bank

The STAR Method (For Interviews)

S: Situation (Context)

T: Task (What needed to be done)

A: Action (What you specifically did)

R: Result (The outcome)

Giving Instructions (Imperative)

Pattern: [Verb] + [Object] + [Adverbial]

Example: "Insert the disc carefully into the slot."

Module-wise Practice Questions

CO1: Pronounce the following words with correct stress: 'Conduct' (Noun) vs 'Conduct' (Verb).
Noun: CON-duct (Stress on first syllable).
Verb: con-DUCT (Stress on second syllable).
CO2: While listening to a technical lecture, what are three things you should note down?
1. The main topic or definition.
2. Key technical specifications or numerical data.
3. Steps in a process or sequence of operation.
CO3: Role Play - You are an engineer reporting a machine failure to your manager. What language functions will you use?
Reporting: "I am reporting that Lathe Machine #4 stopped working at 9 AM."
Stating Cause: "The cause appears to be a motor burnout."
Suggesting: "I suggest we call the maintenance team immediately."
CO4: What is the purpose of a Group Discussion in a professional setting?
The purpose is to exchange ideas, solve a problem, or reach a consensus while demonstrating teamwork, leadership, and communication skills.

Quick Revision

  • IPA: International Phonetic Alphabet helps in standardizing pronunciation.
  • Intonation: The rise and fall of voice in speaking (Falling for statements, Rising for Yes/No questions).
  • Active Listening: Involves paying attention, showing that you are listening, and providing feedback.
  • Soft Skills: Eye contact, body language, and tone are as important as words.
