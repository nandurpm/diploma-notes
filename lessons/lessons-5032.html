What this subject teaches

This course explains how electrical energy is generated in hydroelectric, thermal, gas, diesel and nuclear plants; how plant load, cost and power factor affect operation; how energy is transmitted at high voltage; and how distribution networks, insulators, underground cables and substations deliver power safely.

Module 1 · CO1 · 14 Hours

Electric Power Generation Systems

Generating stations convert energy available in nature or fuel into electrical energy. This module covers energy sources, hydroelectric and thermal stations, site selection, hydel energy calculation, grid connected systems, distributed generation and microgrids.

1.1 Classification of Energy Sources

Energy sources used for electric generation may be classified as conventional and non-conventional, renewable and non-renewable, commercial and non-commercial.

ClassExamplesUse in generation
ConventionalCoal, diesel, gas, nuclear fuel, large hydroLarge scale base-load and peak-load generation.
Non-conventional / renewableSolar, wind, biomass, small hydroDistributed and grid-connected generation with low fuel cost.
CommercialCoal, petroleum, gas, electricity purchased from gridEnergy bought and sold commercially.
Non-commercialFirewood, agricultural waste, animal wasteSmall local energy use; limited direct grid generation.

Malayalam Explanation: Energy source എന്ന് പറഞ്ഞാൽ വൈദ്യുതി ഉണ്ടാക്കാൻ ഉപയോഗിക്കുന്ന ഉറവിടമാണ്. Coal, diesel, gas പോലുള്ളവ fuel ഉപയോഗിക്കുന്നു. Solar, wind, hydro പോലുള്ളവ renewable ആണ്. Renewable source ഉപയോഗിച്ചാൽ fuel cost കുറയും, പക്ഷേ output weather/season അനുസരിച്ച് മാറാം.

1.2 Hydroelectric Power Plant

A hydroelectric station converts potential energy of stored water into mechanical energy in a turbine and then into electrical energy through an alternator.

Water head + discharge + turbine efficiency decide the generated power.
Hydel power equation: Power in kW = 9.81 × Q × H × η, where Q is discharge in m³/s, H is head in m, and η is overall efficiency.

Malayalam Explanation: ഉയരത്തിൽ സൂക്ഷിച്ചിരിക്കുന്ന വെള്ളത്തിന് potential energy ഉണ്ടാകും. വെള്ളം penstock വഴി turbine-ലേക്ക് വരുമ്പോൾ turbine തിരിയും. Turbine alternator-നെ തിരിക്കുന്നതിലൂടെ electricity ഉണ്ടാകും.

1.3 Thermal Generating Stations

Thermal stations use heat energy from fuel to produce mechanical rotation and then electrical output. Coal, gas, diesel and nuclear plants differ mainly in the heat source and prime mover.

PlantBasic workingAdvantagesLimitations
Coal thermalCoal burns in boiler, steam drives turbine.Suitable for base load, mature technology.Pollution, ash handling, slow starting.
Gas turbineCompressed air and gas combustion drive turbine.Quick start, compact.Fuel cost and efficiency sensitivity.
DieselDiesel engine drives alternator.Good standby and small capacity.High running cost, noise, maintenance.
NuclearNuclear fission heat produces steam.High energy density, low fuel transport.High capital cost, safety and waste issues.

1.4 Site Selection Criteria

Selection of a generating station site affects cost, reliability and environmental impact. Consider availability of fuel or water, land, cooling water, transportation, grid connection, load center distance, safety, waste disposal and future expansion.

Malayalam Explanation: Power plant site തിരഞ്ഞെടുക്കുമ്പോൾ fuel/water ലഭ്യത, load center-നോട് ഉള്ള ദൂരം, land cost, cooling water, transport, pollution control, grid connection എന്നിവ നോക്കണം. തെറ്റായ site തിരഞ്ഞെടുപ്പ് long-term cost കൂട്ടും.

1.5 Grid, Distributed Generation and Microgrid

An interconnected grid links many generating stations and load areas. This improves reliability, allows power exchange, reduces reserve requirement and supports economic operation. Distributed generation places small sources near load points. A microgrid is a local electrical network that can operate connected to the main grid or islanded during outages.

Industrial Note: Solar rooftop systems, diesel generators, battery storage and critical loads in a campus can form a microgrid-like arrangement.
Module 2 · CO2 · 15 Hours

Load Curves, Generation Cost, Power Factor and Tariffs

This module links engineering operation with economics. Students learn load curve, demand terms, generation cost, power factor correction and tariff schemes.

2.1 Variable Load and Load Curve

Electrical load varies with time. A load curve shows load demand on the vertical axis and time on the horizontal axis. It helps decide plant capacity, reserve capacity, base load, peak load and operating schedule.

Load curve area gives energy generated/consumed during the period.

Malayalam Explanation: ഒരു ദിവസം മുഴുവൻ load ഒരുപോലെ ആയിരിക്കില്ല. Morning, evening സമയത്ത് demand കൂടും. Load curve നോക്കി ഏത് സമയത്ത് എത്ര generation വേണം എന്ന് തീരുമാനിക്കുന്നു.

2.2 Important Demand Terms

Load factor = Average demand / Maximum demand

Higher load factor means better utilization of plant capacity.

Diversity factor = Sum of individual max demands / Maximum demand of system

Usually greater than one because all loads do not peak together.

Annual units = Average demand × 8760

Used for yearly energy calculation in kWh.

Demand factor = Maximum demand / Connected load

Shows how much connected load is actually demanded.

2.3 Cost of Power Generation

Generation cost has fixed, semi-fixed and running components. Fixed cost exists even if no energy is generated. Running cost changes with units generated.

CostMeaningExamples
Fixed costIndependent of energy generated.Interest, depreciation, salaries, land and building charges.
Semi-fixed costDepends partly on maximum demand/capacity.Maintenance, supervision, reserve equipment.
Running costDepends on actual units generated.Fuel, water treatment, lubricants, operating consumables.

2.4 Power Factor and Improvement

Power factor is the cosine of the phase angle between voltage and current. Low power factor causes higher current, higher losses, poor voltage regulation, larger conductor size and penalties. Improvement methods include static capacitors, synchronous condensers and phase advancers.

Capacitors reduce lagging reactive power drawn from supply.
Capacitor kVAr: Qc = P(tan φ1 − tan φ2), where P is load kW, φ1 is old angle and φ2 is improved angle.

Malayalam Explanation: Low power factor ആണെങ്കിൽ അതേ real power നൽകാൻ കൂടുതൽ current വേണം. അതുകൊണ്ട് cable loss, voltage drop, equipment rating എന്നിവ കൂടും. Capacitor connect ചെയ്താൽ lagging reactive power കുറച്ച് power factor മെച്ചപ്പെടുത്താം.

2.5 Tariffs

Tariff is the rate structure used for charging electrical energy. It should recover cost, encourage efficient use, be simple, fair and suitable for different consumers.

TariffIdeaTypical use
Simple tariffFixed rate per unit.Small consumers.
Flat rate tariffDifferent rate for different consumer categories.Domestic, commercial, industrial separation.
Block rate tariffRate changes with consumption blocks.Energy saving or bulk use encouragement.
Two-part tariffFixed charge + energy charge.Industrial consumers.
Maximum demand tariffDemand charge based on maximum demand plus energy charge.Large consumers.
Power factor tariffPenalty/incentive based on PF.Industries with inductive loads.
TOD / ABTTime-based or availability-based pricing.Grid operation and demand management.
Module 3 · CO3 · 15 Hours

Transmission Systems, Overhead Lines and Sag

This module covers primary and secondary transmission, AC/DC transmission, overhead line components, conductor sag, line phenomena and long-distance transmission systems.

3.1 Transmission System Types

Transmission carries bulk power from generating stations to receiving stations. Primary transmission uses high voltage from generating stations to main substations. Secondary transmission transfers power from main substations to distribution substations.

TypeBasic conceptNotes
AC transmissionAlternating current power transfer using transformers for voltage change.Common for grid networks.
DC transmissionPower transmitted as direct current using converters.Useful for long distance, submarine links and asynchronous interconnection.
Primary transmissionHigh voltage bulk transfer.Reduces current and I²R loss.
Secondary transmissionFrom receiving station to distribution substations.Intermediate voltage level.

3.2 Overhead Line Components

Main components are conductors, line supports, insulators, cross-arms, clamps, earth wire, stay wires, guard wires and lightning arresters. A good line support must be strong, economical, durable and safe.

Important conductor properties are high conductivity, high tensile strength, low specific gravity, corrosion resistance and low cost.

3.3 Sag and Tension

Sag is the vertical distance between the lowest point of a conductor and the straight line joining support points. It is necessary because conductors cannot be pulled perfectly tight; temperature, wind, ice and conductor weight affect sag and tension.

For equal supports and parabolic approximation: sag = wL² / 8T.
Sag formula: y = wL² / 8T for supports at equal level, where w is conductor weight per unit length, L is span and T is horizontal tension.

Malayalam Explanation: Overhead conductor തൂക്കത്തിൽ താഴേക്ക് വളയുന്ന അളവാണ് sag. Sag വളരെ കുറച്ചാൽ tension കൂടും, conductor/support damage ഉണ്ടാകാം. Sag വളരെ കൂടിയാൽ ground clearance കുറയും. അതിനാൽ ശരിയായ sag calculation സുരക്ഷയ്ക്ക് അത്യാവശ്യമാണ്.

3.4 Transmission Line Performance and Phenomena

PhenomenonMeaningEffect
TranspositionChanging conductor positions along the line.Balances inductance and capacitance of phases.
Skin effectAC current concentrates near conductor surface.Increases effective resistance.
Proximity effectCurrent distribution changes due to nearby conductors.Increases loss.
Ferranti effectReceiving end voltage becomes higher than sending end under light load.Important in long lines/cables.
CoronaIonization of air around conductor at high voltage.Power loss, noise, radio interference, ozone.

3.5 Long Distance Transmission: EHV, HVDC and FACTS

Extra High Voltage transmission reduces current and line losses for bulk power transfer. HVDC is useful for very long distances and interconnecting asynchronous grids. FACTS devices use power electronics to improve controllability, stability and power transfer capability.

Module 4 · CO4 · 14 Hours

Electrical Distribution Systems, Insulators, Cables and Substations

This module explains how power reaches consumers through feeders, distributors and service mains. It also covers DC/AC distribution, insulators, underground cables and substations.

4.1 Distribution System Basics

A distribution system receives power from substations and supplies it to consumers. The main parts are feeder, distributor and service mains.

PartFunction
FeederCarries power from substation to distribution area. Current is usually same along its length if no tapping.
DistributorConductor from which consumer tappings are taken. Voltage drop is important.
Service mainsFinal connection from distributor to consumer premises.

Malayalam Explanation: Feeder substation-ൽ നിന്ന് distribution area-ലേക്ക് power കൊണ്ടുപോകുന്നു. Distributor-ൽ നിന്ന് consumers-ലേക്ക് tapping എടുക്കുന്നു. Service mains ആണ് consumer premises-ലേക്ക് പോകുന്ന അവസാന connection.

4.2 Distribution Schemes

SchemeLayoutAdvantagesDisadvantages
RadialSingle path from substation to load.Simple, low cost.Poor reliability; fault interrupts downstream loads.
Ring mainDistributor forms closed ring.Better reliability and voltage regulation.Costlier than radial.
InterconnectedMultiple sources/feeders connected.High reliability, load sharing.Complex protection and operation.

4.3 Insulators and String Efficiency

Line insulators mechanically support conductors and electrically isolate them from poles/towers. Good insulators must have high dielectric strength, high mechanical strength, low porosity, high insulation resistance and resistance to weather.

Types include pin, suspension, strain and shackle insulators. Insulator failure may occur due to cracking, flashover, puncture, pollution, moisture or mechanical damage.

String efficiency: String efficiency = Voltage across whole string / (Number of discs × Voltage across disc nearest conductor) × 100%.

4.4 Underground Cables

Underground cables are used where overhead lines are unsafe, unattractive or impractical. A cable generally contains conductor, insulation, metallic sheath, bedding, armouring and serving.

PartPurpose
ConductorCarries current.
InsulationPrevents leakage current and short circuit.
Metallic sheathProtects insulation from moisture and gases.
BeddingProtects sheath from armouring.
ArmouringMechanical protection.
ServingOuter protection against corrosion and weather.

Methods of laying include direct laying, draw-in system and solid system. Grading reduces dielectric stress concentration in cable insulation.

4.5 Substations

A substation changes voltage level, switches circuits, protects equipment, measures electrical quantities and controls power flow. A typical substation includes transformers, circuit breakers, isolators, busbars, lightning arresters, current transformers, potential transformers, relays and earthing system.

Worked Examples

Step-by-step problems matching the syllabus calculation areas.

Example 1 · Hydel energy

Problem: A hydel plant has Q = 20 m³/s, H = 40 m and η = 0.85. Find power output.

Formula: P = 9.81QHη

Calculation: P = 9.81 × 20 × 40 × 0.85 = 6670.8 kW

Answer: 6.67 MW approximately.

Example 2 · Load factor

Problem: Maximum demand is 500 kW and average demand is 300 kW. Find load factor.

Calculation: Load factor = 300 / 500 = 0.6

Answer: 0.6 or 60%.

Example 3 · Capacitor rating

Problem: A 100 kW load at 0.7 lag is improved to 0.95 lag. Find capacitor kVAr.

φ1 = cos⁻¹0.7, tanφ1 ≈ 1.020. φ2 = cos⁻¹0.95, tanφ2 ≈ 0.329.

Qc = 100(1.020 − 0.329) = 69.1 kVAr

Answer: Use about 69 kVAr capacitor bank.

Example 4 · Sag

Problem: Span L = 200 m, conductor weight w = 0.8 kg/m equivalent load, tension T = 2000 kg. Find sag.

y = wL² / 8T = 0.8 × 200² / (8 × 2000) = 2 m

Answer: Sag = 2 m.
