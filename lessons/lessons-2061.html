This course provides foundational knowledge on natural vegetable, animal, and semi-synthetic fibres. It covers their chemical structure, physical properties, and industrial applications, with a specific focus on cotton, silk, wool, and synthetic polyamides like Nylon 6 and 6,6.

Module 1 | 11 Hours

Classification & Cotton Fibre

1.1 Classification of Textile Fibres

Textile fibres are the fundamental units of textile structures. They are classified based on their Origin and Chemical Nature.

Classification by Origin

  • Natural Fibres:
    • Vegetable (Cellulosic): Cotton, Linen, Jute, Hemp.
    • Animal (Protein): Silk, Wool.
    • Mineral: Asbestos.
  • Man-made Fibres:
    • Regenerated (Semi-synthetic): Viscose Rayon, Acetate Rayon.
    • Synthetic: Nylon, Polyester, Acrylic.
    • Inorganic: Glass, Metallic fibres.

1.2 Cotton: Structure & Properties

Cotton is a natural vegetable fibre obtained from the seed hair of the Gossypium plant.

Structure of Cotton

Fig: Longitudinal view showing natural twists (convolutions) and kidney-shaped cross-section.

Physical Properties

  • Length: 1.25 cm to 6 cm (Staple fibre).
  • Strength: Increases when wet (unique property).
  • Fineness: Varies by variety (Sea Island is finest).
  • Colour: Usually white to creamy.

Chemical Properties

  • Effect of Acid: Disintegrated by hot dilute or cold concentrated mineral acids.
  • Effect of Alkali: Resistant to alkalis; swelling occurs in strong NaOH (Mercerization).
  • Microorganisms: Attacked by mildew and fungi in damp conditions.
Module 2 | 11 Hours

Bast & Animal Fibres

2.1 Bast Fibres: Linen and Jute

Bast fibres are obtained from the stem (phloem) of plants.

Linen (Flax)

Extraction: Processed via Retting (bacterial action in water to loosen fibres from the stem) followed by Scutching and Hackling.

Properties: Stronger than cotton, high luster, good heat conductor (cool to wear).

Jute

Known as the "Golden Fibre". It is cheap, strong, but coarse and harsh. Used mainly for packaging (gunny bags) and carpet backing.

2.2 Animal Fibres: Silk and Wool

Silk (The Queen of Fibres)

Sericulture: The commercial rearing of silkworms (Bombyx mori) for the production of silk.

Lifecycle: Egg → Larva (Caterpillar) → Pupa (in Cocoon) → Moth.

Degumming: Removing the silk gum (Sericin) using soap and water to reveal the lustrous fibroin filaments.

Wool

Scouring: The process of washing raw wool to remove grease (lanolin), dirt, and dried sweat (suint).

Carbonisation: Treatment with acid to remove vegetable matter (burrs, seeds) from wool.

Felting: The interlocking of wool scales under heat, moisture, and pressure.

Module 3 | 10 Hours

Semi-Synthetic Fibres

3.1 Viscose Rayon

Viscose Rayon is regenerated cellulose. It is made from wood pulp or cotton linters.

Manufacturing Flowchart

Wood Pulp → Steeping (NaOH) → Shredding (Alkali Cellulose) → Ageing → Xanthation (CS2) → Dissolving → Ripening → Spinning (Wet Spinning) → Regeneration.

Properties

  • Highly absorbent.
  • Loses 50% strength when wet.
  • Good drape and soft feel.
3.2 Modern Semi-Synthetics
  • Lyocell: Produced using a solvent spinning process (NMMO), more eco-friendly than viscose.
  • Modal: A type of rayon that is highly resistant to shrinkage and fading.
  • Bamboo Fibre: Regenerated cellulose from bamboo pulp, known for antibacterial properties.
Module 4 | 11 Hours

Synthetic Fibres & Polymerization

4.1 Principles of Polymerization

Monomer: The small repeating unit.

Polymer: A long chain molecule made of many monomers.

Types of Polymerization

  1. Addition Polymerization: Monomers join without loss of any small molecules (e.g., Polyethylene).
  2. Condensation Polymerization: Monomers join with the elimination of small molecules like water or ammonia (e.g., Nylon 6,6).
4.2 Polyamides: Nylon 6,6 and Nylon 6

Nylon 6,6

Made from Hexamethylene diamine and Adipic acid. Both monomers have 6 carbon atoms, hence the name "6,6".

Nylon 6

Made from a single monomer: Caprolactam. It involves ring-opening polymerization.

Spinning Methods

Method Mechanism Example
Melt Spinning Polymer is melted and extruded into cold air. Nylon, Polyester
Dry Spinning Polymer dissolved in solvent; solvent evaporated by hot air. Acetate, Acrylic
Wet Spinning Polymer solution extruded into a chemical coagulating bath. Viscose Rayon

Formula Bank & Calculations

Draw Ratio Calculation

Drawing is the process of stretching the extruded filament to align polymer chains and increase strength.

Formula: Draw Ratio = L_final / L_initial

Example: A nylon filament of length 10cm is stretched to 45cm. Calculate the draw ratio.
Given: L_initial = 10cm, L_final = 45cm
Calculation: 45 / 10 = 4.5
Answer: Draw Ratio is 4.5:1.

Module-wise Practice Questions

1. Define Textile Fibre. (2 Marks)
A textile fibre is a unit of matter characterized by flexibility, fineness, and a high ratio of length to thickness, which renders it capable of being spun into yarn.
2. Differentiate between Nylon 6 and Nylon 6,6. (5 Marks)
  • Nylon 6: Produced from Caprolactam; contains 6 carbons in the repeating unit; lower melting point (~215°C).
  • Nylon 6,6: Produced from Hexamethylene diamine and Adipic acid; contains two 6-carbon units; higher melting point (~265°C).
3. Explain the process of 'Retting' in Linen production. (3 Marks)
Retting is the process of using moisture and bacterial action to dissolve the cellular tissues and pectins that bind flax fibres to the stem. Types include Water retting, Dew retting, and Chemical retting.

Glossary

Convolution:
The natural twist found in cotton fibres.
Lumen:
The central hollow canal in a cotton fibre.
Thermoplastic:
A material that softens when heated and hardens when cooled.
Spinneret:
A metal plate with tiny holes through which polymer dope is extruded to form filaments.
