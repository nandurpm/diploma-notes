Module 1: Mechanical Elements

Duration: 9 Hours | CO1

M1.01 & M1.02: Shaft, Axle, Pulley, Keys, and Coupling

1. Shaft vs. Axle

Shaft: A rotating machine element used to transmit power from one place to another. In textile machines, main shafts drive multiple units.

Axle: A stationary or rotating member that supports rotating wheels, pulleys, or gears but does not transmit torque/power.

Malayalam Note: Shaft പവർ ട്രാൻസ്മിറ്റ് ചെയ്യാൻ ഉപയോഗിക്കുന്നു (ഉദാഹരണത്തിന് മോട്ടോറിൽ നിന്ന് മെഷീനിലേക്ക്). എന്നാൽ Axle ഭാരം താങ്ങാൻ മാത്രമാണ് ഉപയോഗിക്കുന്നത്, അത് കറങ്ങണമെന്നില്ല.

2. Pulleys

Pulleys are used to transmit power using belts. Common types in textile mills:

  • Flat Pulley: Used with flat belts for long-distance transmission.
  • V-Pulley: Used for high torque at short distances.
  • Fast and Loose Pulley: Used to start or stop a machine without stopping the main drive shaft.

3. Keys and Couplings

Keys: Temporary fasteners used to connect a shaft to a pulley or gear to prevent relative motion. Types: Sunk key, Woodruff key, Feather key.

Couplings: Used to connect two shafts together at their ends.

  • Rigid Couplings: Used when shafts are perfectly aligned.
  • Flexible Couplings: Used when there is slight misalignment (e.g., Universal coupling).

M2.02 & M2.03: Belt and Chain Drives

Belt Drives

In textile machines like the Carding machine or Spinning frame, belts are vital. Open Belt Drive: Shafts rotate in the same direction. Cross Belt Drive: Shafts rotate in opposite directions.

Roller Chain Drive

Used where a positive drive is needed without slip, but the distance is too large for gears. Commonly seen in the drive to the lattice in blowroom machinery.

Malayalam Note: ബെൽറ്റ് ഡ്രൈവിൽ സ്ലിപ്പ് (Slip) ഉണ്ടാകാൻ സാധ്യതയുണ്ട്. എന്നാൽ ചെയിൻ ഡ്രൈവ് ഒരു 'Positive Drive' ആണ്, അതായത് ഇതിൽ സ്ലിപ്പ് ഉണ്ടാകില്ല.

Module 3: Gears and Calculations

Duration: 13 Hours | CO3

M3.01 & M3.02: Types of Gears
  • Spur Gear: Teeth are parallel to the axis. Used for parallel shafts.
  • Bevel Gear: Used for shafts intersecting at 90 degrees (e.g., Differential motion in Roving frame).
  • Worm and Worm Wheel: Used for large speed reduction in a small space. The worm is like a screw.
  • Pawl and Ratchet: Allows motion in only one direction. Used in take-up motions of looms.
M3.03: Gear Trains and Speed Calculations

Simple Gear Train: Each shaft carries only one gear.

Compound Gear Train: At least one shaft carries two gears. This is used to achieve high velocity ratios in limited space.

Velocity Ratio Formula:

For two gears 1 (Driver) and 2 (Driven):

N₂ / N₁ = T₁ / T₂
Where N = Speed in RPM, T = Number of teeth.

Module 4: Cams, Tappets and Variable Speed

Duration: 8 Hours | CO4

M4.01: Tappets and Cams

Cams and Tappets convert Rotary Motion into Reciprocating (up and down) Motion.

Textile Application: In a weaving loom, Tappet shedding uses cams to lift and lower heald frames to create the shed for the shuttle to pass through.

M4.02: Variable Speed Drives

Textile processes often require speed changes (e.g., as a bobbin builds up in size).

  • Stepless Cone Pulleys: Used in the Roving frame to gradually change the speed of the bobbin as its diameter increases.
  • PIV (Positive Infinitely Variable) Drives: A chain-based drive that allows precise, continuous adjustment of the output speed.

Formula Bank

1. Belt Drive Speed Ratio

N₂ / N₁ = D₁ / D₂

N: RPM, D: Diameter
2. Gear Speed Ratio

N₂ / N₁ = T₁ / T₂

T: Number of Teeth
3. Belt Slip Percentage

% Slip = [(V₁ - V₂) / V₁] × 100

V₁: Surface speed of driver, V₂: Surface speed of driven

Practice Q&A

1. What is the main difference between a Shaft and an Axle?
A shaft is designed to transmit torque and power (it rotates), whereas an axle primarily supports the weight of rotating components and may or may not rotate.
2. Why are V-belts preferred over flat belts for short distances?
V-belts provide better grip due to the wedging action in the pulley groove, allowing for higher torque transmission with less slip at short center distances.
3. What is a 'Positive Drive'?
A drive where there is no possibility of slip between the driver and driven members, ensuring a constant velocity ratio. Examples: Gear drives and Chain drives.
4. Define 'Slip' in belt drives.
Slip is the relative motion between the belt and the pulley surface, occurring when the frictional grip is insufficient. It reduces the velocity ratio.

Quick Revision

  • Mechanical Elements: Shaft (Power), Axle (Support), Key (Fastener), Coupling (Connection).
  • Transmission: Belts (Flexible/Slip), Gears (Rigid/Positive), Chains (Flexible/Positive).
  • Gears: Spur (Parallel), Bevel (Intersecting), Worm (High Reduction).
  • Cams: Convert Rotary to Reciprocating motion.
  • Variable Speed: Cone pulleys and PIV drives are essential for winding processes.

Glossary

Torque
The twisting force that tends to cause rotation.
Velocity Ratio
The ratio of the speed of the driver to the speed of the driven member.
Shedding
The process of separating warp threads into two layers to form a tunnel (shed).
Heald Frame
A component in a loom that holds the heald wires through which warp ends pass.
