## 2026-08-21 - Standardizing Engineering Unit Exponents

**Finding:** Unit exponents in fluid mechanics and physics lesson pages often alternate between plain numbers (`m3`, `kg/m3`, `N/m3`, `m3/s`) and Unicode superscripts (`m³`, `kg/m³`, `N/m³`, `m³/s`), even within the same file.
**Learning:** Inconsistent unit notation damages student trust and readability on formula/example-dense pages, particularly when adjacent variables already use Unicode superscripts (e.g., `V²`).
**Prevention:** Standardize volume, area, and density unit exponents to Unicode superscripts (`²`, `³`) across lesson pages while preserving exact technical numerical values and formulas.

## 2026-08-28 - Standardizing Temperature Unit Notation

**Finding:** Thermal process thresholds in manufacturing and materials lessons occasionally use ASCII text notation (`deg C`) instead of standard Unicode degree symbols (`°C`).
**Learning:** Inconsistent symbol usage for temperatures (e.g. `450 deg C` vs `450 °C`) detracts from visual professionalism and consistency across technical diploma study materials.
**Prevention:** Standardize temperature unit representations to Unicode degree symbols (`°C`, `°F`) across lesson topics, rule banks, and answer keys without modifying underlying numerical values or taught concepts.
